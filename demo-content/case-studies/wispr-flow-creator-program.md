# Wispr Flow: creator programme

> [!summary] At a glance
> "Wispr Flow's creator programme" is really three programmes run by different people. (1) An **organic UGC army**: about 80 mostly student creators posting daily from new, dedicated TikTok and Instagram accounts. Its builder says it reached 500M+ views in about three months at a $0.74 CPM (*claimed*) [1][2]. (2) A **performance-paid ad creative engine**: about 50 creators paid 7% of the ad spend behind their videos, shipping 500–700 new Meta, TikTok and AppLovin ads a month (*claimed*) [7][8]. (3) **Sponsored creator partnerships** with developer, AI and productivity creators on YouTube, LinkedIn, podcasts and newsletters, grown from about 10 to 200+ creators (*claimed*) [11]. An affiliate programme on Dub sits alongside them [13]. The company's growth is *verified* in outline: a $280M Series B at a $2B valuation, and Menlo's statement that revenue grew 30x+ year on year (as of 2026-08) [17][18]. No source isolates how much of that the creators drove.

## Who built it

- **Tobin Tang** built the organic UGC programme as a social media intern on a University of Waterloo co-op term, Sep–Dec 2025 [1][5][6]. He had been making personal-finance TikToks before Wispr [6]. He left to lead UGC at Polymarket in Jan 2026 [5]. His X bio now lists Waterloo, with Polymarket and Wispr as past roles (as of 2026-10) [21].
- **Hector Serrano Martin** has been at Wispr since Sep 2024 as "marketing hire #2". He built founder storytelling, Meta creative strategy and the paid creator programme [10].
- **Eric Zaworski** runs Meta media buying [9][20].
- **Eduardo Sanchez-Ubanell** has run sponsored creator partnerships since Mar 2026. He previously did creator partnerships at TikTok and YouTube, and was creator-marketing director at Edelman [11]. Follow: LinkedIn.
- **Tanay Kothari** (CEO) commissioned the organic push in July 2025 after watching Cluely's UGC results [1].

## How it worked

**Engine 1: organic UGC (Tang, 2025)**
1. **Dedicated accounts.** Every creator opened a brand-new TikTok and Instagram account for Wispr. Each followed a strict warm-up routine before posting anything about the product, to avoid being throttled [2].
2. **Hire ambassadors, not UGC pros.** Tang preferred people who loved the product, had time, and had *taste*. People new to content often did better because they weren't anchored on view counts. 79 of the 80 creators were undergraduates [2].
3. **Sourcing:** friends, friends of friends, his own creator network, cold email to mid-sized creators and DMs to micro-creators. He avoided agencies and Discord pools [2]. Creators recruited their own peers too: one recruit brought in 16 creators in 8 days [4].
4. **Selection:** about 1,000 applied and roughly 60 were chosen [1]. Tang interviewed 400+ creators and says the first ten seconds of a video tell you most of what you need [2]. Every creator had a 30-day trial [2].
5. **Pay:** a retainer plus a large bonus, for 5–7 videos a week. The recruiting post advertised "up to $5,000 per video" [3]. The full rate card was never published.
6. **Brief:** half of each creator's content was fully free-form [1]. The rest came from a library of specific hooks. One example: dictate a hard name like Tchaikovsky, then react with shock when it's spelled right [1].
7. **Replication:** posts were monitored in real time. Any video passing 1M views on its first day had its script pushed to every creator [1]. Some "timeless scripts" went out to everyone [2].
8. **Tiering:** 10 "strategists" invented and tested new concepts, and 60 "executors" replicated the winners [2].

**Engine 2: performance-paid ad creative (Serrano and Zaworski, 2026)**
1. **Platform:** Trybe. Creators submit videos and Wispr runs them as partnership ads on Meta, AppLovin and TikTok [7][8].
2. **Pay:** 7% of the ad spend behind each creator's videos, paid weekly. Wispr chose spend over sales because subscriptions take time to convert [7][8]. A "smart retainer" sets a floor: hit 25 videos in a month and you're guaranteed $1,000 [7].
3. **Recruiting:** start with 4–5 creators, each filling a gap in the ad library (a format, an audience or a market). Find them through Instagram keyword searches and send 100–200 DMs [7]. About 5–7 of roughly 7,000 Trybe applicants were accepted [7]. Creators who refer someone at their level earn $100 [7].
4. **Scaling:** 5 → 10 → 20 → 40, reaching 50 active creators in four months. Beyond 40–60 creators, add only people who fill a gap [7].
5. **Run them like a sales team:** coaches, a weekly Thursday call with the media buyer, 1:1 sessions, gear giveaways, and a San Francisco retreat for the top six [7][15].
6. **No clones.** Serrano says Meta groups and suppresses creative that is more than roughly 60% similar. Meta publishes no such threshold, so treat the number as a practitioner's rule of thumb [7][9].
7. **Optimise for habit.** The account judges an ad by words dictated after signup, not installs [9].

**Engine 3: sponsored creators (Sanchez-Ubanell, 2026)**
The roster started with a handful of developer and AI creators and widened to hundreds across industries [12]. He sorts creators by expected CPM and keeps per-platform rate cards. He runs a standard brief and reviews every script, and one person manages 100+ concurrent deals [11]. Tracked links, promo codes and warehouse queries tie the content to signups [11]. Briefs require creators to use the product in their own workflow for days before scripting [22].

## Results

| Claim | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 500M+ views, 80 creators, $0.74 CPM in ~3 months (CEO says 60 days) | *claimed* | [1][2] |
| 35 creators and 100M views in under 3 months; a 16-creator squad drove 53M views in 3 weeks | *claimed* (creator) | [4] |
| 500–700 new ads/month from ~50 creators; Meta account grew from ~100 to 3,500+ ads since Jan 2026 | *claimed* | [7][9] |
| Sponsored creators: 49M+ views, 108K clicks, 379 placements in H1 2026; 6,000+ tracked signups; budget from <$50K to $350K+/month; blended CPC down ~50% | *claimed* (LinkedIn profile) | [11] |
| Affiliates: thousands of partners at ~4x average ROAS | *claimed* (vendor case study) | [13] |
| $280M Series B at $2B valuation; 10,000+ enterprises using Flow | *verified* (press) | [17][19] |
| Revenue up 30x+ year on year | *claimed* (lead investor) | [18] |
| Oct 2026 outside tracking: 161 attributed accounts, 5.1M views in 30 days, median 826 views per video, 3.7% with caption disclosure | *reported* | [14] |

**Counter-evidence.** The outside tracking suggests the organic engine is far smaller now than the 500M-view peak. Inference: it shrank after Tang left [14]. The sources disagree on 60, 70 or 80 creators and on 60 days versus three months [1][2]. Views are not revenue, and none of the figures has been audited [9][20]. Critics call the 7% model exploitative, because the $1,000 floor works out to $40 a video [16]. They also note that pay tied to spend rewards spend rather than profit [15]. The brand is legally liable for creators' disclosures, and few captions carry one [14][15].

## Why it worked

- **The product demos itself in seconds.** Seeing a hard name spelled correctly is visual proof that fits any format [1].
- **It was cheap to try**, with a free plan and a $12–15 Pro tier, so a view could turn into a user in the same session [9].
- **The selection bar was high**, and the culture treated creators as a community rather than vendors [1][2][7].
- **Pay was matched to the job:** retainers for organic reach, a share of spend for ad creative, and rate cards for sponsorships.
- **Consumer reach fed enterprise.** Menlo says every organic user is future pipeline for team deals [18].

## When it transfers, and when it doesn't

**Transfers if:** the product's value shows on screen in under 10 seconds; the audience is broad (students, professionals in general); there's a free or low-cost self-serve entry point; you already run paid social with a trustworthy conversion event (for engine 2); and someone in-house has native creator instincts. Tang and Serrano were both creators first [6][10].

**Doesn't transfer if:** you sell high-ACV B2B SaaS with a buying committee and no self-serve entry; the value is invisible on screen (data, integrations, compliance); your ad budget can't fund enough spend per creative to learn from (one practitioner says a read takes about five days); or you work in a regulated category where undisclosed endorsement is a serious risk [9][15].

**For B2B SaaS**, engine 3 is the template: credible niche creators, product use before any script, and tracked signups. Engines 1 and 2 only work where there's a PLG motion with individual users. The enterprise layer then comes from LinkedIn and sales, not TikTok [9][18]. US-venture conditions applied: $361M in funding and a San Francisco talent pool. Flag both before applying this outside the US or to bootstrapped contexts.

## How to run your own

- [ ] Pick the engine: organic reach, ad creative or sponsored credibility. Don't blend their pay models.
- [ ] Write down the one on-screen "proof moment" a creator can show in under 10 seconds.
- [ ] Hire one person with a creator background to own it end to end.
- [ ] Start with 4–5 creators, each filling a named gap. Scale in steps and hold a 30-day trial.
- [ ] Make product use a condition of the brief, before any scripting.
- [ ] Build a hook library with specific actions, not themes. Leave half the content free-form.
- [ ] Monitor daily. Replicate first-day winners in organic. In paid, force new concepts so ads don't cluster.
- [ ] Set pay against your CAC, with a floor tied to delivering a set number of videos.
- [ ] Write in disclosure rules and ad-usage rights (duration and platforms) from the first contract.
- [ ] Measure a habit or activation event plus tracked signups. Never views alone.
- [ ] Run a weekly creator call that shares what the ad account shows.
