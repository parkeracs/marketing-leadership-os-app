# Clay: founder, executive and ecosystem advocacy

> [!summary] At a glance
> Clay's LinkedIn presence runs in three layers. A co-founder posted daily from the start. Agencies and freelancers post because being "the Clay expert" wins them clients. Since 2025 there has also been a managed executive programme that pairs each buyer persona with one Clay leader [1][4][5]. The executive layer is run like a channel: it has an owner, a weekly cadence, a quality bar and an awareness target [1]. Clay says it produced ~6M organic impressions and 80k new followers in one quarter (*claimed*, as of 2026-05) [1]. It also says more than half its pipeline now traces back to marketing, with no cold outbound (*claimed*, as of 2026-07) [2].

## Who built it

- **Varun Anand** (co-founder) found the first users, cold-email agency owners, in WhatsApp and Slack groups. He then started posting daily with early employee **Eric Nowoslawski** and coached customers to post [4][5].
- **Kareem Amin** (CEO) and Anand coined the "GTM engineer" title for Clay's own customer-facing team [13].
- **Bruno Estrella** joined in December 2023 from five years running growth at Webflow, when Clay had about 20 staff [6]. He designed the persona-mapped executive programme and published how it works [1].
- **Sarah Khasrovi** runs the programme day to day [1][16].
- **Davide Grieco** (Head of Growth, previously a Clay customer at Verkada) turns the attention into pipeline [2].
- Follow them on LinkedIn. Estrella and Grieco publish the most about the mechanics [1][7][8].

## How it worked

**1. Seed: piggyback on people who already had a reason to post (2022–23).** Agency owners posted about Clay unprompted, to look expert in a new tool. Nobody was paying them an affiliate fee [4][5]. Clay "pounced": Anand and Nowoslawski posted every day, and Clay briefed customers on new features and helped them make content [4]. The rule was to show people how to do something valuable to them [4]. Estrella calls this unglamorous work: you spot an agency signing up and get on a call to help them create content [6].

**2. Formalise the ecosystem into tiers.** By 2024 a partner counted four tiers [17]:
- Creators: 357 of them, on a 20% revenue share, with ad sponsorship
- Experts: 51 vetted implementers
- Club hosts: 32
- Enterprise partners: 16 agencies above $150k MRR

You had to apply to be a Creator. Applicants needed an existing LinkedIn habit, prior Clay mentions and real usage. Perks included an affiliate link, ready-made content, paid promotion of their posts as thought-leader ads, and early feature access. Clay also built a tool that auto-generates feature videos in the creator's own face and voice [6]. In 2025 the Experts tiers became the Solutions Partner Program, with four levels from Artisan to Elite Studio [14]. Partners get:
- certification and a listing in the partner directory
- a 20% referral revenue share
- grants of $500–15,000 from the Partner Growth Fund [14]

The Creators programme was paused until March 2026, per Clay's FAQ [15]. Estrella says creators mostly wanted to be associated with a credible product, not paid [7]. By mid-2024 the ecosystem already included bootcamps, newsletters and "Claygencies", some with multi-million-dollar run rates (*claimed*) [12].

**3. Name the career, not the product.** "GTM engineer" started as an internal title. Clay staff used it on LinkedIn, agencies adopted it for status, and Clay deliberately stayed out of defining it [13]. Clay Clubs, Clay University cohorts, certifications and the Sculpt conference grow the pool of people whose skills fit Clay [3][16][19]. Clubs feed back into content: event material becomes LinkedIn posts, the posts become blog posts, and the blog posts become guides [4].

**4. Run executive content as a channel (2025–present).** Estrella's process [1]:
- **Start from the buyers, not from who is willing to post.**
  - Users (GTM engineers, ops, growth) get Everett Berry (Head of GTM Engineering) and Grieco.
  - Marketing and revenue leaders get Estrella, Anand and Rob Cook (SDRs), with Todd Busler (enterprise sales) and Becca Lindquist (Head of Sales) added later.
  - Founders get Anand and Amin.
- **Make it a core awareness metric** that marketing is accountable for.
- **Give it a dedicated owner.** Estrella's line: "You need to have someone who's borderline annoying to ensure this moves forward."
- **Add contractor writers** (Alec Paul, Good Content) to scale it.
- **Keep a weekly rhythm:** weekly meetings plus Slack nudges.
- **Quality bar:** before posting, ask whether the reader would actually learn anything. If not, don't post.

One podcast account describes one writer per executive, meeting weekly. The writer captures the thinking and the executive owns the voice [7]. Another says the executives write their own posts, with teammates sharpening hooks [3]. Clay's company-wide AI writing policy says authors must stand behind every sentence they share [24].

**5. Content mix and cadence.** Grieco's published mix (as of 2026-07) [3]:
- ~20% promotional, with a call to action
- 25–40% personal
- ~40% thought leadership
- up to 15% playful

His team avoids posting about features and launches [9]. Third-party counts suggest cadence varies by role (*reported*, as of 2026-06): Amin posts roughly every three weeks and gets the highest average likes, while Anand posts about twice a week and drives the most volume [18]. Voices are deliberately different: contrarian essays, memes, milestones, one-liners, video [19].

**6. Measure it.**
- Clay tracks a "qualified engagements" metric. It logs every post that mentions Clay and checks who engaged against named-account lists [7].
- It also uses self-reported attribution [6].
- Executive posts promote the "How Clay Uses Clay" livestreams, so registrations by poster become a downstream proof point [8].
- The growth team hands sellers a weekly "campaign in a box" and books call blocks after each episode [3].

**Counter-evidence.**
- Of 2,270 posts by the top ten listed Clay Experts, 41% promoted Clay and only 1.6% were critical (*reported*) [21].
- A competitor CEO argued in 2025 that the GTM-engineer "movement" was manufactured. He counted only 79 open US roles and found 82 of 650 GTM engineers worked at Clay (*reported*, biased source) [22]. Later counts put open roles above 3,000 (*reported*, 2026) [23].
- Some practitioners say their feeds are saturated with repetitive Clay posts and filter them out [25].
- The founding timeline differs between sources: six, seven or eight years to $1M ARR [2][7][10].

## Why it worked

- **Incentives matched before any programme existed.** Agencies grew their businesses by looking expert in Clay, so their posts were self-interested and credible [4][6].
- **The product produces things worth showing.** A finished table or workflow is a natural post [6].
- **Every buyer is on one channel.** Estrella notes that all of Clay's personas consume LinkedIn, so mapping voices to personas avoids running separate channels [1].
- **Operator credibility.** Executives post about their own craft, not the product [1][9].
- **Frequency.** Estrella holds that people forget you within about three months if your posting frequency drops [7].
- **Brand and legitimacy spend.** Clay hired a Head of Brand early, as employee #18, and ran billboards to look credible [7][10].

## When it transfers, and when it doesn't

It transfers when:
- The buyer is a practitioner who lives on LinkedIn.
- The product is a building tool with visible output.
- There is a services layer, such as agencies, consultants or freelancers, who profit from being seen as the expert.
- Executives are genuine operators in their buyers' discipline.

It needs:
- **Founders willing to post for years.** Anand posted daily from the start [4].
- **Real headcount.** That means an owner, contractor writers and a growth team to convert the attention [1][2].

It transfers poorly when:
- The buyers are not on LinkedIn, or are regulated or risk-averse.
- The product has no showable output.
- The deal is high-ACV enterprise and buyers distrust vendor content.
- It runs in markets where personal posting is culturally rarer. This is an inference.

The ecosystem layer also carries a cost. When partners are rewarded for posting, the feed gets saturated and less critical [21][25]. Category ownership can also be contested, as a 2026 naming fight over the GTM-engineer title shows [23].

## How to run your own

- [ ] List your two to four core buyer personas. Confirm where each one actually consumes content.
- [ ] Pair each persona with the most credible internal voice. Pick the most credible person, not the most willing one.
- [ ] Make executive and employee reach a named awareness metric, with an owner who is accountable for it.
- [ ] Appoint a programme lead with time and licence to chase people.
- [ ] Hold weekly sessions with each voice. Add writers who capture each voice's ideas without replacing them.
- [ ] Set a quality bar: would the reader learn something? Ban unedited AI copy.
- [ ] Set a content mix per voice (for example promo / personal / thought leadership / playful), and keep feature posts rare.
- [ ] Find outsiders who already post about you, such as agencies, consultants and power users. Help them make content before you formalise anything.
- [ ] Then tier them: application criteria, certification, a directory listing, a referral share, co-marketing funds, early access.
- [ ] Give the community local, user-run events whose material can be recycled into content.
- [ ] Route attention to a conversion asset, such as a use-case webinar, and track registrations by poster.
- [ ] Measure engagement from named accounts plus self-reported attribution, not likes.
