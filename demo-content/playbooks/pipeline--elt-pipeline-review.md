# The ELT pipeline review: a meeting guide

**The job:** get the exec team to fix off-track pipeline every week, with an owner and a date on every off-track item.

**What it is:** a weekly, CEO-chaired meeting on whether pipeline will carry this quarter and the next, and what to do about the parts that won't. It is an alignment meeting that happens to use numbers, not a numbers review ([[The CEO-Driven Pipeline Meeting - Andy MacMillan|Andy MacMillan]]). Status arrives in writing beforehand: on track is a one-line confirmation, and off track gets the detail every week until it's fixed ([[No Surprises - Weekly Async Pipeline Updates and the Marketing Command Center - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]).

| **People** | CEO, CFO, CMO, CRO, VP Sales, RevOps, revenue marketing: Lacy's pipeline council list ([[Revenue Diaries Entry 77 - Own the Number, Pipeline Council and the Revenue Handbook - Kyle Lacy\|Kyle Lacy]]) |

| **Done when** | Every off-track item has a cause, a fix, an owner and a date |

## Before you start

1. **One tracker, one set of definitions.** Every pipeline owner has a row; track creation as a weekly pace against plan, not a quarterly total, because in week 11 you can only fix a rate you've watched since week 2 ([[Pipeline Generation Meeting vs Forecast Call - Pete Furseth|Pete Furseth]]). If numbers are still disputed, fix that first; see *Sizing* below.
2. **The pre-read rule:** every owner posts by the Friday deadline. Updates are required even when the numbers are flat: if you own a row of pipeline, you own the update ([[No Surprises - Weekly Async Pipeline Updates and the Marketing Command Center - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]).

## The prep calendar

*Lacy's weekly rhythm, with the meeting added after the Monday read. The meeting slot and the same-day send are an adaptation of the play.*

| When | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **Friday, end of day** | Each pipeline owner posts the update: on or off track, pacing against plan, and if off, what's broken, what they're doing and what changes in the next 1–2 weeks ([[No Surprises - Weekly Async Pipeline Updates and the Marketing Command Center - Kyle Lacy\|Kyle Lacy]]) | Inbound, outbound, partner owners |
| **Monday** | CMO, CRO and RevOps lead read the updates and log questions, risks and gaps in the same document ([[No Surprises - Weekly Async Pipeline Updates and the Marketing Command Center - Kyle Lacy\|Kyle Lacy]]). Off-track owners prepare a cause and a fix | CMO, CRO, RevOps |
| **After the Monday read** | The meeting. It works the off-track rows and the risks logged on Monday | All |
| **Meeting day** | Decisions and owners sent | RevOps or chief of staff |
| **Next Friday** | Owners answer the logged questions in their next update ([[No Surprises - Weekly Async Pipeline Updates and the Marketing Command Center - Kyle Lacy\|Kyle Lacy]]) | Pipeline owners |

**Status.** Each owner marks their row on track or off track, with pacing against plan ([[No Surprises - Weekly Async Pipeline Updates and the Marketing Command Center - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]). No source sets a threshold for off track. Agree any thresholds in the definitions doc, not in the room.

| Person | Owns in the meeting |
|---|---|

## The off-track script

Use one line per off-track item, punchline first, with the ask and the date explicit ([[15 principles for managing up|Wes Kao]]):

> *"[Metric] is off track, [actual] against [plan], because [cause]. We're doing [fix]. I need [ask] from [person] by [date]."*

Rules for the room:
1. **Pre-wire the CRO** on any off-track item that touches sales. Agree the numbers beforehand; no pipeline discrepancies, no finger-pointing ([[CMO Board Meeting Success|Carilu Dietrich]]).
2. **Flag risk at the first itch** in the Monday log, so the CEO never hears it first in the meeting ([[15 principles for managing up|Wes Kao]]; [[Revenue Diaries Entry 77 - Own the Number, Pipeline Council and the Revenue Handbook - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]).
3. **Restate agreed trade-offs** every week, such as brand spend delaying pipeline, so a planned dip isn't read as a miss ([[Why you should get buy-in throughout a project|Wes Kao]]).
4. **Bring a recommendation, not a question:** the fix, its rationale, the risk and its mitigation, and the first step if approved ([[15 principles for managing up|Wes Kao]]).
5. **No attribution debates.** Attribution is directional insight, not a scoreboard; the room works one shared pipeline goal ([[Dear Marketers, Is sales & marketing alignment harder than ever|MKT1]]).
6. **Lead block 4 with starting coverage, then pipegen by source.** Pipegen on plan while coverage falls means the model is wrong ([[Why Great Marketers Look at Pipeline Coverage Not Just Pipeline Generation - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).

## Sizing it to your situation

| If… | Then |
|---|---|
| **Sales and marketing don't share a meaning of "pipeline"** | Widen it to 10–12 people from both teams, CEO in the chair. Budget weeks to agree definitions and fix reporting first ([[The CEO-Driven Pipeline Meeting - Andy MacMillan\|Andy MacMillan]]) |
| **The forecast eats the hour** | Split it out. The forecast call works this quarter's deals; a generation meeting works sources for the next one to two quarters. Run the forecast first and hand its gap to generation ([[Pipeline Generation Meeting vs Forecast Call - Pete Furseth\|Pete Furseth]]) |
| **Past roughly $100M revenue** | Split into regional sessions ([[The CEO-Driven Pipeline Meeting - Andy MacMillan\|Andy MacMillan]]) |
| **The CEO doesn't attend** | CRO and CMO send the CEO the reds and fixes in one note afterwards. *Adaptation of the play* |

## Worked example: the tracker

*Illustrative. Halden is the fictional company from [[pipeline-and-lead-management]]: week 6 of 13. Plan to date is 6/13 of the quarter; status is each owner's call.*

| Row | Owner | Plan to date | Actual | Status | If off: the fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hand-raisers (demo requests) | CMO | 240 | 228 | On track | |
| Hand-raisers called within 1 hour | SDR lead | 90% | 71% | Off track | SDRs back on inbound Monday |
| Marketing stage-2 opportunities | CMO | 56 | 41 | Off track | Scheduler on the demo form |
| Partner stage-2 opportunities | Partnerships | 20 | 19 | On track | |
| SDR outbound stage-2 opportunities | CRO | 15 | 8 | Off track | See below |
| AE outbound stage-2 opportunities | CRO | 10 | 9 | On track | |
| New ARR closed | CRO | $1.25M | $1.05M | On track | |
| Next-quarter coverage (forecast) | CMO | 2.8x | 2.1x | Off track | Pull partner events forward |

**The SDR outbound item, in one line:** *"SDR outbound opportunities are off track, 8 against 15, because two of five SDRs moved to inbound cover when the SLA slipped. We're moving them back on Monday and adding a scheduler to the demo form. I need the CRO to approve the scheduler licence by Friday."*

**What the CEO took away:** the SDR outbound and SLA rows share a cause, so two fixes come from a single decision.

## Signs it's working, signs it's failing

- **Working within a quarter:** off-track items are back on track within two weeks of an owned fix; the CEO asks the CMO about next quarter, not just this one; the CRO defends marketing's numbers when the CMO isn't in the room.
- **Failing:** sections are read-outs; numbers get disputed in the room; the CFO asks "how is this calculated?"; the same off-track item appears three weeks running with no new fix.

*No source sets these thresholds; they are an adaptation of the play.*

## Why this format: the evidence

**Where the sources agree:** status goes in writing before the meeting, and the live time goes to off-track items and decisions ([[No Surprises - Weekly Async Pipeline Updates and the Marketing Command Center - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]; [[Revenue Diaries Entry 77 - Own the Number, Pipeline Council and the Revenue Handbook - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]; [[Pipeline Generation Meeting vs Forecast Call - Pete Furseth|Pete Furseth]]).

**Where they split:** who chairs, and how big. MacMillan has the CEO chair 10–12 people from both teams every week; Lacy has the CMO, CRO and RevOps review weekly and RevOps run the monthly council as neutral ground. *Default:* CEO in the chair with Lacy's council-sized exec list. *Switch to MacMillan's* wider room while the teams don't trust each other's numbers.
