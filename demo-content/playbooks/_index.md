# Playbooks

One playbook per recurring marketing-leadership job. **A playbook is something you can run, not something you read.** Someone new to the job can start on Monday and know what to produce, who is involved and by when, what to do when the situation forks, and how to tell whether it's working.

**Each playbook recommends a default route,** built from where the trusted sources converge (3+ independent sources, none contradicting). Where sources split, it still picks a default and says when to switch. The owner's personal views live in `principles/`, never here.

Every hub follows the same skeleton, so they scan:

1. **Header block:** the job, when to pull the play, time, owner and people, what you walk out with, and when it's done.
2. **Before you start:** the decisions to settle first.
3. **The play:** phases with a time box. Each phase has instructions, the exact wording where words matter, if/then rules with a default, the output, the trap at that step, and a pause-point checklist.
4. **Situations that change the play,** including when not to run it.
5. **A worked example** for a fictional company, labelled as illustrative.
6. **Signs it's working, signs it's failing.**
7. **Templates** you can copy.
8. **Why this play: the evidence.** Where the sources agree, where they split and which default the play takes, and further reading.
9. **Next plays.**

A **meeting guide** (`type: meeting-guide`) is a child for one recurring meeting: prep calendar, agenda with timings, who presents what, and a filled-in example.
