# Pipeline and lead management

**The job:** set up, or repair, the interface between marketing, sales and RevOps: one signed set of definitions, fast routing, a coverage model by source, and a weekly rhythm that fixes problems instead of assigning blame.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pull this play when** | You're new in a marketing leadership role, or "the leads are bad" has become a recurring argument |
| **Time** | About one quarter to set up, then a permanent weekly rhythm. *No source gives a set-up timeline; adaptation of the play* |
| **Owner** | You, jointly with the CRO. **Key people:** RevOps (builds and arbitrates), SDR lead, demand gen lead, CFO (signs off the coverage model) |
| **You walk out with** | 1) A signed definitions doc: stages, exit criteria, owners. 2) A written SLA with auto-reassignment. 3) Routing rules live in the CRM. 4) A coverage model with targets by source. 5) A weekly rhythm with a fixed scrub schedule |
| **Done when** | "The leads are bad" gets answered with data in a week, and the CRO and you present one pipeline number |

## Before you start

1. **Agree who arbitrates.** RevOps decides routing and ownership disputes, not the sales manager ([[Dear Marketers, Is sales & marketing alignment harder than ever|MKT1]]; [[Revenue Diaries Entry 77 - Own the Number, Pipeline Council and the Revenue Handbook - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]).
2. **Get CRM history you can trust:** source as a required picklist ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]) and week-3 pipeline snapshots, because the coverage method needs about nine quarters of them ([[Target Pipeline Coverage is Not the Inverse of Win Rate|Dave Kellogg]]).

## The play

### Phase 1 · Weeks 1–2: diagnose

1. **Measure follow-up first:** the share of inbound that got any human response, and time to first touch. SaaStr's human team answered fewer than 40% of inbound, as of 2026-04 ([[Our 1.25 Humans + 20 AI Agents Closed 140 Percent of What Our All-Human Sales Team Did|Jason Lemkin]]).
2. **Submit your own demo form** and time the reply ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]).
3. **Pull pipeline, win rate and cycle length from the CRM, by source** ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]), with each source's conversion to close beside its volume ([[Pipeline Generation Meeting vs Forecast Call - Pete Furseth|Pete Furseth]]).
4. **Name the constraint in one sentence, with one focus metric.** Demand that exists but stalls at the handoff is misalignment; leads that qualify but won't close are a product/market fit problem ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]). Read the levers in this order: demo-to-SQO rate, demo quality and volume, win rate, ACV, paid spend, CAC payback ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]).

**Output:** a one-page funnel diagnosis, SLA compliance on top.
**Trap:** opening with a definitions fight. Follow-up matters more than stage taxonomy, possibly up to about $10M ARR ([[The 1 Most Important Thing You Can Do With Your Leads - Implement an SLA|Jason Lemkin]]).

**Pause point, week 2:**
- [ ] Response share and time-to-first-touch measured
- [ ] Own form tested end to end
- [ ] Constraint named in one sentence, with one focus metric

### Phase 2 · Weeks 3–5: define and sign

1. **Write the stage list** with sales and RevOps, and define "pipeline" in it: with no adjective, every opportunity with a close date in the period ([[Why You Should Always Create Sales Opportunities at Zero Dollar Value - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]). Name stages for what they are (inbound, demo request), not for the team that owns them ([[Dear Marketers, Is sales & marketing alignment harder than ever|MKT1]]). Give every stage written criteria for moving on; stage weights only mean something on consistent definitions ([[Why You Should Always Create Sales Opportunities at Zero Dollar Value - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).
2. **Require two keys at pipeline entry:** the SDR and the AE both sign it off against written criteria. Create every opportunity at $0 until value is discussed with the customer ([[Why You Should Always Create Sales Opportunities at Zero Dollar Value - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).
3. **Write the Pipeline Management Rules:** one rule each for close date, stage, forecast category and value. Scrubs check against them ([[Always Scrubbing the Pipeline Means Never Scrubbing the Pipeline|Dave Kellogg]]).
4. **Pick one shared metric before closed-won** ([[Your VP Sales and VP Marketing Should Be Your Mom and Dad of Revenue|Jason Lemkin]]).
   - *Default:* count of sales-accepted (stage 2) opportunities, set as counts, not dollars. Measure close rate from stage 2, never from "demo" ([[The Top Two High-Level Questions About Sales - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).
   - *If MQL volume is the headline,* demote it to a leading indicator and measure marketing on pipeline created. Warn the exec team that volume will fall before quality rises ([[A Three-Tier Model for B2B Lead Engagement - Jon Miller|Jon Miller]]).
5. **Get the doc signed** by the heads of sales, marketing and RevOps ([[Revenue Diaries Entry 77 - Own the Number, Pipeline Council and the Revenue Handbook - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]).

**Output:** the signed definitions doc and the Pipeline Management Rules.
**Trap:** measuring conversion only from a late stage. Stages 1–3 become a dumping ground ([[Why You Should Always Create Sales Opportunities at Zero Dollar Value - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).

**Pause point, week 5:**
- [ ] Every stage has written criteria for moving on
- [ ] Two-key entry written down
- [ ] Shared metric agreed with the CRO
- [ ] Signed by all three heads

### Phase 3 · Weeks 5–8: route and score

1. **Write the SLA in these words:** *"Hand-raisers (demo and contact requests) get a human call within 1 hour of routing. Other MQLs get a first touch the same day. A lead not touched in its window is reassigned automatically."* ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]; [[A Complete Guide to Speed-to-Lead - Justin Norris|Justin Norris]]; [[The 1 Most Important Thing You Can Do With Your Leads - Implement an SLA|Jason Lemkin]])
   - *If you can staff it,* tighten to a 10-minute call, timed from routing; an auto-email doesn't count ([[A Complete Guide to Speed-to-Lead - Justin Norris|Justin Norris]]).
2. **Put a scheduler in the form's confirmation flow;** the SLA is the fallback ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]; [[A Complete Guide to Speed-to-Lead - Justin Norris|Justin Norris]]).
3. **Route hand-raisers only to reps free that minute,** and hold them in their own queue until someone is ([[A Complete Guide to Speed-to-Lead - Justin Norris|Justin Norris]]).
4. **Use round-robin before territories,** track rep capacity, and send low-scored leads to SDRs, not the bin ([[The 1 Most Important Thing You Can Do With Your Leads - Implement an SLA|Jason Lemkin]]).
5. **Decide on scoring.**
   - *Default:* no formal score. Route hand-raisers straight through and use fit to sort the rest ([[A Three-Tier Model for B2B Lead Engagement - Jon Miller|Jon Miller]]; [[The Evolution of Software Marketing Hey Marketing, Go Get This|Dave Kellogg]]).
   - *Switch to scoring when* reps can no longer work every lead inside the SLA (*no source sets the trigger; adaptation of the play*). Run a lead scoring council co-owned by sales and marketing, meeting at least quarterly, and calibrate the model to lifetime value, not MQL volume ([[Should We Lower Our Lead Score Threshold to Send More MQLs to AEs - Insight Partners|Insight Partners]]).

**Output:** the SLA, live routing, and an SLA dashboard by rep.
**Trap:** scoring that was never tested against closed deals. It's worth little ([[The Evolution of Software Marketing Hey Marketing, Go Get This|Dave Kellogg]]).

**Pause point, week 8:**
- [ ] SLA published and auto-reassignment tested
- [ ] Every routing branch has a fallback owner
- [ ] Overdue leads flagged daily, by rep
- [ ] Scoring decision written down

### Phase 4 · Weeks 6–10: coverage and credit

1. **Derive coverage from your own history.** Take new ARR closed ÷ week-3 current-quarter pipeline, average it over about nine quarters, and invert it. Never invert the win rate ([[Target Pipeline Coverage is Not the Inverse of Win Rate|Dave Kellogg]]).
   - *If you have little history,* estimate: coverage = (1 − share of bookings created and closed in-period) ÷ (dollar win rate × (1 − push rate)) ([[How Much Pipeline Coverage Do You Need - Hayes Davis|Hayes Davis]]).
2. **Work backwards from the new-ARR target.** Set what pipeline already in the quarter will yield against the target, and turn the gap into opportunities at your average deal size ([[Why Every Startup Needs an Inverted Demand Generation Funnel - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).
3. **Give each source a target and an owner:** marketing, SDR outbound, AE outbound, partners. Set them as opportunity counts and plan to about 110% of need while a new source spins up. Kellogg's reasonable mix for a ~$50M ARR enterprise company: marketing 60%, partners 20%, SDR 10%, AE 10%, each ±10 points, as of 2021-10 ([[The Four Sources of Pipeline and the Balance Across Them - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).
4. **Quarterback coverage:** forecast next quarter's day-one coverage across all four sources ([[The Top Two High-Level Questions About Sales - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).
5. **Credit one source per opportunity:** whoever generated it, not whoever processed it ([[The Four Sources of Pipeline and the Balance Across Them - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).
6. **Report influenced pipeline on its own row,** never added to sourced ([[CMO Board Meeting Success|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[Marketing's Old Scorecard Is Breaking|Laurie Ehrbar]]).
   - *If the sales cycle is under 90 days,* report sourced pipeline directly. *If longer,* model it with finance and state the assumptions ([[Revenue Diaries Entry 77 - Own the Number, Pipeline Council and the Revenue Handbook - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]).

**Output:** the coverage model, signed by the CFO and CRO, with a weekly pace per source.
**Trap:** accepting a 4–5x coverage ask. Walk the six causes first: unscrubbed pipeline, losing too much, low quality, sales outranking marketing, no nurture, reps squatting ([[What a Pipeline Coverage Target of 3x Says To Me|Dave Kellogg]]).

**Pause point, week 10:**
- [ ] Coverage from week-3 history, not win rate
- [ ] Four sources, four owners, counts not percentages
- [ ] Influenced on a separate row

### Phase 5 · Weeks 10–13: launch the rhythm

1. **Start these meetings, and keep them separate:**

   | Meeting | When | Who | Job |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | SLA check | Daily, automated | RevOps | Flag and reassign overdue leads |
   | Owner updates | Weekly, async | Each source owner | On or off track, pacing, fix ([[Revenue Diaries Entry 77 - Own the Number, Pipeline Council and the Revenue Handbook - Kyle Lacy\|Kyle Lacy]]) |
   | ELT pipeline review | Weekly | CEO chairs; CRO, CMO, CFO, RevOps | Off-track items and the weekly view ([[The CEO-Driven Pipeline Meeting - Andy MacMillan\|Andy MacMillan]]; [[No Surprises - Weekly Async Pipeline Updates and the Marketing Command Center - Kyle Lacy\|Kyle Lacy]]) → [[pipeline--elt-pipeline-review]] |
   | Pipeline generation sync | Weekly or fortnightly | Demand gen, SDR lead, sales, RevOps | Next one to two quarters, as a weekly creation pace by source and segment ([[Pipeline Generation Meeting vs Forecast Call - Pete Furseth\|Pete Furseth]]) |
   | Pipeline scrub | Weeks 2, 5, 8 | Sales managers | Check every opportunity against the rules; freeze the week-3 snapshot ([[Always Scrubbing the Pipeline Means Never Scrubbing the Pipeline\|Dave Kellogg]]) |
   | Pipeline council | Monthly | Exec team, run by RevOps | Four-part written memo ([[Revenue Diaries Entry 77 - Own the Number, Pipeline Council and the Revenue Handbook - Kyle Lacy\|Kyle Lacy]]) |

2. **Never let the forecast call double as the scrub** ([[Always Scrubbing the Pipeline Means Never Scrubbing the Pipeline|Dave Kellogg]]).
3. **When someone says "the leads are bad", run this ladder in order:**
   1. Were they worked? Check SLA compliance and response share ([[The 1 Most Important Thing You Can Do With Your Leads - Implement an SLA|Jason Lemkin]]).
   2. Rep by rep: leads received → share worked → speed → meetings. Starved, flooded or slow ([[Crafting an Effective BDR Metrics Scorecard - Carilu Dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]])?
   3. Did sales accept them at stage 2? Then "not real" is off the table ([[Always Scrubbing the Pipeline Means Never Scrubbing the Pipeline|Dave Kellogg]]).
   4. Compare stage-2 rejection rates by source. Falling lead quality shows up there, not in the win rate ([[Target Pipeline Coverage is Not the Inverse of Win Rate|Dave Kellogg]]).
   5. Still stuck? Sales, marketing and CS each mark the same 25 accounts fit or no-fit; the differences are the finding ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]).
   6. Sales wants more leads? A/B test a lower threshold by territory first ([[Should We Lower Our Lead Score Threshold to Send More MQLs to AEs - Insight Partners|Insight Partners]]).

**Output:** four quarters of rhythm on the calendar.
**Trap:** attribution arguments in the pipeline meeting. Treat attribution as directional insight, not a scoreboard ([[Dear Marketers, Is sales & marketing alignment harder than ever|MKT1]]), and count inbound pipeline narrowly: a hand-raiser asking for a meeting ([[Metrics that Matter to the Chief Marketing Officer - Ray Rike|Ray Rike]]).

**Pause point, week 13:**
- [ ] Scrub weeks booked for the year
- [ ] Forecast call and generation sync are separate meetings
- [ ] Ladder used on the last dispute

## Situations that change the play

| If… | Then |
|---|---|
| **Account-based or enterprise** | Agree ICP, account tiering and rules of engagement with sales first; without them, account-based won't end "lead quality" fights ([[Dear Marketers, Is sales & marketing alignment harder than ever\|MKT1]]). Qualify accounts, not leads ([[A Three-Tier Model for B2B Lead Engagement - Jon Miller\|Jon Miller]]) |
| **Product-led** | Report three lines: usage with a buyer (PQL), usage with no buyer (marketing finds one), top-down lead with no usage. Never point sales at a buyer-less account ([[The Ultimate Guide to Product-Led Sales - Elena Verna on Lenny's Podcast\|Elena Verna]]). Prioritise accounts, not users ([[PQLs Overpromised and Under-Delivered - Kyle Poyar\|Kyle Poyar]]). |
| **Short cycle, inbound-led SMB** | Less coverage needed. Plan from cost per meeting to AE capacity ([[How Much Pipeline Coverage Do You Need - Hayes Davis\|Hayes Davis]]; [[My Team Drives 4x Revenue Per AE vs Competitors - Aviv Canaani\|Aviv Canaani]]) |
| **High inbound, thin team** | AI takes first touch; humans close. Best leads go to your best closers ([[Our 1.25 Humans + 20 AI Agents Closed 140 Percent of What Our All-Human Sales Team Did\|Jason Lemkin]]) |
| **Teams don't share a meaning of "pipeline"** | Run a CEO-chaired meeting with both teams first ([[The CEO-Driven Pipeline Meeting - Andy MacMillan\|Andy MacMillan]]) → [[pipeline--elt-pipeline-review]] |
| **When not to run it:** a handful of reps, under about $10M ARR | Run Phase 1 and the SLA only. Skip scoring and stage taxonomy ([[The 1 Most Important Thing You Can Do With Your Leads - Implement an SLA\|Jason Lemkin]]) |

## Worked example

*Illustrative. Halden is a fictional company: Series B, $24M ARR, sales-led with SDRs, average deal $30K.*

**Phase 1 finding:** 31% of hand-raisers got a human call inside an hour; 22% got none. Fix one is the SLA.

**Coverage maths for next quarter:**

| Step | Number |
|---|---|
| New ARR target | $3.0M |
| Trailing nine-quarter week-3 conversion | 36% → coverage 2.8x |
| Pipeline needed at week 3 | $3.0M × 2.8 = $8.4M |
| Already dated into the quarter | $2.4M |
| Gap to create | $6.0M ÷ $30K = 200 opportunities |
| Plan to 110% | 220 stage-2 opportunities |

**By source:**

| Source | Share | Opportunities | Weekly pace (13 wks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marketing | 55% | 121 | 9.3 |
| Partners | 20% | 44 | 3.4 |
| SDR outbound | 15% | 33 | 2.5 |
| AE outbound | 10% | 22 | 1.7 |
| **Total** | 100% | **220** | 16.9 |

**The CRO asked for 4x.** The six-cause check found unscrubbed out-quarter opportunities; after the scrub, 2.8x held.

## Signs it's working, signs it's failing

- **Working by week 8:** 90%+ of hand-raisers called inside the SLA; 360Learning reps hit 90–100% on a 10-minute SLA, as of 2024-06 ([[A Complete Guide to Speed-to-Lead - Justin Norris|Justin Norris]]).
- **Working next quarter:** one pipeline number everywhere; disputes close with data within a week.
- **Failing:** pipeline drops "because we cleaned it"; coverage asks keep rising; pipegen beats plan while starting coverage falls, so the model is wrong ([[Why Great Marketers Look at Pipeline Coverage Not Just Pipeline Generation - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).

## Templates

**Weekly owner update** ([[Revenue Diaries Entry 77 - Own the Number, Pipeline Council and the Revenue Handbook - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]):

> **Source:** inbound. **Status:** on / off track. **Pacing:** N of M opportunities vs plan. *If off:* what's broken · what we're doing · what changes in the next 1–2 weeks.

**Monthly council memo:** 1) what's working, what needs fixing; 2) attainment by channel, segment and industry; 3) coverage for the next two quarters; 4) next quarter's goals by segment.

## Why this play: the evidence

**Where the sources agree** (3+ sources each):
- Fix follow-up before definitions ([[The 1 Most Important Thing You Can Do With Your Leads - Implement an SLA|Jason Lemkin]]; [[A Complete Guide to Speed-to-Lead - Justin Norris|Justin Norris]]; [[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]).
- Write the rules down and get them signed ([[Always Scrubbing the Pipeline Means Never Scrubbing the Pipeline|Dave Kellogg]]; [[Why You Should Always Create Sales Opportunities at Zero Dollar Value - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]; [[Revenue Diaries Entry 77 - Own the Number, Pipeline Council and the Revenue Handbook - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]; [[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]).
- Keep the forecast call separate from generation and from the scrub ([[Always Scrubbing the Pipeline Means Never Scrubbing the Pipeline|Dave Kellogg]]; [[Pipeline Generation Meeting vs Forecast Call - Pete Furseth|Pete Furseth]]; [[The Top Two High-Level Questions About Sales - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).
- Derive coverage from history, not win rate ([[Target Pipeline Coverage is Not the Inverse of Win Rate|Dave Kellogg]]; [[How Much Pipeline Coverage Do You Need - Hayes Davis|Hayes Davis]]; [[Metrics that Matter to the Chief Marketing Officer - Ray Rike|Ray Rike]]).
- One source per opportunity; influence on its own row ([[The Four Sources of Pipeline and the Balance Across Them - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]; [[CMO Board Meeting Success|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[Marketing's Old Scorecard Is Breaking|Laurie Ehrbar]]).

**Where they split, and the default this play takes:**
- **SLA window.** Under one hour (Kuehnle), same day or within 24 hours (Lemkin), 10 minutes (Norris). *Default:* one hour for hand-raisers, same day for the rest. Tighten to 10 minutes once routing knows who is free.
- **Score or not.** Insight's public article assumes a score and runs a council to tune it; Kellogg and Lemkin say untested scoring doesn't help; Miller says hand-raisers need no score. *Default:* none until reps can't work every lead inside the SLA.
- **What marketing is measured on.** Sourced pipeline at stage 2 (Kellogg), one shared number with influence (Ehrbar; MKT1's Dear Marketers), or closed-won with no assists ([[My Team Drives 4x Revenue Per AE vs Competitors - Aviv Canaani|Aviv Canaani]]). *Default:* stage-2 opportunity counts, influence on its own row. Tie-breaker: Lacy's 90-day rule.
- **How much coverage.** Kellogg's rule of thumb is 3x, and his last company hit plan on 2.5x, as of 2024-06 ([[Target Pipeline Coverage is Not the Inverse of Win Rate|Dave Kellogg]]); CROs increasingly ask for 3.5–5x, as of 2021-03 ([[What a Pipeline Coverage Target of 3x Says To Me|Dave Kellogg]]). *Default:* your own week-3 history; a higher ask is a symptom.
