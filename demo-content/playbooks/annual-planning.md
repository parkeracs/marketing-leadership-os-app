# Annual marketing planning

**The job:** agree what marketing will do next year, what it will cost and what it will deliver, so that the CEO, the CRO, finance and your own team all back the same plan.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pull this play when** | The company opens its planning cycle, in Q3 at the latest: next year's plan is won or lost in this year's Q3–Q4 ([[If You Want to Hit Next Year's Plan You Have to Make the Hires Now - Jason Lemkin\|Jason Lemkin]]) |
| **Time** | August to January for a calendar fiscal year, aiming for board approval at the Q3 board, around Thanksgiving, not in February (as of 2024-08) ([[Annual Planning Part 3 - The Marketing Budget - CJ Gustafson\|CJ Gustafson]]). The phase dates count back from that board. *No source covers the phase split; adaptation of the play* |
| **Owner** | You. **Key people:** CEO, CFO or FP&A, CRO, RevOps, your sub-team leads, the board |
| **You walk out with** | 1) A bottom-up pipeline model per motion. 2) A budget split into people, programmes and other. 3) Three scenarios and a priced menu of optional programmes. 4) A board deck of 5–8 slides. 5) A plan-on-a-page shared company-wide |
| **Done when** | The board has approved the plan, the CRO presents your numbers, and every marketer can say how their work ladders up |

## Before you start

Settle these by the end of August, or you rebuild in October.

1. **Get the planning calendar and finance's guardrails:** S&M and marketing as a share of revenue, the people/programmes split and the CAC or payback target. Without them, a $10M bottom-up ask meets a $5M envelope ([[How to Build a Marketing Plan and Get It Approved - Rowan Tonkin on Exit Five|Rowan Tonkin]]).
2. **Get the three company numbers the CEO and board have agreed:** the growth target, net revenue retention and the profitability balance. Every marketing dollar is judged against them ([[Presenting Your Yearly Plan to the Board - Stijn Hendrikse on B2B SaaS Marketing Snacks|Stijn Hendrikse]]; [[Annual Planning for SaaS Companies - Rory O'Driscoll|Rory O'Driscoll]]).
3. **Get into finance's model and set marketing's assumptions in it yourself,** from your own reporting, before anyone else sets them for you ([[Annual Planning Imperative - CAC Ratios - Carilu Dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]]).
4. **Get sales' draft capacity plan.** Marketing's pipeline number inherits sales' hiring ([[Think of Demandgen Like Any Other Sales Support Resource|Dave Kellogg]]).
5. **Clean this year's data:** spend, pipeline by source, conversion by channel and segment ([[Annual Planning Imperative - CAC Ratios - Carilu Dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]]).

## The play

### Phase 1 · August to early September: review and listen

1. **If you're new, or the CEO is,** walk the CEO through everything marketing does and agree the next 12 months' priorities before you budget anything ([[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail|Jason Lemkin]]).
2. **Run a stop / continue / start review** of every current programme with the people who run them. Never trend last year forward ([[Did You Just Make a Plan or a Budget|Dave Kellogg]]).
3. **Ask each peer exec what they need from marketing next year,** and invite their input now, not after the plan exists ([[Dear Marketers, How Do You Handle Marketing Requests|Dear Marketers (MKT1)]]).
4. **If the sales relationship is poor, fix it first** ([[AI Marketing Has A Disease - Kyle Lacy on Topline|Kyle Lacy]]).

**Output:** guardrails, company numbers, the stop / continue / start list and peer requests, in one document.
**Trap:** trending last year forward. That's a budget, not a plan, and budgets get cut across the board ([[Did You Just Make a Plan or a Budget|Dave Kellogg]]).

**Pause point, mid-September:**
- [ ] Guardrails and the three company numbers in hand
- [ ] Marketing's assumptions set in finance's model
- [ ] Stop / continue / start done with the team
- [ ] Sales' capacity draft received

### Phase 2 · September: strategy and bets

1. **Set roles with the "W":** you share context (strategy, targets, guardrails), sub-teams propose plans, you integrate them into one plan, then teams tweak and confirm. Say who makes the final call ([[The Secret to a Great Planning Process - W Framework - Lenny Rachitsky|Lenny Rachitsky]]).
2. **Run a team strategy session on bringing the corporate strategy to life,** after finance's first plan and before any modelling ([[The Bottom-Up Planning Imperative|Carilu Dietrich]]).
3. **Set concrete objectives, then cost each one,** so any cut drops an objective, not a percentage ([[Did You Just Make a Plan or a Budget|Dave Kellogg]]).
4. **Back a few focused, bold bets.** Don't spread the peanut butter ([[Allocation Models - The C-Suite Imperative - Carilu Dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]]).
5. **Publish goals and non-goals.** Non-goals make saying no defensible ([[Dear Marketers, How Do You Handle Marketing Requests|Dear Marketers (MKT1)]]).
6. **If execs pull marketing in different directions,** run "how would we spend $100?" with them in one room. The CEO makes the call if they can't agree ([[Allocation Models - The C-Suite Imperative - Carilu Dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]]).

**Output:** the bets with rough business cases, non-goals and the allocation split.
**Trap:** spreading budget evenly across every exec's priority, so no bet gets enough to work ([[Allocation Models - The C-Suite Imperative - Carilu Dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]]).

**Pause point, end of September:**
- [ ] Final-call owner named
- [ ] Bets named, each tied to a company number
- [ ] Non-goals published
- [ ] Allocation split agreed, not assumed

### Phase 3 · Late September to October: the numbers

1. **Build a reverse funnel per motion.** Walk up from the new-ARR target: deals (target ÷ ASP), then opportunities, then earlier stages, at your own conversion rates. Build one funnel each for new business, upsell and any segment whose ASP differs ([[Why Every Startup Needs an Inverted Demand Generation Funnel - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]; [[How to Build a Marketing Plan and Get It Approved - Rowan Tonkin on Exit Five|Rowan Tonkin]]).
2. **Use last quarter's actual rates, not the annual average,** and keep new and expansion close rates separate ([[Annual Planning Imperative - CAC Ratios - Carilu Dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]]).
3. **Size to the quota sales carries,** typically 20–30% above the financial plan (as of 2023-08), then subtract what sales, partners and product source themselves ([[Annual Planning Part 3 - The Marketing Budget - CJ Gustafson|CJ Gustafson]]).
4. **Time-phase pipeline.** SMB pipeline lands in-quarter, mid-market one quarter ahead, enterprise two (as of 2023-08). Start next year's demand gen in this year's Q4 ([[Annual Planning Part 3 - The Marketing Budget - CJ Gustafson|CJ Gustafson]]; [[If You Want to Hit Next Year's Plan You Have to Make the Hires Now - Jason Lemkin|Jason Lemkin]]; [[Why Every Startup Needs an Inverted Demand Generation Funnel - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).
5. **Set targets by source as opportunity counts, not percentages.** If a new source is spinning up, fund marketing to about 110% of need (as of 2021-10) ([[The Four Sources of Pipeline and the Balance Across Them - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).
6. **Size the budget from your CAC ratio, not % of revenue:** new ARR × CAC ratio. Break out marketing's own CAC components, or the ask looks fluffy next to sales ([[Annual Planning Imperative - CAC Ratios - Carilu Dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]]).
7. **Build headcount and budget month by month,** and hire ahead of need, as sales must for Q1–Q2 reps by mid-Q4 ([[If You Want to Hit Next Year's Plan You Have to Make the Hires Now - Jason Lemkin|Jason Lemkin]]). Then split people, programmes and tech, with enough people to spend the programmes ([[Annual Planning Part 3 - The Marketing Budget - CJ Gustafson|CJ Gustafson]]).
8. **Price pipeline in demand-gen cost per opportunity,** not cost per lead ([[How to Calculate Cost Per Opportunity - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).
9. **Give each sub-team a budget range** and have it build bottom-up inside it, every campaign mapped to a company goal, then roll up against the goals ([[How to Build a Marketing Plan and Get It Approved - Rowan Tonkin on Exit Five|Rowan Tonkin]]). Tag every line Direct, Influence, Enablement or Internal ops, with a "risk if cut" column ([[A Guide to Budgeting - Four Modes of Spend - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]).
10. **Name a protected brand bucket.** *Default:* Lacy's stage split, about 80/20 demand/brand below $20M ARR and 70/30 at $20–150M (as of 2025-11). *If you're a larger brand with high share,* move towards brand ([[Budgeting for Brand in 2026 - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]; [[The 5 Principles of Growth in B2B Marketing - Binet and Field|Binet and Field]]).
11. **Build three scenarios:** C-60 base, C-10 stretch about 20% above, and C-90 about 20% below for cash (as of 2024-11). Add a priced menu of optional programmes ([[The 3 Financial Plans You Need - C-90, C-60 and C-10 - Jason Lemkin|Jason Lemkin]]; [[Board-Level Questions On The Marketing Budget|Dave Kellogg]]).
12. **Close the top-down vs bottom-up gap with named, shared assumptions,** never silently. *If it won't close,* argue the profit goal; never get talked up on revenue to protect an expense line ([[The Bottom-Up Planning Imperative|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[Make a Plan That You Can Beat|Dave Kellogg]]).

**Output:** the model, the budget, the scenarios and the menu.
**Trap:** one averaged funnel or annual-average rates. It starves the small-deal team and flatters Q1 ([[Why Every Startup Needs an Inverted Demand Generation Funnel - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]; [[Annual Planning Imperative - CAC Ratios - Carilu Dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]]).

**Pause point, end of October:**
- [ ] One funnel per motion, on last quarter's rates
- [ ] Source targets in opportunities, agreed with each owner
- [ ] Headcount month by month
- [ ] Every line tagged with a mode and "risk if cut"
- [ ] CAC payback computed on the plan itself ([[Annual Planning Part 3 - The Marketing Budget - CJ Gustafson|CJ Gustafson]])

### Phase 4 · November: align and approve

1. **Pre-wire one to one: CRO, then CFO, then CEO.** Settle any CMO–CRO difference before the board sees the plan; visible misalignment is a red flag in itself ([[Board-Level Questions On The Marketing Budget|Dave Kellogg]]).
2. **Agree with the CRO how you'll show who drove revenue:** last-touch source and the team that worked the deal, side by side. Frame it as resourcing, not credit ([[Annual Planning That Doesn't Suck - Sam Kuehnle on Sour and SaaS|Sam Kuehnle]]).
3. **Present a go-to-market plan, not a marketing plan,** with the pipeline mix agreed with sales and CS. Open with what missed and the fix ([[Presenting Your Yearly Plan to the Board - Stijn Hendrikse on B2B SaaS Marketing Snacks|Stijn Hendrikse]]).
4. **Answer the board's real question:** *"What do I have to believe for this plan to work?"* Show about ten assumptions against two years' actuals ([[Annual Planning for SaaS Companies - Rory O'Driscoll|Rory O'Driscoll]]).
5. **Send a pre-read and say you want sign-off.** Stop presenting once you have it ([[How to present to your CEO|Wes Kao]]).
6. **If you're asked in the room for a number you don't believe,** ask for time and come back with the maths from your average quarter over the last four ([[Annual Planning That Doesn't Suck - Sam Kuehnle on Sour and SaaS|Sam Kuehnle]]).
7. **Pre-agree a mid-year check-in:** invest more if on track, hold if not ([[Annual Planning for SaaS Companies - Rory O'Driscoll|Rory O'Driscoll]]).

**Output:** the approved plan, ideally at the Q3 board around Thanksgiving (as of 2024-08) ([[Annual Planning Part 3 - The Marketing Budget - CJ Gustafson|CJ Gustafson]]).
**Trap:** a trended budget. "Where did this budget come from?" is the board's first question, and the answer must be one model that sales, marketing and finance share ([[Board-Level Questions On The Marketing Budget|Dave Kellogg]]).

**Pause point, before the board:**
- [ ] CRO presents the same numbers
- [ ] CFO's obvious objections answered in the deck
- [ ] Pre-read sent
- [ ] Mid-year check-in agreed

### Phase 5 · December to January: launch

1. **Share a plan-on-a-page company-wide,** with laddered goals and dependencies. Keep the detail for the team: "the beacon, not the blizzard" ([[Communicating Marketing Strategy - Plan-on-a-Page, Integrated Campaign and Budget Templates|Carilu Dietrich]]).
2. **Plan the team at 85–90% of capacity** (as of 2025-01), and say so in the plan ([[Communicating Marketing Strategy - Plan-on-a-Page, Integrated Campaign and Budget Templates|Carilu Dietrich]]).
3. **Push budget ownership to every people manager** ([[Did You Just Make a Plan or a Budget|Dave Kellogg]]).
4. **Restate the accepted trade-offs in every regular update,** and re-sell the plan mid-year before anyone reopens it ([[Why you should get buy-in throughout a project|Wes Kao]]).

**Output:** the plan-on-a-page and owned budgets.
**Trap:** treating sign-off as the finish line. Approval decays unless you re-earn it ([[Why you should get buy-in throughout a project|Wes Kao]]).

## Situations that change the play

| If… | Then |
|---|---|
| **The CRO's capacity model goes first and marketing gets what's left** | Reframe as one sales-and-marketing envelope. Ask the CEO which number matters, usually CAC, and commit to it ([[AI Marketing Has A Disease - Kyle Lacy on Topline\|Kyle Lacy]]) |
| **You can't see nine months ahead** | Treat the plan as a hypothesis. Agree each quarter what would justify going faster, staying or slowing ([[Stop Running Your Startup Like a Public Company\|Topline]]) |
| **You're told to cut** | Bring cut scenarios with a recommendation. Take them to the CRO first, and cut what's easiest to turn back on ([[Building a CEO-CMO Partnership That Works - Ashley Deibert on The Get\|Ashley Deibert]]) |
| **Q1 misses and the reflex is to hire** | Find the broken system first: demand generation, conversion or customer value ([[Stop Running Your Startup Like a Public Company\|Topline]]) |
| **Sales hires above plan** | Raise the demand-gen budget with it, per seller ([[Think of Demandgen Like Any Other Sales Support Resource\|Dave Kellogg]]) |
| **A peer asks for unplanned work** | "I can, but it delays [bet]. Which do you want first?" ([[Why no is a complete sentence is dangerous advice\|Wes Kao]]) |
| **When not to run it:** you arrive after the plan is approved | Run [[first-90-days]] and validate the inherited number there. *No source covers this case; adaptation of the play* |

## Worked example

*Illustrative. Fernhill is a fictional company: Series B, $30M ARR, mid-market, a 20% opportunity-to-close win rate, a $40K ASP, and marketing sourcing 60% of opportunities.*

**Pipeline model excerpt (new business, C-60):**

| Close quarter | New ARR | Deals | Opps needed | Marketing opps | Created in |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | $2.4M | 60 | 300 | 180 | Q4 this year |
| Q2 | $2.8M | 70 | 350 | 210 | Q1 |
| Q3 | $3.2M | 80 | 400 | 240 | Q2 |
| Q4 | $3.6M | 90 | 450 | 270 | Q3 |
| **Year** | **$12.0M** | **300** | **1,500** | **900** | |

**Budget:** 900 marketing opportunities at $3,000 demand-gen cost each gives $2.7M programmes. People $2.7M and tech $0.6M make $6.0M (45/45/10). Marketing CAC ratio: 0.5.

**Scenarios:** C-60 $12.0M · C-10 $14.4M · C-90 $9.6M. The CEO's top-down ask is $14M.

**Menu item:** "+120 opportunities for $450K ($3,000 plus 25%, each). At 20%, about 24 deals: $960K ARR."

**Board deck slide list:**
1. This year: what missed, why, the fix
2. Next year's goals, tied to growth, NRR and profit
3. Org chart
4. S&M efficiency: CAC ratio and payback against history
5. Budget mix and cost per opportunity
6. Pipeline and coverage by source, by quarter
7. Scenarios and the ten assumptions
8. The optional menu, priced

## Signs it's working, signs it's failing

- **Working at approval:** the CRO presents marketing's numbers; cuts, if any, remove whole objectives, not a flat percentage ([[Did You Just Make a Plan or a Budget|Dave Kellogg]]).
- **Working in Q1:** starting coverage about 3.0x (as of 2024-09) ([[Why Great Marketers Look at Pipeline Coverage Not Just Pipeline Generation - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).
- **Failing:** pipegen on plan while coverage falls, meaning the model has drifted; or coverage near 2.4x, from which most companies can't make plan ([[Why Great Marketers Look at Pipeline Coverage Not Just Pipeline Generation - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]). Or the budget is reopened mid-year.

## Templates

**Stop / continue / start** (one row per programme):

> Programme · Owner · Spend · Pipeline sourced · Call (stop, continue, start) · Why

**Budget submission line:**

> Line item · Budget holder · Budget · Mode (Direct, Influence, Enablement, Internal ops) · Pipeline $ or evidence · Metric · Risk if cut

**Plan-on-a-page:**
1. Company goals: growth, NRR, profit
2. Marketing's bets, each laddered to a goal
3. Non-goals
4. Pipeline targets by source, by quarter
5. Budget: people, programmes, other
6. Dependencies on sales, product and finance
7. Capacity held back for in-year asks

## Why this play: the evidence

**Where the sources agree** (3+ independent sources, used to build the default route):
- Build bottom-up from the target, on your own rates, and triangulate with top-down ([[Why Every Startup Needs an Inverted Demand Generation Funnel - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]; [[The Bottom-Up Planning Imperative|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[Annual Planning Part 3 - The Marketing Budget - CJ Gustafson|CJ Gustafson]]).
- Get finance's guardrails and the company numbers before you build ([[How to Build a Marketing Plan and Get It Approved - Rowan Tonkin on Exit Five|Rowan Tonkin]]; [[Annual Planning Part 3 - The Marketing Budget - CJ Gustafson|CJ Gustafson]]; [[Presenting Your Yearly Plan to the Board - Stijn Hendrikse on B2B SaaS Marketing Snacks|Stijn Hendrikse]]).
- Time-phase pipeline and start next year's demand gen in Q4 ([[Why Every Startup Needs an Inverted Demand Generation Funnel - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]; [[If You Want to Hit Next Year's Plan You Have to Make the Hires Now - Jason Lemkin|Jason Lemkin]]; [[Annual Planning Part 3 - The Marketing Budget - CJ Gustafson|CJ Gustafson]]).
- Bring scenarios and options, not one ask ([[How to Present an Operating Plan to Your Board - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]; [[The 3 Financial Plans You Need - C-90, C-60 and C-10 - Jason Lemkin|Jason Lemkin]]; [[Building a CEO-CMO Partnership That Works - Ashley Deibert on The Get|Ashley Deibert]]).
- Settle it with sales before any group review ([[Board-Level Questions On The Marketing Budget|Dave Kellogg]]; [[AI Marketing Has A Disease - Kyle Lacy on Topline|Kyle Lacy]]; [[Annual Planning That Doesn't Suck - Sam Kuehnle on Sour and SaaS|Sam Kuehnle]]).

**Where they split, and the default this play takes:**
- **How confident should the plan be?** Lemkin's base plan is a C-60; Kellogg wants a plan you can beat. *Default:* C-60 as the commitment, stretch funded separately. *Switch* lower if you can't see how to beat C-60: the commitment must be a plan you can beat ([[Make a Plan That You Can Beat|Dave Kellogg]]).
- **What weight do % of revenue benchmarks get?** Finance uses them as guardrails ([[How to Build a Marketing Plan and Get It Approved - Rowan Tonkin on Exit Five|Rowan Tonkin]]; [[Board-Level Questions On The Marketing Budget|Dave Kellogg]]); Dietrich rejects them for sizing, as they invite "yes, but we're different" (as of 2024-09). *Default:* size with the CAC ratio and treat the benchmark as a guardrail, never the ask.
- **Brand vs demand.** Lacy runs 20–40% brand by stage; Binet and Field put the B2B optimum near 46/54 brand/activation (as of 2019-10). *Default:* Lacy's split as a named bucket; move towards Binet and Field as share grows.
- **People vs programmes.** Kellogg's 45/45/10, Tonkin's 65/35 programmes/people, Gustafson's "rarely past 60/40". *Default:* headcount month by month, then check against 45/45/10 and stay inside 60/40 either way.
