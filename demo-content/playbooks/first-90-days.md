# First 90 days as a marketing leader

| | |
|---|---|
| **The job** | Turn a new marketing leadership role into earned trust and an agreed plan: learn the business fast, deliver something real early, and leave day 90 with a mandate, a plan and a budget the CEO and CRO back |
| **Pull this play when** | You've accepted a VP Marketing, Head of Marketing or CMO role. The play starts when you accept, not on day 1 |
| **Time and effort** | Acceptance to day 90 to diagnose and agree the plan. Visible impact lands in days 90–180, so day 90 is a checkpoint, not a finish line ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody\|Kevin Briody]]) |
| **Owner and people** | You. **With:** the CEO, the CRO, the CFO, product and CS leaders, your direct reports and skip-levels, the top two SDRs and best reps, and 10+ customers |
| **You walk out with** | A number you committed to in week 1 · a written mandate · one named funnel constraint and focus metric · a stop / stay / scale list · a team assessment · your own pipeline model · an approved day-90 readout · a board format you reuse every quarter |
| **Done when** | The CEO has approved the readout, success is written down in the CEO's words, and the CRO backs your pipeline numbers |

**Why it matters.** Failing to deliver promised results is the top reason CEOs and CFOs remove a CMO, and the miss usually starts with what you agree to in the first weeks. The figures are in *Numbers and tactics*.

## The play

A **route** in five phases. Each step gives the move, what it produces and the trap; the method sits in the deep dive linked at the end.

### Before day 1: settle the inputs

1. **Send the CEO a 60-day plan** with a force-ranked top five on page one, including what you *won't* change, and get the five agreed. *Output:* an agreed top five. *Trap:* a top five copied from your last company. ([[Before You Start as a VP Do a Real 60-Day Plan|Jason Lemkin]]) → [[first-90-days--listening-and-diagnosis#Before day 1|method]]
2. **Ask for the onboarding pack** (the last four board decks, the plan and its assumptions, budgets, dashboards, research) and for the CEO's visible endorsement. *Output:* the pack, read before you arrive. *Trap:* arriving to learn what you could have read. ([[How to Onboard Your CMO|Carilu Dietrich]]) → [[first-90-days--listening-and-diagnosis#Before day 1|method]]
3. **Negotiate the learning period** ("a 45-day assessment, then a readout") and sit in on a board meeting if one falls before you start. *Output:* an agreed date for your first readout. *Trap:* an open-ended "take your time" that runs out unannounced. ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]; [[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Isabelle Papoulias]])

### Phase 1 · Days 1–30: listen, commit, show up

4. **Hold the five conversations with the CEO,** in order: situation, expectations, style, resources, your development. Under-promise. *Output:* success defined in the CEO's words. *Trap:* absorbing unrealistic expectations instead of resetting them now. ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]) → [[first-90-days--readout-and-the-ceo#The five CEO conversations|method]]
5. **Commit to a number you control in week 1** (leads, meetings or pipeline from a channel) and show progress by week 4. Leave the inherited company number for Phase 3. *Output:* a weekly-tracked commitment. *Trap:* signing up to the inherited target before you've modelled it. ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]) → [[first-90-days--early-wins-and-the-number#The week-1 number|method]]
6. **Publish a short mandate document** in weeks 3–4: your focus, how to work with you, what you'll investigate. *Output:* a written mandate you use to say no. *Trap:* a deck nobody rereads. ([[Diary of My First 90 Days as a Fractional CMO - Asia Orangio|Asia Orangio]])
7. **Run the listening tour:** every senior leader gets the same six questions, word for word, and customers come first when internal meetings crowd them out. *Output:* a synthesis of where leaders disagree. *Trap:* a sample of only your direct reports. ([[Executive Listening Tour Interview Guide - Erik R Miller|Erik R. Miller]]; [[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]) → [[first-90-days--listening-and-diagnosis#The listening tour|method]]
8. **Get close to sales:** join live calls, meet the CRO weekly, and ask to join the CEO's go-to-market conversations with the CRO. *Output:* a weekly CRO slot. *Trap:* the CRO shaping the CEO's view of marketing while you're not in the room. ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]; [[How to Be the CMO Everyone Wants to Work With - Dave Kellogg on Exit Five|Dave Kellogg]])
9. **Send the CEO a Friday note** (what I learned, who I met, early thoughts), with each hunch labelled by how sure you are. *Output:* a readout that is half-agreed before you give it. *Trap:* saving it all for a big reveal. ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Bernd Leger]]; [[How to on-board yourself when you join a new team|Wes Kao]]; [[15 principles for managing up|Wes Kao]]) → [[first-90-days--readout-and-the-ceo#Managing up week to week|method]]

**Decision rules.**
- *Default:* act now on cheap, reversible or long-requested fixes; hold structural and budget moves until Phase 2. *If* the CEO is already asking what you're doing, ship a reversible win in the first three weeks.
- *Default:* keep one or two projects you do yourself and delegate the rest. *If* the team is small and under pipeline pressure, or you're the first marketing leader, be hands-on.

### Phase 2 · Days 31–60: diagnose and deliver early wins

10. **Run four checks in parallel:** objectives and the pipeline commitment; budget and the demand model; team sentiment and performance; sales and RevOps alignment. *Output:* the budget-versus-target gap, found early. *Trap:* finding it on day 90. ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]) → [[first-90-days--listening-and-diagnosis#The four checks and the funnel audit|method]]
11. **Audit the funnel to one constraint and one focus metric,** per product line or vertical that behaves differently. *Output:* a one-sentence constraint. *Trap:* a list of twenty problems. ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]) → [[first-90-days--listening-and-diagnosis#The four checks and the funnel audit|method]]
12. **Sort every inherited programme** into stop, stay the course or scale against that constraint, with a visible "not now" list. *Output:* the sort, with a reason per line. *Trap:* adding work before cutting any. ([[How to Prioritize Marketing Activities and Avoid Random Acts of Marketing|MKT1]]; [[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]) → [[first-90-days--listening-and-diagnosis#Sorting the inherited plan|method]]
13. **Cut the funnel to 4–5 metrics and get sales to sign off the definitions,** then agree one shared pipeline metric with the CRO around day 45. *Output:* signed definitions. *Trap:* building an attribution model instead. ([[The First 100 Days as a First-Time CMO - Kyle Lacy on UserGems|Kyle Lacy]]; [[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]])
14. **Assess the team** against written, weighted criteria, using the same template for everyone. *Output:* the big team changes, decided by day 90. *Trap:* judging people before writing the criteria. ([[Leading the Team You Inherit - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]) → [[first-90-days--listening-and-diagnosis#The team assessment|method]]
15. **Deliver collective early wins** that pass the quick-win test, at least one tied to what the CEO cares about most, and market them internally. *Output:* a drumbeat of wins delivered with the team. *Trap:* a personal, visible win that creates months of clean-up. ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]; [[The Quick Wins Paradox|Mark Van Buren and Todd Safferstone]]; [[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]) → [[first-90-days--early-wins-and-the-number#Choosing early wins|method]]

### Phase 3 · Days 61–90: the plan and the readout

16. **Build your own pipeline model** and reset the inherited number with it if the maths says so. *Output:* a model you can defend line by line. *Trap:* presenting someone else's model as yours. ([[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]; [[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]) → [[first-90-days--early-wins-and-the-number#Resetting the inherited number|method]]
17. **Pre-wire every number with the CRO, then the CFO and your GTM peers,** before the CEO sees it. *Output:* one voice with sales. *Trap:* a pipeline dispute in the readout. ([[CMO Board Meeting Success|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[Traits of a High-Performing CMO - Dave Kellogg on Exit Five|Dave Kellogg]]) → [[first-90-days--readout-and-the-ceo#Pre-wiring and the readout|method]]
18. **Make one end-to-end ask at day 90:** mission, plan, budget, team changes and the explicit asks, together. *Output:* an approved readout. *Trap:* asks spread across many meetings, so none land. ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]) → [[first-90-days--readout-and-the-ceo#Pre-wiring and the readout|method]]
19. **Set the board format you'll reuse every quarter,** and red-team the deck with execs playing the board. *Output:* a five-slide format. *Trap:* a new format every meeting. ([[The 3C Framework for CMO Board Meetings - Jaleh Rezaei|Jaleh Rezaei]]; [[Not Failing Board Meetings as a CMO - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]) → [[first-90-days--readout-and-the-ceo#The first board|method]]

**Decision rule.** *Default:* build on the CEO's view and what already works. *If* the evidence contradicts it, put the facts on the table as a dispassionate analyst. *If* the "marketing problem" is really a strategy problem, take it to the C-suite. (Split 5 in *The evidence*.)

### Phase 4 · Days 90–180: impact

20. **Reallocate the budget around day 80, baseline marketing's contribution at day 90, and hold a retrospective at day 180.** *Output:* a contribution baseline. *Trap:* treating the approved readout as the finish. ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]) → [[first-90-days--readout-and-the-ceo#Days 90–180|method]]

## What the best do differently

- **Most leaders treat the listening tour as introductions; the best run it as research.** They ask every leader the same questions word for word and treat the disagreement as the finding, because where leaders differ on something that should have one answer is usually the real problem ([[Executive Listening Tour Interview Guide - Erik R Miller|Erik R. Miller]]; [[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]).
- **Most leaders either accept the inherited target or refuse to commit; the best do both halves.** They commit in week 1 to a number they control, then reset the inherited one with their own model before day 90, because "if you can't defend the maths, you don't own it yet" ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]; [[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]; [[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]).
- **Most leaders chase a personal, visible quick win; the best pick collective ones that are also correct.** They finish what the team had stalled and bring forward decisions they'd make anyway, because the way a win is delivered decides whether it builds or burns credibility ([[The Quick Wins Paradox|Mark Van Buren and Todd Safferstone]]; [[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Paige O'Neill]]; [[The First 90 Days as CMO What I Actually Did - Dom O'Brien|Dom O'Brien]]).
- **Most leaders save their conclusions for the readout; the best seed them weekly and pre-wire sales and finance.** By day 90 the CEO is confirming a plan the CRO and CFO already back, so the one ask lands ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Bernd Leger]]; [[CMO Board Meeting Success|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[Traits of a High-Performing CMO - Dave Kellogg on Exit Five|Dave Kellogg]]).
- **Most leaders import the last company's playbook; the best treat it as data.** They protect what works and build the first bet from evidence inside this business, because a different CEO, market and stage turns old lessons into scar tissue ([[How Your Best Lessons Can Become Your Biggest Obstacles|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[Before You Start as a VP Do a Real 60-Day Plan|Jason Lemkin]]; [[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]).

## By stage

|---|---|---|---|
| **The leader you're hired to be** | A VP who formalises a flat team and builds new functions, usually after a fundraise ([[What Kind of CMO Should You Hire - Carilu Dietrich\|Carilu Dietrich]]) | A scale expert who builds routines and systems; around $20M a CMO "quarterback" becomes the hire ([[CMO Success Stage by Stage - Kimberly Whitler and Jonathan Metrick\|Whitler and Metrick]]; [[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail\|Jason Lemkin]]) | An officer who leads a layered organisation with a broader remit, as the company reshapes to look like a public one ([[CMO Success Stage by Stage - Kimberly Whitler and Jonathan Metrick\|Whitler and Metrick]]; [[What Kind of CMO Should You Hire - Carilu Dietrich\|Carilu Dietrich]]) |

| **Who the readout must convince** | The founder-CEO and the CRO (inferred from Lemkin on founder views) | The CEO, the CFO and a board that asks "where would you put $X?" (inferred: Dietrich's board question, placed at the stage where the board starts asking it) | A board and executive team preparing to be judged from outside, so definitions and pipeline numbers must hold up to scrutiny (inferred from Dietrich and Whitler and Metrick on the pre-IPO reshaping; no source says it of the first 90 days) |

**Across every column:** marketing patterns change sharply at roughly $15M, $50M and $150M, so a playbook from another stage misfires even when it worked ([[The Top 5 Reasons CMOs Get Fired|Carilu Dietrich]]).

## Situations that change the play

Diagnose the situation **per area, not per company**: demand gen can be a turnaround while brand is a sustaining success ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]], STARS model).

| If… | then… |
|---|---|
| **Turnaround:** pipeline missing, board impatient | Compress the listening phase ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody\|Kevin Briody]]); make fast, tough calls ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins\|Michael Watkins]]); commit to a number in week 1 ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It\|Jason Lemkin]]) |
| **Interim seat** | Reverse the order: the big bet first, then quick wins, with a tightly scoped mandate agreed before you join ([[Here Is How I Approach Starting a New Job - Elena Verna\|Elena Verna]]) |
| **Realignment:** numbers fine, trouble ahead | Use the listening tour to build the case for change; play people's words back to them ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing\|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]) |
| **Sustaining success** | Protect what works first; quick wins are optimisations you're about 80% sure of; then "invent the challenge" ([[Here Is How I Approach Starting a New Job - Elena Verna\|Elena Verna]]; [[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins\|Michael Watkins]]) |
| **First-ever marketing leader** | You are the function: write, run experiments, build campaigns ([[The 1 Clear Sign a VP Will Fail\|Jason Lemkin]]); budget time to explain modern marketing ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody\|Kevin Briody]]) |
| **The founder was the marketer** | Build on the founder's evidence of what works ([[Before You Start as a VP Do a Real 60-Day Plan\|Jason Lemkin]]; [[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail\|Jason Lemkin]]) and ask for founder time ([[first-90-days--readout-and-the-ceo#The five CEO conversations\|method]]) |
| **You inherited a target you didn't set** | Validate or reset it with data inside 90 days, before it becomes yours ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing\|Scott Gordon]]) |
| **Large inherited team** | Delegate most of it; keep one or two projects yourself ([[What New Marketing Leaders Get Wrong - Dear Marketers\|MKT1]]) |
| **PLG or self-serve** | Look for wins in the website funnel, CTAs at the biggest drop-off, and ownerless pricing ([[How to make an impact in your first 90 days - Kyle Poyar\|Kyle Poyar]]) |
| **A board meeting lands before day 90** | Share a high-level direction anyway: why you joined and the risks you see ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months\|Carilu Dietrich]]) |
| **Fractional engagement** | Run the light version: an information download, people and financial alignment, then "get executors executing" ([[Diary of My First 90 Days as a Fractional CMO - Asia Orangio\|Asia Orangio]]) |
| **Enterprise, PE-backed or GCC** | The tenure and expectation data is mostly US large-company; flag it before applying it |
| **When not to run it:** the job is head of demand gen, not the whole function | Use [[demand-gen--mandate-and-strategy]] and [[demand-gen--prioritising]] instead |

## Worked example

*Illustrative. [[example-company|Northwind]]: Series B spend-management software, $18M ARR, sales-led with SDRs. You start as VP Marketing on 6 January.*

- **Situation:** demand gen is a realignment. Pipeline runs $7.4M a quarter against an inherited $9M target; marketing sources 55%.
- **Week 1 commitment to Priya (CEO):** "About 100 of our 250 demos a month come from companies with 200+ employees. Marketing will take that to 150 a month by the end of March, tracked weekly." The $9M is left alone until the model exists.
- **Week 3 board:** a direction, not a plan: why you joined, and two risks (the demo mix and an unvalidated target).
- **Listening-tour variance:** asked "who owns the key decisions?", Priya says marketing should own the number, Marcus (CRO) notes the SDRs report to him, and Elena (CFO) runs planning and doubts brand spend. The finding: nobody agrees who owns pipeline creation, which the day-45 shared metric settles.
- **Day-45 constraint sentence:** "Only 18% of our 250 demos become stage-2 opportunities, against 35% in our best segment (500–2,000 employees), because 60% of demos come from companies under 200. Focus metric: demo → stage 2 in the 500–2,000 segment." Kuehnle's *Lead Gen 101*.
- **The sort:** stop paid social (it fills the wrong demos) and the two field events (not tied to stage 2); stay the course on webinars and content (reach the buyer, don't touch the constraint); scale the partner programme (its demos skew to 500–2,000).
- **Pre-wiring order:** Marcus (every pipeline number and the stage-2 definition), Elena (the $2.6M programme budget against the model), the GTM peers, then Priya.
- **The model, day 75:** marketing's 45 stage-2 opportunities a month are worth $4.05M a quarter; other sources add $3.35M. Lifting demo → stage 2 from 18% to 24% on the same 250 demos gives 60 a month, $5.4M from marketing, **$8.75M in total**.
- **Readout, slide 1:** "$7.4M a quarter against $9M, because our demos come from the wrong companies. $8.75M by Q3 from the same 250 demos by moving spend into the 500–2,000 segment. That leaves $0.25M: the asks either close it or reset the target to $8.75M. The asks: fill the two vacancies, and one decision on SDR focus."

**Documented cases (real, from the sources):**
- *Bernd Leger:* a weekly Friday note to the CEO, then one end-to-end assessment at day 90; the CEO gave him 90 minutes with the whole exec team ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Renegade Marketing]]).
- *Isabelle Papoulias:* showed her written 90-day plan to the CEO on day 3, and sat in on a board meeting before her start date ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Renegade Marketing]]).
- *Scott Gordon:* reset marketing's expected share of pipeline at his new company inside 90 days; leadership respected the data-backed "this is what we can meet" ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Renegade Marketing]]).
- *Kyle Lacy (Lessonly):* gave no feedback for 30 days, then cut an over-instrumented funnel to four or five metrics that became the basis of sales alignment ([[The First 100 Days as a First-Time CMO - Kyle Lacy on UserGems|Kyle Lacy]]).
- *Heidi Bullock (Tealium):* set a long-term strategy and a short-term demand goal, and hit the short-term goal fast ([[The B2B CMO Imperative 2026 - Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan|Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan]]).

## How good are you?

**Mid-point test, day 45:** can you state the CEO's day-90 expectations in the CEO's words, and does the CRO agree with your read of the pipeline?

**Signs it's working:** a number committed in week 1 with progress by week 4 ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]) · the sales leader trusts you by day 60 ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]) · clear dashboards by about month 2 ([[How to Onboard Your CMO|Carilu Dietrich]]) · major team changes known by day 90 ([[Leading the Team You Inherit - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]) · one readout approved ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]).

**Signs it's failing:** no number by the end of week 1 · fewer than 10 customer conversations by day 30 (this play's threshold; no source sets one) · your first visible act is a rebrand, a website or a martech change · something you were hired to fix hasn't started by day 90, so it will be quietly taken off you ([[If Your VP Hasnt Done Some Key Part of the Job by Day 90 They Never Will|Jason Lemkin]]) · the readout surprises anyone in the room.

| | Competent | Strong | Top 1% |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The number** | Commits to the inherited target and reports against it | Commits in week 1 to a number marketing controls and shows progress by week 4 | Resets the company number with a model the CFO adopts as the plan, then beats it |
| **The diagnosis** | Lists what's broken | Names one constraint and one focus metric per segment | Can show the reasoning behind every stop, stay and scale call, and moves budget on it without asking for more |
| **CEO and peers** | Holds regular one-to-ones | Arrives at the readout with the CRO and CFO already agreed | Is in the CEO's go-to-market conversations; the CRO defends marketing's numbers when you're absent |
| **The team** | Has met everyone | Has a written assessment and decided changes by day 90 | The team ships a win the CEO credits to them |
| **The readout** | Is approved with changes | Is approved as presented, with one ask | Becomes the plan the annual plan and board format start from |

The top 1% column rests on the mission's definition (a forecast the CEO and CFO trust, diagnosis before spend, peer of the CRO, flat-budget reallocation); see [[setup/mission|the mission]].

## The evidence

**The stakes.** S&P 500 CMOs last 4.1 years against a 5.0-year C-suite average, as of 2026-01 ([[CMO Tenure Study 2025 - The Evolution of Marketing Leadership|Spencer Stuart]]). In growth-stage SaaS the canon puts the median nearer 18–24 months, as of 2026-03 ([[How to Be the CMO Everyone Wants to Work With - Dave Kellogg on Exit Five|Dave Kellogg]]). Only 27% of CEOs and CFOs say their CMO beat expectations, and failing to deliver promised results is the top removal trigger, at 69%, as of 2025-02 ([[Gartner - Only 45 Percent of CMOs Surpass Senior Executive Expectations|Gartner]]).

### Where the sources agree

Each move has 3+ independent sources, at least one from the canon, and none contradicting. These built the default route.

- **Write your plan down early and share it with the CEO.** It surfaces misaligned priorities while they're cheap to fix, and gives you something to point to when you say no ([[Before You Start as a VP Do a Real 60-Day Plan|Jason Lemkin]]; [[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]; [[Diary of My First 90 Days as a Fractional CMO - Asia Orangio|Asia Orangio]]). *Steps 1, 6, 9.*
- **Talk to customers early, before the internal view takes over.** Customers tell you what the building can't ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[A CMOs First 90 Days - CMO Coffee Talk with Matt Heinz|Matt Heinz]]; [[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]). *Step 7.*
- **Get close to sales in the first weeks:** join live calls, meet the sales leader weekly, get sales to sign off the funnel definitions ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]; [[A CMOs First 90 Days - CMO Coffee Talk with Matt Heinz|Matt Heinz]]; [[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]; [[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]). *Steps 8, 13.*
- **Protect what already works, and treat your last company's playbook as data** ([[How Your Best Lessons Can Become Your Biggest Obstacles|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[Before You Start as a VP Do a Real 60-Day Plan|Jason Lemkin]]; [[Here Is How I Approach Starting a New Job - Elena Verna|Elena Verna]]; [[The First 90 Days as CMO What I Actually Did - Dom O'Brien|Dom O'Brien]]; [[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]). *Step 1.*
- **Audit the inherited plan, budget and programmes before committing new money or work** ([[How to Prioritize Marketing Activities and Avoid Random Acts of Marketing|MKT1]]; [[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]; [[Diary of My First 90 Days as a Fractional CMO - Asia Orangio|Asia Orangio]]; [[The First 90 Days as CMO What I Actually Did - Dom O'Brien|Dom O'Brien]]; [[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]). *Steps 10–12.*
- **Size any number you commit to from the bottom up, and under-promise** ([[The Top 5 Reasons CMOs Get Fired|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]; [[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]; [[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]; [[Gartner - Only 45 Percent of CMOs Surpass Senior Executive Expectations|Gartner]]). *Steps 5, 16.*
- **Pre-align the numbers with the CRO and CFO before they reach the CEO or the board** ([[CMO Board Meeting Success|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[Not Failing Board Meetings as a CMO - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]; [[Traits of a High-Performing CMO - Dave Kellogg on Exit Five|Dave Kellogg]]). *Step 17.*

### Where the sources disagree

**1. Act from day one, or observe first?**
- *Act:* the 90-day ramp is a luxury; around day 31 the CEO starts asking what you're doing, so lead by delivering ([[What to Do in Your First 30 Days as a Marketing Leader - Dave Gerhardt|Dave Gerhardt]]; [[Here Is How I Approach Starting a New Job - Elena Verna|Elena Verna]]). Both canon.
- *Observe:* Kyle Lacy gave no feedback for his first 30 days ([[The First 100 Days as a First-Time CMO - Kyle Lacy on UserGems|Kyle Lacy]]). Canon.
- *Where the canon lands:* both in parallel ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]; [[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]), as does Briody's three-lenses-at-once model. See [[run-the-quick-wins-and-the-roadmap-in-parallel]].
- *Default:* act on cheap, reversible or long-requested fixes; hold structural and budget moves until the diagnosis (Phase 1 decision rules).
- **Decided by:** how reversible and how cheap the move is.

**2. Quick wins, or early wins?**
- *Quick wins:* grab them early; they buy time ([[What to Do in Your First 30 Days as a Marketing Leader - Dave Gerhardt|Dave Gerhardt]]; [[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]).
- *Early wins:* they must be substantive and collective; personal quick wins are where new leaders derail ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]; [[The Quick Wins Paradox|Mark Van Buren and Todd Safferstone]]). O'Brien calls it "visible vs correct" ([[The First 90 Days as CMO What I Actually Did - Dom O'Brien|Dom O'Brien]]).
- *The canon:* Gerhardt sits with quick wins; Dietrich asks for a drumbeat of visible wins tied to the CEO's passion area ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]).
- *Default (convergence):* collective wins that pass Briody's quick-win test, delivered with the team, at least one tied to the CEO's passion area (step 15). Briody, Van Buren and Safferstone, Watkins, Gartner's toolkit and Dietrich all fit it.
- **Decided by:** whether the CEO is already asking "what are you doing?". If so, ship cheap, reversible fixes early; if you're the first marketing leader with nobody senior to check your bets, diagnose first.

**3. Commit to a number early, or hold off?**
- *Commit early:* in week 1 ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]). Canon.
- *Hold off:* validate or reset the inherited number inside 90 days ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]; [[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]).
- *Default (the resolution the research converged on):* commit early to a number you control; reset the company-level number with data by day 90 (steps 5 and 16). Miller and Sloan's two tracks fit it.
- **Decided by:** whether the number is one marketing controls or one it inherited.

**4. Do the work, or delegate?**
- *Do:* CMOs who stop doing the work fail, until at least $250M ARR ([[The 1 Clear Sign a VP Will Fail|Jason Lemkin]]).
- *Delegate:* new leaders fail by not delegating ([[What New Marketing Leaders Get Wrong - Dear Marketers|MKT1]]). Both canon.
- *Default (convergence):* keep one or two projects you do yourself and delegate the rest; Kramer's reconciliation, which Lemkin's note also allows (Phase 1 decision rules).
- **Decided by:** the size of the inherited team and the pipeline pressure on it.

**5. Follow the CEO, or lead them?**
- *Follow:* build on the CEO's view and what works; a contrarian plan is a bet you have to win ([[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail|Jason Lemkin]]).
- *Serve sales and educate the CEO:* when in doubt serve sales, and lead a product-background CEO to understand marketing ([[Traits of a High-Performing CMO - Dave Kellogg on Exit Five|Dave Kellogg]]).
- *Escalate:* if the "marketing problem" is really a strategy problem, take it to the C-suite (Carilu Dietrich; no library note linked).
- *Where they meet:* all three align first and differ on how hard to lead. All canon.
- *Default (the shared ground):* build on the CEO's view, and switch as in the Phase 3 decision rule.
- **Decided by:** whether the CEO's view of what works is backed by current evidence. Lemkin's own exception: a CEO who was never the company's marketer leaves you more room.

**6. How fast to change people?**
- *Fast:* move fast on toxic or weak leaders and bring in people you trust ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]). Practitioner CMOs "rarely regretted moving fast" ([[A CMOs First 90 Days - CMO Coffee Talk with Matt Heinz|Matt Heinz]]).
- *Fair chance:* give people a fair chance, but know your major changes by day 90 ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).
- *The canon:* fast.
- *Default (convergence: three sources including the canon, and Watkins agrees on the day-90 deadline):* move fast on toxic or weak leaders once the written assessment confirms it; have every major change decided by day 90 (step 14).
- **Decided by:** the STARS situation of the area: a turnaround moves faster.

**7. Is 90 days even the right window?**
- *A false finish line:* impact lands in days 90–180 ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]).
- *Just a quarter:* Watkins never claimed transitions take 90 days; a quarter is a planning unit ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).
- *Default:* neither source treats day 90 as the end, so the play runs Phase 4 to day 180. Neither source is canon; the default is the side both lean towards.
- **Decided by:** the length of the sales cycle: Lemkin expects meaningful progress within one sales cycle, so a cycle longer than 90 days pushes visible impact past the readout.

### Numbers and tactics ⏳

- S&P 500 CMOs last 4.1 years against a 5.0-year C-suite average, as of 2026-01 ([[CMO Tenure Study 2025 - The Evolution of Marketing Leadership|Spencer Stuart]]).
- Growth-stage SaaS median CMO tenure is cited as 18–24 months, as of 2026-03 ([[How to Be the CMO Everyone Wants to Work With - Dave Kellogg on Exit Five|Dave Kellogg]]). No primary data exists for venture-backed VP Marketing tenure.
- 27% of CEOs and CFOs say their CMO exceeded expectations; the top removal trigger is failing to deliver promised results (69%); 22% have significant clarity on marketing's accountabilities, as of 2025-02 ([[Gartner - Only 45 Percent of CMOs Surpass Senior Executive Expectations|Gartner]]).
- 40% of executives underperform on their objectives during a transition (Gartner new-to-role toolkit, date unknown) ([[Gartner - Only 45 Percent of CMOs Surpass Senior Executive Expectations|Gartner]]).
- Commit to a number in week 1 and show progress by week 4; the sales leader should trust you within about 60 days, as of 2025-08 ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]).
- For a marketing head, the day-90 bar is genuinely new leads, as of 2023-05 ([[If Your VP Hasnt Done Some Key Part of the Job by Day 90 They Never Will|Jason Lemkin]]).
- Validate or reset an inherited pipeline target inside 90 days; one cited case expected a budget of 5–10% of revenue, found 1%, and still faced a 25% growth target, as of 2026-06 ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]).
- CMO180 milestones: CEO day 1, CRO day 3, CFO day 5; first quick win around day 25; shared pipeline metric with the CRO around day 45; 3–5 priorities by day 50; budget reallocation around day 80; readout and contribution baseline at day 90; retrospective at day 180 (date unknown) ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]).
- Clear marketing dashboards within the first 2 months; CEO and new CMO meet twice a week for the first few months, as of 2024-04 ([[How to Onboard Your CMO|Carilu Dietrich]]).
- Learning period: a 45-day assessment, then a readout; major team changes known by day 90, as of 2020-08 ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).
- Quick wins are things you're about 80% sure of; shop a big bet around day 30, as of 2025-07 ([[Here Is How I Approach Starting a New Job - Elena Verna|Elena Verna]]).
- Fix operational leaks (lead response, MQL quality, handoff friction) in 30–60 days, as of 2025-11 ([[The B2B CMO Imperative 2026 - Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan|Jon Miller]]).
- Several CMOs aimed to deliver a team assessment or plan within about 30 days, or before the first board if sooner, as of 2024-01 ([[A CMOs First 90 Days - CMO Coffee Talk with Matt Heinz|Matt Heinz]]).
- Leaders keep doing hands-on work until at least $250M ARR, as of 2025-11 ([[The 1 Clear Sign a VP Will Fail|Jason Lemkin]]).
- A CMO becomes the right hire at roughly $20M ARR; most SaaS CEOs hold strong marketing opinions by about $10M ARR, as of 2022-12 ([[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail|Jason Lemkin]]).
- Marketing patterns change at stage gates of roughly $15M, $50M, $150M, $300M and $1B, as of 2024-02 ([[The Top 5 Reasons CMOs Get Fired|Carilu Dietrich]]).
- A first marketing team stays flat until about 8–10 people, as of 2023-06 ([[What Kind of CMO Should You Hire - Carilu Dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]]).
