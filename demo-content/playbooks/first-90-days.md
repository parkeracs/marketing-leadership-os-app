# First 90 days as a marketing leader

**The job:** turn a new marketing leadership role into earned trust and a plan that is agreed and funded. Learn the business fast, deliver something real early, and leave day 90 with a mandate the CEO and CRO back.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pull this play when** | You've accepted a VP Marketing, Head of Marketing or CMO role, from the day you sign |
| **Time** | 90 days, plus 1–2 weeks before day 1. Visible impact lands in days 90–180, so day 90 is a checkpoint, not the finish ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody\|Kevin Briody]]) |
| **Owner** | You. **Key people:** CEO, CRO or sales leader, CFO, your direct reports, 10+ customers |
| **You walk out with** | 1) A pipeline number you committed to in week 1. 2) A one-sentence funnel constraint. 3) A stop / continue / scale list. 4) A team assessment. 5) An approved day-90 readout: plan, budget, team changes and asks |
| **Done when** | The CEO has approved the readout, and the CRO is presenting the numbers alongside you, not reacting to them |

## Before you start

Settle these before day 1. Each one is harder to fix once you're in the seat.

1. **Agree what the role is, in writing.** Is it strategist, commercialiser or P&L owner, and how will success be measured? When responsibilities, expectations and measures don't line up, the role sets anyone in it up to fail ([[Why CMOs Never Last|Whitler and Morgan]]; [[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).
2. **Send the CEO a 60-day plan** with a force-ranked top five on page one, including what you *won't* change, and get explicit agreement on the five ([[Before You Start as a VP Do a Real 60-Day Plan|Jason Lemkin]]).
3. **Negotiate the learning period.** Use these words: *"A 45-day assessment, then a readout and plan."* ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]])
4. **Ask for the onboarding pack:** the last four board decks, the fundraising deck, OKRs with scores, the financial plan and its assumptions, budgets, dashboards and any research. If a board meeting falls before your start date, ask to sit in ([[How to Onboard Your CMO|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Gier, Leger and Papoulias]]).
5. **Name the situation**, per area rather than per company: turnaround, realignment, sustaining success or first-ever leader. The situation sets the pace of everything below → *Situations that change the play* ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).

## The play

### Phase 1 · Days 1–30: listen and commit

1. **Commit to a number in week 1.** Pick a lead, meeting or pipeline number that marketing controls, and show progress by week 4 ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]).
   - *Default:* commit to a number you control now.
   - *If you inherited a company-level pipeline target,* don't adopt it yet. Validate it or reset it with data by day 90 (Phase 3) ([[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]).
2. **Hold five conversations with the CEO, in this order:** situation, expectations, working style, resources, your development. Under-promise in every one ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).
3. **Run the listening tour** in this order: CEO → CRO → CFO → product and CS → direct reports → skip-levels → the top two SDRs and best reps. Each conversation sharpens the next ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]). Ask every senior leader the same six questions, word for word (*Templates* below). **The variance between their answers is the finding** ([[Executive Listening Tour Interview Guide - Erik R Miller|Erik R. Miller]]).
4. **Book 10+ customer conversations before day 30,** and protect them when internal meetings crowd the calendar. Sit in on live sales calls too ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]).
5. **Send the CEO a Friday note every week:** what I learned, who I met, early hunches, each labelled with how confident you are (*Templates*) ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Gier, Leger and Papoulias]]; [[How to on-board yourself when you join a new team|Wes Kao]]).
6. **Meet the sales leader one-to-one every week** from week 1, and sit in on their pipeline meetings ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]; [[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]).

**Output:** the committed number, CEO expectations written in the CEO's words, and listening-tour notes in one document.
**Trap:** a listening sample made up only of your own team, or "at my last company…" in every meeting. Treat your last playbook as data, not rules ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]; [[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]).

**Pause point, day 30:**
- [ ] Number committed and tracked weekly
- [ ] Five CEO conversations held
- [ ] 10+ customer conversations
- [ ] Same six questions asked of every senior leader
- [ ] Four Friday notes sent

### Phase 2 · Days 31–60: diagnose and win

1. **Audit the funnel to one constraint.** Run it end to end, from site visit to closed-won, separately for each product line or segment that behaves differently. Finish with **one sentence naming the constraint and one focus metric** that proves it. Read the levers in this order: demo → SQO rate, demo quality and volume, win rate, ACV, paid spend, CAC payback ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]).

   | If the funnel shows… | The constraint is… | Focus metric |
   |---|---|---|
   | Demos rarely become qualified opportunities | Lead gen as activity, not pipeline | Demo → SQO |
   | Real demand, but the handoff breaks | Marketing / sales misalignment | Demo → SQO |
   | Deals qualify but don't close | Product or positioning, not top of funnel | Win rate |
   | A healthy funnel against a target with no maths behind it | The goal, not the funnel | Volume vs target |
   | Conversion fine, unit economics not | Expensive wins | CAC payback |
   | Everything healthy | A volume game | Spend and pipeline volume |

2. **Sort every inherited programme** into **stop, continue or scale**, judged against the constraint:
   - scale what helps fix the constraint;
   - keep running, at low effort and with no new money, what reaches your ideal customers but doesn't touch it;
   - stop the rest.

   Make it part of a stop-doing audit, before any new money goes in ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]).
3. **Check the money in parallel, not after.** Cover the pipeline commitment and its assumptions, the budget against the demand model, and the definitions sales and marketing use. A gap between budget and target found on day 90 is too late ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]).
4. **Ship one early win with the team.** It must pass all four tests:
   - visible to the people who matter;
   - doable in 60 days with what you have;
   - fixes a pain someone has already named;
   - delivered *with* the team, not by you alone.

   *Default:* finish a stalled project or fix an operational leak, such as lead response time or handoff friction ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]; [[The Quick Wins Paradox|Van Buren and Safferstone]]). *In a turnaround,* make the big bet first and take quick wins second ([[Here Is How I Approach Starting a New Job - Elena Verna|Elena Verna]]).
5. **Assess the team against written, weighted criteria**: competence, judgement, energy, focus, people skills and trust. Use the same template for everyone. Decide the big team changes by day 90, and in a turnaround sooner ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]; [[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]).
6. **Cut the funnel to 4–5 metrics, agree the definitions with sales,** and get sales to sign them off ([[The First 100 Days as a First-Time CMO - Kyle Lacy on UserGems|Kyle Lacy]]).

**Output:** the constraint sentence, the stop / continue / scale list, one shipped win, the team assessment, and the signed definitions.
**Trap:** restyling instead of diagnosing. A rebrand, a website or a martech change inside 90 days creates months of clean-up and proves nothing about pipeline ([[The First 90 Days as CMO What I Actually Did - Dom O'Brien|Dom O'Brien]]).

**Pause point, day 60:**
- [ ] Constraint named in one sentence
- [ ] Stop / continue / scale list shared
- [ ] One win shipped and shown to the CEO
- [ ] Team assessed on the same criteria
- [ ] Definitions signed by sales

### Phase 3 · Days 61–90: plan and readout

1. **Build your own pipeline model.** Cover conversion by segment, where deals stall, and which sources actually create pipeline. Reset the inherited number with it if the model says so ([[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]).
2. **Pre-wire, in this order:** CRO, then CFO, then your go-to-market peers, then the CEO. Every number agreed with the CRO before the CEO sees it ([[CMO Board Meeting Success|Carilu Dietrich]]).
3. **Make one end-to-end ask at the readout.** Cover mission, plan, budget, team changes and the explicit asks. Asks spread across many meetings get lost ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Gier, Leger and Papoulias]]).
4. **Commit only to a number you can beat.** Failing to deliver promised results is the top reason marketing leaders are removed, and the miss starts on the day the target is set ([[The Top 5 Reasons CMOs Get Fired|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[Make a Plan That You Can Beat|Dave Kellogg]]).
5. **Set the board format you'll reuse every quarter.** Answer three questions every time (the strategy, the present: did we hit pipeline and why, and the future: will we hit next quarter), with every number pre-agreed with the CRO and an explicit ask ([[CMO Board Meeting Success|Carilu Dietrich]]).

**Output:** the approved readout (outline in *Templates*) and the reusable board format.
**Trap:** a contrarian plan. Build on the CEO's tested view and on what already works. A plan that overrides the CEO is a bet you must win ([[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail|Jason Lemkin]]).

**Pause point, day 90:**
- [ ] Model built from your own data
- [ ] CRO and CFO saw every number first
- [ ] Readout approved
- [ ] Next quarter's number set from the model, not inherited

## Situations that change the play

| If… | Then |
|---|---|
| **Turnaround:** pipeline missing, board impatient | Compress Phase 1 to 2–3 weeks. Make the big bet before the quick wins. Decide on people sooner ([[Here Is How I Approach Starting a New Job - Elena Verna\|Elena Verna]]; [[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins\|Michael Watkins]]) |
| **Sustaining success:** numbers fine | Protect what works first. Quick wins are optimisations you're about 80% sure of. Take a big bet to the CEO around day 30 ([[Here Is How I Approach Starting a New Job - Elena Verna\|Elena Verna]]) |
| **First-ever marketing leader** | You are the function. Write, run experiments and build campaigns yourself while you listen. "Strategy people" fail here ([[The 1 Clear Sign a VP Will Fail\|Jason Lemkin]]) |
| **Founder-CEO was the marketer** | Extract the founder's thesis first. Ask for about 3 hours in the first two weeks, then 45 minutes a week ([[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other\|Kristen Pecka]]) |
| **A board meeting lands before day 90** | Share a direction anyway: why you joined and the risks you see ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months\|Carilu Dietrich]]) |
| **Fractional or interim** | Run one month per area: information download, people and money, then execution. Keep forecast, budget and plan as separate documents ([[Diary of My First 90 Days as a Fractional CMO - Asia Orangio\|Asia Orangio]]) |

## Worked example

*Illustrative. Northwind is a fictional company: Series B, $18M ARR, sales-led, an inherited target of $9M new pipeline a quarter.*

**Week 1 commitment:** "Marketing will generate 60 qualified meetings a month by week 6, from 41 today." The commitment is controllable, so the $9M target is left alone until the model exists.

**Day-45 constraint sentence:** "We have enough demos. Only 18% become qualified opportunities, against 35% in the segment that closes fastest, because 60% of demos come from companies under 50 employees that we don't sell to well. Focus metric: demo → SQO in the 200–1,000-employee segment."

**Stop / continue / scale (extract):**

| Programme | Call | Why |
|---|---|---|
| SMB paid social | Stop | Fills demos outside the ideal customer profile |
| Webinar series | Continue | Reaches buyers, doesn't touch the constraint, no new money |
| Partner co-selling in mid-market | Scale | Highest demo → SQO of any source |

**Day-90 readout, slide 1:** "We will move from $6.1M to $8.4M quality pipeline a quarter by Q3, by moving spend to mid-market. That is below the $9M target. Here is the model, and here is what closes the gap."

## Signs it's working, signs it's failing

- **Working at day 45:** you can state the CEO's day-90 expectations in the CEO's own words, and the CRO agrees with your read of the pipeline.
- **Working at day 90:** the CRO defends marketing's numbers when you're not in the room, and the CEO asks you about next quarter, not just this one.
- **Failing:** you have no number by week 2; your sample is only your own team; your first visible act is a rebrand; asks arrive one meeting at a time; the readout surprises anyone in the room.

## Templates

**The six listening-tour questions** (ask every senior leader, word for word, separately; [[Executive Listening Tour Interview Guide - Erik R Miller|Erik R. Miller]]):
1. What are the top three priorities for the company this year?
2. How will we know we've succeeded?
3. Who owns the key decisions in go-to-market?
4. What is the biggest constraint on growth?
5. What should marketing change first?
6. What should marketing not touch?

**The Friday note:**

> **Week N.** *Learned:* three bullets. *Met:* names and roles. *Hunches:* one or two, each marked high, medium or low confidence. *Next week:* what I'll test.

**The day-90 readout outline:**
1. Where we are: the constraint sentence and the funnel numbers behind it.
2. What I learned: the listening-tour variance and the customers' words.
3. What we'll stop, keep and scale.
4. The model and the number we commit to.
5. Team changes.
6. The asks: budget, people, decisions, all in one place.

## Why this play: the evidence

**Where the sources agree** (3+ independent sources, used to build the default route):
- Write the plan down early and share it with the CEO ([[Before You Start as a VP Do a Real 60-Day Plan|Jason Lemkin]]; [[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Gier, Leger and Papoulias]]; [[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).
- Talk to customers early ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]; [[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]).
- Get close to sales in the first weeks ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]; [[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]; [[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Gier, Leger and Papoulias]]).
- Protect what already works, and treat your last playbook as data ([[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail|Jason Lemkin]]; [[Here Is How I Approach Starting a New Job - Elena Verna|Elena Verna]]; [[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]).
- Audit the inherited plan before committing new money ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]; [[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]; [[The First 90 Days as CMO What I Actually Did - Dom O'Brien|Dom O'Brien]]).
- Size any number from the bottom up, and under-promise ([[The Top 5 Reasons CMOs Get Fired|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]; [[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]).

**Where they split, and the default this play takes:**
- **Commit to a number in week 1, or hold off?** Lemkin says commit at once; Gordon and Pecka say validate the inherited number first. *Default:* commit to a number you control in week 1, and reset the company number with your model by day 90.
- **Quick wins or substantive wins?** Operators say grab quick wins. Watkins and HBR's quick-wins research say personal quick wins derail new leaders ([[The Quick Wins Paradox|Van Buren and Safferstone]]). *Default:* one win that passes the four tests, delivered with the team.
- **Change people fast, or give them time?** Dietrich and Lemkin say move fast on weak leaders; Watkins says give a fair chance. *Default:* assess everyone by day 60 and decide by day 90; in a turnaround, sooner.
