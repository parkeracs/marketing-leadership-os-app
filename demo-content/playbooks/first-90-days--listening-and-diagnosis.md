# First 90 days: listening and diagnosis

## What this part is

Everything between accepting the role and knowing what's actually going on: the asks before day 1, the listening tour, and the diagnosis of the money, the funnel, the inherited plan and the team. It expands steps 1–3, 7 and 10–14 of [[first-90-days|the hub]]. The number you commit to is in [[first-90-days--early-wins-and-the-number]]; the CEO conversations are in [[first-90-days--readout-and-the-ceo]].

## The idea underneath

**You are paid for a diagnosis before you are paid for a plan, and the diagnosis has a shelf life.** A newcomer sees what insiders have stopped seeing, and people tell a newcomer things in the first two weeks that they won't say once you're part of the establishment ([[The First 90 Days as CMO What I Actually Did - Dom O'Brien|Dom O'Brien]]). Beginner's mind is a one-time asset ([[How to on-board yourself when you join a new team|Wes Kao]]), and you will never be listened to more, or have a better chance to propose a radical idea, than now ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]). So the listening has to be fast and structured, not polite and open-ended.

**Listening is research, not relationship-building.** Most tours produce impressions. A structured one produces answers you can line up against each other: ask every leader the same questions, and where they quietly disagree on something that should have one answer is the most useful thing you'll learn ([[Executive Listening Tour Interview Guide - Erik R Miller|Erik R. Miller]]). Contradictions between smart people are structural signals about how information flows, not a contest to referee ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]).

**CMOs fail by misreading the system, not for lack of marketing skill.** The three ways are pattern-matching from the last company, moving fast on the wrong things, and listening so long the new-hire goodwill runs out ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]). Your past wins are data, not rules: a different CEO, culture and market moment move the goalposts ([[How Your Best Lessons Can Become Your Biggest Obstacles|Carilu Dietrich]]).

**Diagnose one constraint, not a list.** Demand gen amplifies whatever already exists, so more spend on a broken funnel buys more of the break. The audit should end in one bottleneck you can say in a sentence and one metric that proves it ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]). The same logic drives the sort of inherited programmes: you can't keep adding work, so cut against the constraint before you add anything ([[How to Prioritize Marketing Activities and Avoid Random Acts of Marketing|MKT1]]).

**Run the money in parallel with the listening.** The gap between the budget and the target is the most expensive thing to find late. A new leader who finds it at day 90 has already been judged against a number the budget could never buy ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]).

## The method

### Before day 1

1. **Send the CEO a 60-day plan** with a force-ranked top five on page one: what you'd do first, who you'd hire, what you'd fix and what you would *not* change. Go through it until you agree the five. The "won't change" list shows respect for what already works ([[Before You Start as a VP Do a Real 60-Day Plan|Jason Lemkin]]).
2. **Ask for the onboarding pack** if the CEO doesn't offer it: the last four board decks, the fundraising deck, OKRs with scoring, the financial plan with sales and finance assumptions, budgets, dashboards, QBRs and research. Ask for visible endorsement to the board and company before you arrive, and for a briefing on the "unmentionables", the conflicts under existing relationships ([[How to Onboard Your CMO|Carilu Dietrich]]).
3. **Ask to sit in on a board meeting** if one falls before your start date. One CMO called it the single most valuable onboarding step ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Isabelle Papoulias]], at 44:18).
4. **List the information, access and decisions you'll need,** plus one easy win for month one ([[How to on-board yourself when you join a new team|Wes Kao]]).
5. **Negotiate the learning period up front:** "a 45-day assessment, then a readout and plan" ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).
6. **Stress-test your plan** against three questions: what if the budget is cut in month two, the inherited launch slips, or the person described as excellent turns out to be the structural problem? ([[The First 90 Days as CMO What I Actually Did - Dom O'Brien|Dom O'Brien]])

### The listening tour

1. **Sequence it so each conversation sharpens the next:** CEO → CRO → CFO → product and CS → direct reports → skip-levels → peers and partners. Add the top two SDRs and the best reps, and run it in person ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]; [[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]).
2. **Ask every senior leader the six alignment questions** (*Templates*), separately and word for word. The variance between answers is the finding ([[Executive Listening Tour Interview Guide - Erik R Miller|Erik R. Miller]]).
3. **When two smart people contradict each other** (the CEO says the ICP is enterprise, the top AE says mid-market closes faster), don't pick a winner. Ask each why they see it differently ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]). To make an ICP disagreement concrete, run the 25-account test ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]).
4. **Protect customer conversations first** when internal meetings start eating your calendar. Customers give you what the inside doesn't have, and something unique to bring to the board before you're "indoctrinated" ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]).
5. **Use the CEO's doors:** shadow their prospect and customer meetings, tour the most successful customers and listen to win-loss recordings ([[How to Onboard Your CMO|Carilu Dietrich]]).
6. **Sit in on sales calls and QBRs, then play their words back.** One CMO presented last at a two-day sales QBR using sellers' own quotes, with their photos ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]).
7. **Share hunches as you go,** labelled with your level of conviction, and turn each call or document into analysis plus a recommendation ([[How to on-board yourself when you join a new team|Wes Kao]]; [[15 principles for managing up|Wes Kao]]).
8. **Stop saying "at my last company".** Read how this CEO actually makes decisions: one company aligns everything to the CEO's preferences, another brings a democracy of leaders along ([[How Your Best Lessons Can Become Your Biggest Obstacles|Carilu Dietrich]]).
9. **If the CRO has more CEO time on go-to-market than you,** ask to join those conversations. Otherwise the CRO shapes the CEO's view of marketing, sometimes with marketing as the scapegoat ([[How to Be the CMO Everyone Wants to Work With - Dave Kellogg on Exit Five|Dave Kellogg]], at 19:47).
10. **Stop adding interviews when new themes stop appearing** ([[Executive Listening Tour Interview Guide - Erik R Miller|Erik R. Miller]]).

### The four checks and the funnel audit

1. **Run four checks in parallel, not in sequence:** business objectives and the pipeline commitment; budget and the demand model; team initiatives, sentiment and performance; alignment with sales and RevOps. Start from what the CEO and CFO promised the board ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]).
2. **Find out how the company defines trial and conversion** in the business *and* in the systems. They often differ ([[Diary of My First 90 Days as a Fractional CMO - Asia Orangio|Asia Orangio]]).
3. **Run a revenue performance audit end to end,** from impressions or site visits to closed-won, separately for each product line or vertical that behaves differently. Match it to one of the six diagnoses (*Templates*) and write the constraint sentence ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]; diagnoses from Affect's funnel audit).
4. **Read the levers in a fixed order:** demo → SQO rate, demo quality and volume, win rate, ACV, paid spend, CAC payback ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]).
5. **If the funnel has plenty of entries but few closes,** the gap is positioning and enablement, not top of funnel: fix that before hiring for demand gen ([[Growth Tactics from OpenAI and Stripes First Marketer - Krithika Shankarraman on Lennys Podcast|Krithika Shankarraman]], at 11:17). Start with win/loss on the last 20–30 closed deals ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]).
6. **If the funnel is over-instrumented,** cut it to 4–5 metrics and agree the definitions with sales before anything else. Then install a weekly funnel-and-forecast meeting across sales, marketing and CS ([[The First 100 Days as a First-Time CMO - Kyle Lacy on UserGems|Kyle Lacy]]). Use the vault's shared definitions in [[pipeline--definitions-and-routing]] rather than writing new ones.
7. **If you're short of time,** commission external audits of parts of the function ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]).

### Sorting the inherited plan

1. **List every programme running and sort each into stop, stay the course or scale** before adding anything. Keep a visible "not now" list with a reason for each item, so other teams see their idea was considered ([[How to Prioritize Marketing Activities and Avoid Random Acts of Marketing|MKT1]]).
2. **Sort against the constraint the audit named:** scale what helps fix it; stay the course, at low effort and with no new money, on what reaches the ICP but doesn't touch it; stop the rest ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]).
3. **Hunt for what everyone has learned to work around:** an unquestioned channel, a weekly report nobody reads, an agency older than the team. Ask naive questions in weeks 1 and 2: "Why do we do it this way?", "What would happen if we stopped this?" ([[The First 90 Days as CMO What I Actually Did - Dom O'Brien|Dom O'Brien]]).
4. **Open the "marketing graveyard":** old personas, decks and past attempts, read with healthy scepticism, so you know what was already tried ([[Diary of My First 90 Days as a Fractional CMO - Asia Orangio|Asia Orangio]]).

### The team assessment

1. **Write and weight your criteria before you assess anyone:** competence, judgement, energy, focus, people skills, trust, weighted to 100% for this situation and how critical each role is. In a turnaround you need people already up to speed; in sustaining success you can afford to develop high-potentials ([[Leading the Team You Inherit - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).
2. **Use the same interview template for everyone:** strengths and weaknesses of the current strategy, the challenges and opportunities, under-used resources, how the team could work better, what they'd prioritise in your seat. Watch who volunteers information and who blames ([[Leading the Team You Inherit - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).
3. **Play the findings back to the team** and watch the reaction: it shows the culture and the power dynamics ([[Leading the Team You Inherit - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).
4. **Map capability on Kellogg's pillars:** product marketing, demand gen, corporate comms and sales development. Score yourself too ([[The Decomposition of Marketing|Dave Kellogg]]).
5. **Move fast on toxic or weak leaders,** and bring in one or two people you've worked with before ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]). Tell the keepers first, so a departure doesn't make everyone wonder if they're next ([[A CMOs First 90 Days - CMO Coffee Talk with Matt Heinz|Matt Heinz]]). The timing split is below.

## By stage

- **At ~$10–20M ARR** the team work is clarifying who does what, assessing capability and creating transition paths in a flat org; **at $30–50M** it's setting up functional leads; **from $50M** it's adding a layer ([[Building Your B2B Marketing Org from 1M to 100M ARR - Agata Celmerowski|Agata Celmerowski]]).
- **The listening tour widens with stage** (inferred from the hub's *By stage* table): at $10M the founder's channel views are the main input; by $100M directors and finance carry more of it. Stop when new themes stop appearing; a small company gets there quickly ([[Executive Listening Tour Interview Guide - Erik R Miller|Erik R. Miller]]).

## Templates

**The 60-day plan, page one** ([[Before You Start as a VP Do a Real 60-Day Plan|Jason Lemkin]]):
> **Top five, force-ranked:** 1. … 2. … 3. … 4. … 5. …
> **What I won't change:** …
> **Agreed with [CEO] on [date].**

**The six alignment questions** (every senior leader, separately, word for word; [[Executive Listening Tour Interview Guide - Erik R Miller|Erik R. Miller]]):
1. What are the top three priorities?
2. How will we know we've succeeded a year from now?
3. Who owns the key decisions?
4. What is the biggest constraint on growth?
5. What should the new leader change first?
6. What should the new leader not touch?

**The 25-account ICP test** ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]): sales, marketing and CS each mark fit or no-fit on the same list of about 25 accounts. Where the answers differ is the finding.

**The six funnel diagnoses** ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]; from Affect's funnel audit):

| Diagnosis | What the funnel shows | Focus metric |
|---|---|---|
| Lead Gen 101 | Activity, not pipeline: demos rarely become SQOs, and those that do don't close | Demo → SQO |
| Marketing / Sales Misalignment | Real demand, broken handoff | Demo → SQO |
| Product / Market Fit Issue | Deals qualify but don't close; more top of funnel won't help | Win rate |
| Unrealistic Goals | A healthy funnel against a target with no maths behind it | Volume |
| Efficient Funnel, Expensive Wins | Conversion is fine, unit economics aren't | CAC payback |
| Healthy Funnel | A volume game | Spend and pipeline volume |

**The constraint sentence:** "Our constraint is ___, shown by ___. Focus metric: ___." One per product line or vertical that behaves differently.

**The stop / stay the course / scale sort** (the sort: [[How to Prioritize Marketing Activities and Avoid Random Acts of Marketing|MKT1]]; the rule: [[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]):

| Programme | Helps fix the constraint? | Reaches the ICP? | Call | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | Yes | | Scale | |
| | No | Yes | Stay the course: low effort, no new money | |
| | No | No | Stop | |

Plus a "not now" list, with a reason for each item.

## Numbers and tactics ⏳

- A budget gap found on day 90 is too late; one cited case expected 5–10% of revenue as budget, found 1%, and faced a 25% growth target, as of 2026-06 ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]).
- ICP test on about 25 accounts; win/loss on the last 20–30 closed deals; for a handoff problem, expect demo → SQO to move within 30–60 days, as of 2026-10 ([[B2B SaaS Demand Gen Checklist - Sam Kuehnle|Sam Kuehnle]]).
- Agreeing budget and goals with a CEO who also acted as CFO took two to three weeks of iteration, as of 2025-06 ([[Diary of My First 90 Days as a Fractional CMO - Asia Orangio|Asia Orangio]]).
- Funnel cut to four or five metrics (date unknown) ([[The First 100 Days as a First-Time CMO - Kyle Lacy on UserGems|Kyle Lacy]]).
- A marketing team stays flat until about 8–10 people, as of 2023-06 ([[What Kind of CMO Should You Hire - Carilu Dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]]).

## Where the sources disagree

**How fast to change people?** The canon says move fast on weak or toxic leaders ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]), and practitioner CMOs "rarely regretted moving fast" ([[A CMOs First 90 Days - CMO Coffee Talk with Matt Heinz|Matt Heinz]]). Watkins says give people a fair chance but know your major changes by day 90, sooner in a turnaround ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]). *Default:* move fast on toxic or weak leaders once the written assessment confirms it; decide every major change by day 90. **Decided by:** the STARS situation of the area. Full record in [[first-90-days#Where the sources disagree|the hub]], split 6.

## Failure modes

- A listening sample made up only of direct reports.
- Pattern-matching from the last company, or listening so long the goodwill runs out ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]).
- Finding the gap between the budget and the target too late ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]).
- Restyling instead of diagnosing: a week-1 rebrand, website or martech change ([[The First 90 Days as CMO What I Actually Did - Dom O'Brien|Dom O'Brien]]; [[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]).
- Building an attribution model in the first 100 days instead of a simple funnel sales understands ([[The First 100 Days as a First-Time CMO - Kyle Lacy on UserGems|Kyle Lacy]]).
