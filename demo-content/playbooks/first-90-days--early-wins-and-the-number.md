# First 90 days: early wins and the number

## What this part is

The two things a CEO judges first: the number you commit to, and the wins you deliver while you're still learning. It expands steps 5, 15 and 16 of [[first-90-days|the hub]]. Related principle: [[run-the-quick-wins-and-the-roadmap-in-parallel]] (draft).

## The idea underneath

**The CEO's scorecard is written in the first 90 days, and the first line is a number.** Committing to leads, meetings or pipeline is the signal that you're doing the job; strategy without a commitment reads as a red flag ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]). What a new leader hasn't started by day 90 is usually never done, and gets quietly taken off them; for a marketing head the day-90 bar is genuinely new leads ([[If Your VP Hasnt Done Some Key Part of the Job by Day 90 They Never Will|Jason Lemkin]]).

**But the number that gets CMOs fired is usually the one they agreed to on day one.** Failing to deliver the strategy's promised results is the top removal trigger ([[Gartner - Only 45 Percent of CMOs Surpass Senior Executive Expectations|Gartner]]), and missed expectations start when the expectations are set ([[The Top 5 Reasons CMOs Get Fired|Carilu Dietrich]]). Inherited targets often come from generic analyst benchmarks that don't fit the business ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]). So the resolution is two numbers: one you control and commit to now, and the company number you reset with your own model before it becomes yours. An inherited model tells you what someone else believed; if you can't defend the maths, you don't own it yet ([[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]).

**Predictability buys political capital.** Running a short-term demand goal you hit fast alongside the long-term strategy is what earns the room for longer bets ([[The B2B CMO Imperative 2026 - Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan|Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan]]).

**How you win matters as much as the win.** New leaders who struggled showed the behaviours of someone over-intent on an early win; those who thrived delivered it with their team, and some leaders got the win and still lost because of how they got it ([[The Quick Wins Paradox|Mark Van Buren and Todd Safferstone]]). The trap is optimising for *visible* wins over *correct* ones ([[The First 90 Days as CMO What I Actually Did - Dom O'Brien|Dom O'Brien]]). The best early wins serve the long game and model the behaviour you want ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).

**Do the work yourself.** The best predictor of a VP failing is whether they still do the work; hands-on work gives you patterns and early warnings that dashboards show too late ([[The 1 Clear Sign a VP Will Fail|Jason Lemkin]]).

## The method

### The week-1 number

1. **Commit in week 1 to a lead, MQL, SQL or pipeline number you control,** and show progress against it by week 4 ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]).
2. **Ask the CRO what share of pipeline sales leadership is counting on marketing to source,** so the hidden expectation surfaces before you commit ([[A CMOs First 90 Days - CMO Coffee Talk with Matt Heinz|Matt Heinz]]).
3. **Run two tracks:** the short-term demand goal you hit fast, and the long-term strategy beside it ([[The B2B CMO Imperative 2026 - Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan|Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan]]).
4. **Start whatever you were hired to fix before day 90,** or expect it to be removed from your remit ([[If Your VP Hasnt Done Some Key Part of the Job by Day 90 They Never Will|Jason Lemkin]]).
5. **Do some of the work yourself:** write, run experiments, build campaigns ([[The 1 Clear Sign a VP Will Fail|Jason Lemkin]]).

### Resetting the inherited number

1. **Check the inherited target against the evidence, in this order:** what the CEO and CFO promised the board, then the pipeline commitment and its assumptions, then the budget and the demand model ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]], at 14:27).
2. **Build your own pipeline model by day 90:** conversion by segment, where deals stall, which sources really influence pipeline, and what marketing can actually move ([[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]). The modelling method lives in [[forecasting--the-marketing-model]].
3. **Negotiate the number from that bottom-up analysis,** and don't sign up to one you can't beat ([[The Top 5 Reasons CMOs Get Fired|Carilu Dietrich]]).
4. **Reset it with data inside 90 days.** Leadership respects a data-backed "this is what we can meet" ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]).

### Choosing early wins

1. **Test every candidate quick win:** visible to the people who matter, doable in 60 days with what you have, fixes a pain others have already named, and builds credibility with a named group. If it needs major resources or a strategy, it isn't a quick win ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]).
2. **Protect what already works first.** Quick wins are things you're about 80% sure of from past patterns, usually optimisations. Ask everyone "if you were me, what would you focus on?" Shop a big bet around day 30, and ask people why it won't work before you commit ([[Here Is How I Approach Starting a New Job - Elena Verna|Elena Verna]]).
3. **Look first where the win is already half-built:** a project the team started that stalled for lack of a sponsor ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Paige O'Neill]]). Choose one you can repeat, not a one-off ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Ali McCarthy]]).
4. **Or deliver what sales keeps asking for:** tactical messaging from a two-week tiger team, help on a launch's packaging and pricing, opening doors on live deals ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]).
5. **Fix the obvious operational leaks** (lead response time, MQL quality, handoff friction) in 30–60 days to buy permission for bigger change ([[The B2B CMO Imperative 2026 - Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan|Jon Miller]]).
6. **Make it collective,** delivered with your team. Watch for the five derailers: obsessing over detail, reacting badly to criticism, intimidating people, jumping to conclusions and micromanaging ([[The Quick Wins Paradox|Mark Van Buren and Todd Safferstone]]).
7. **Accelerate decisions you'd make anyway:** if the agency should go in month three, cut it in month two. Don't invent early decisions to look decisive ([[The First 90 Days as CMO What I Actually Did - Dom O'Brien|Dom O'Brien]]). If you need time on the big calls, make a few small, visible decisions early while you defer them ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).
8. **In a turnaround or interim seat, reverse the order:** the big bet first, then quick wins ([[Here Is How I Approach Starting a New Job - Elena Verna|Elena Verna]] for interim seats; [[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]] on fast, tough calls in a turnaround).
9. **In a PLG or self-serve business,** audit the website and funnel, test CTAs at the biggest drop-off, and look at pricing and packaging, which often has no owner ([[How to make an impact in your first 90 days - Kyle Poyar|Kyle Poyar]]).

### Marketing the wins

1. **Design a drumbeat of visible wins,** and tie at least one to the CEO's passion area, not just what you think matters ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]).
2. **Market them internally:** treat other teams as an audience, share goals and plans in advance, and give them a fixed rhythm for input ([[How to market marketing internally|MKT1]]).

## By stage

- **At ~$10M ARR** the founder-CEO usually holds firm, tested channel views, so the first number and the first wins should build on that engine rather than replace it ([[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail|Jason Lemkin]]; [[Before You Start as a VP Do a Real 60-Day Plan|Jason Lemkin]]).
- **From ~$30M ARR** a board starts asking where an extra $X would go and what it would return (inferred placement of [[The Top 5 Reasons CMOs Get Fired|Carilu Dietrich]]'s board question), so the reset model has to answer it.
- **At ~$100M ARR** the number sits inside a forecast the board, and later outside investors, will hold the company to (inferred; no source covers the first 90 days at this stage).

## Templates

**The quick-win test** ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]): it passes only if it is all four.
- [ ] visible to the people who matter
- [ ] doable in 60 days with what you have
- [ ] fixes a pain others have already named
- [ ] builds credibility with a named group

**The week-1 commitment:** "Marketing will take [metric we control] from [today] to [target] by [date], tracked weekly, with visible progress by week 4. The company target of [inherited number] stays as it is until I bring you a model on [date]."

**The number-reset sequence** ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]; [[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]):
1. What did the CEO and CFO promise the board?
2. What is the pipeline commitment, and what assumptions sit under it?
3. What budget and demand model does it rest on?
4. My model: conversion by segment, where deals stall, which sources influence pipeline, what marketing can move.
5. The number I can beat, and what would close the gap.

## Numbers and tactics ⏳

- Commit in week 1, progress by week 4; expect real wins inside 90 days and meaningful progress within one sales cycle, as of 2025-08 ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]).
- New MQLs within 90 days; roughly 40% of VP hires don't work out, so CEOs check at 60 or 90 days, as of 2024-08 ([[If Your VP Hasnt Done Some Key Part of the Job by Day 90 They Never Will|Jason Lemkin]]).
- Quick wins: about 80% confidence; big bet around day 30, as of 2025-07 ([[Here Is How I Approach Starting a New Job - Elena Verna|Elena Verna]]).
- Operational leaks fixed in 30–60 days, as of 2025-11 ([[The B2B CMO Imperative 2026 - Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan|Jon Miller]]).
- Quick wins doable within 60 days; first quick win around day 25 (date unknown) ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]).
- The new-leaders study behind the quick-wins paradox surveyed more than 5,400 new leaders, as of 2009-01 ([[The Quick Wins Paradox|Mark Van Buren and Todd Safferstone]]).

## Where the sources disagree

- **Quick wins or early wins?** Quick wins early ([[What to Do in Your First 30 Days as a Marketing Leader - Dave Gerhardt|Dave Gerhardt]]; [[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]) against substantive, collective early wins ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]; [[The Quick Wins Paradox|Mark Van Buren and Todd Safferstone]]). *Default:* collective wins that pass the quick-win test. **Decided by:** whether the CEO is already asking "what are you doing?".
- **Commit early or hold off?** Week 1 ([[How Do I Know If My New VP of Marketing Is Going To Make It|Jason Lemkin]]) against validating the inherited number first ([[The First 90 Days Just Got Faster - O'Neill, Gordon and McCarthy on Renegade Marketing|Scott Gordon]]; [[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]). *Default:* commit early to a number you control; reset the company number by day 90. **Decided by:** whether marketing controls the number or inherited it.

Full record in [[first-90-days#Where the sources disagree|the hub]], splits 2 and 3.

## Failure modes

- Wins that don't matter to the boss, or that import the last company's idea of a win ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]).
- Visible wins that create nine months of clean-up; changing martech or rebranding inside 90 days ([[The First 90 Days as CMO What I Actually Did - Dom O'Brien|Dom O'Brien]]).
- Becoming a "strategy person" who doesn't generate pipeline ([[The 1 Clear Sign a VP Will Fail|Jason Lemkin]]).
- Signing up for a number you can't beat ([[The Top 5 Reasons CMOs Get Fired|Carilu Dietrich]]; [[Gartner - Only 45 Percent of CMOs Surpass Senior Executive Expectations|Gartner]]).
- Overpromising early wins before you know the context ([[Here Is How I Approach Starting a New Job - Elena Verna|Elena Verna]]).
