# First 90 days: the readout and the CEO

## What this part is

Setting expectations with the CEO, managing up through the quarter, pre-wiring the plan, the day-90 readout, the first board appearance, and what happens after day 90. It expands steps 4, 9 and 17–20 of [[first-90-days|the hub]]. Ongoing board practice belongs to [[board-meetings]]; the CRO relationship to [[sales-and-marketing]].

## The idea underneath

**Most CMO failures are expectation failures, and most expectation failures are design failures.** When responsibilities, expectations and measures don't line up, the role sets any occupant up to fail; companies rarely say which kind of CMO they're hiring, and expectations routinely exceed authority ([[Why CMOs Never Last|Kimberly Whitler and Neil Morgan]]). Few CEOs and CFOs say their CMO has made marketing's accountabilities clear, so the fix is to agree in writing what marketing owns and is measured on ([[Gartner - Only 45 Percent of CMOs Surpass Senior Executive Expectations|Gartner]]). Most early failures come from expectations neither side said out loud ([[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]).

**The CEO has already been the marketer.** By the time a company hires a marketing leader, the CEO has a tested view of what works; the number one failure is substituting your own scaling vision for it. Diverging is a bet you have to win, and in marketing being fully right is rare ([[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail|Jason Lemkin]]). Outsiders also start behind on founder trust and on product depth, and one off-brand piece of work can break that trust ([[Building Wiz - Raaz Herzberg on Lennys Podcast|Raaz Herzberg]]).

**Trust buys leeway.** A CMO with strained C-suite relationships gets no time to turn lagging numbers around; one with trust and visible change often does ([[The Top 5 Reasons CMOs Get Fired|Carilu Dietrich]]). Credibility, not strategy, is the CMO's real problem: the CMOs who keep their seat treat the C-suite as their first team, learn each peer's P&L problems rather than teaching them marketing, and surface misses before anyone asks ([[The B2B CMO Imperative 2026 - Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan|Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan]]).

**The readout should confirm, not reveal.** If the CEO has had a weekly note and the CRO and CFO have already agreed the numbers, the readout is a decision, not a debate. Asks made piecemeal get lost; one end-to-end ask lands ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]). Arriving as one voice with sales keeps pipeline disputes out of the room ([[Traits of a High-Performing CMO - Dave Kellogg on Exit Five|Dave Kellogg]]; [[CMO Board Meeting Success|Carilu Dietrich]]).

**The first board sets a format you'll live with.** Changing how you talk about marketing each time signals you don't have a handle on it; board members mostly don't understand marketing, but they understand how companies grow ([[The 3C Framework for CMO Board Meetings - Jaleh Rezaei|Jaleh Rezaei]]).

## The method

### The five CEO conversations

1. **Hold Watkins's five conversations, in order:** (1) the situation; (2) expectations: what success is, by when and how it's measured, resetting unrealistic ones early rather than absorbing them; (3) style: channel, frequency, and which decisions they want a say in; (4) resources, once you know more; (5) your development, once there's trust. Under-promise ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]). Cover what the CEO counts as marketing's number and who owns pipeline.
2. **Name the role type early** (strategist, commercialiser or P&L leader) and agree the measures in writing, especially if the job description says "drive growth" but you only own communications ([[Why CMOs Never Last|Kimberly Whitler and Neil Morgan]]).
3. **Find the CEO's channel opinions explicitly,** build on what works and align with the CEO's vision ([[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail|Jason Lemkin]]).
4. **If the CEO was the original marketer,** start from the founder's evidence on what works ([[Before You Start as a VP Do a Real 60-Day Plan|Jason Lemkin]]) and ask for founder time: about 8 hours over the first quarter (3 hours in the first two weeks, then 45 minutes a week), the first five customer calls, and one agreed marketing-owned number ([[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Alexis Chevallier, recorded in the Pecka note]]).
5. **Name what you need from the CEO:** access from day one, honesty about what's broken, what was tried and what's untouchable, a real budget conversation by day 30, and patience ([[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]).
6. **If you're an outsider in a technical market,** invest explicitly in founder trust and product depth ([[Building Wiz - Raaz Herzberg on Lennys Podcast|Raaz Herzberg]], at 34:01).

### Managing up week to week

1. **Send a Friday note** every week: what I learned, who I met, early thoughts ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Bernd Leger]], at 14:26). Show the CEO a written 90-day plan early; one CMO did it on day 3 and used it to steer what she was pulled into ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Isabelle Papoulias]], at 23:17).
2. **Recommend rather than ask, lead with the punchline, and run the trust loop:** say what you'll do, do it, say you're doing it, say you did it ([[15 principles for managing up|Wes Kao]]).
3. **In CEO meetings, look forward:** about a fifth on the past and the rest on what's next, where you're blocked and the risks ([[How to present to your CEO|Wes Kao]]).
4. **Keep checking in with the CEO, CRO, CFO and CPO:** ask whether you're communicating enough and at the right level ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]).
5. **When performance comes up, be the dispassionate analyst:** put the facts on the table without blame, then ask whether it's a problem and how big a priority. Approach the CEO as one voice with the sales leader; when in doubt, serve sales; and lead a product-background CEO to understand marketing ([[Traits of a High-Performing CMO - Dave Kellogg on Exit Five|Dave Kellogg]], at 06:42).
6. **When you're challenged, stop talking and get curious about why.** You can't be defensive if you're not talking ([[How to Be the CMO Everyone Wants to Work With - Dave Kellogg on Exit Five|Dave Kellogg]], at 15:40).
7. **Win peers by learning their P&L problems,** and send the CEO and CFO a monthly risk register with at least one metric in the red ([[The B2B CMO Imperative 2026 - Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan|Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan]]).
8. **If the "marketing problem" is really a strategy problem,** take it to the C-suite (Carilu Dietrich; no library note linked).

### Pre-wiring and the readout

1. **Pre-align every number with the CRO,** then the CFO and your GTM peers, so you can tell the CEO that sales, CS and finance already support it ([[CMO Board Meeting Success|Carilu Dietrich]]).
2. **Build the readout in a simple shape:** where we are, what I've learned, where we're going (Kimberley Kasper; no library note).
3. **Make one big ask, around day 90, as an end-to-end assessment:** mission, operating principles, the plan, budget, team changes and the explicit asks ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]).
4. **Commit only to a number you can beat** ([[Gartner - Only 45 Percent of CMOs Surpass Senior Executive Expectations|Gartner]]).
5. **For a fractional or interim engagement,** keep forecast, budget and plan as three separate documents: where past performance projects, what cash allows, and backing into the target ([[Diary of My First 90 Days as a Fractional CMO - Asia Orangio|Asia Orangio]]).

### The first board

1. **If the first board meeting arrives before your plan is ready** (it usually does), share a high-level direction: the reasons you joined and the risks you saw ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]).
2. **Answer the board's three questions:** strategic (market dynamics, growth levers), present (did we hit pipeline and revenue, and why) and future (will we hit next quarter and the year). Pre-align every number with the CRO, and be explicit about the ask: FYI, advice, a decision or money ([[CMO Board Meeting Success|Carilu Dietrich]]).
3. **Build the deck from a blank page,** go straight to the hot issue and the fix, design slides for discussion, and answer the question asked. Then book one-to-ones with the relevant directors ([[Six Tips on Presenting to the Board of Directors|Dave Kellogg]]).
4. **Set a format you'll reuse every quarter:** about five slides, three standing objectives (pipeline, awareness, conversion) each with metrics, a highlights-and-lowlights slide, and an ask for help ([[The 3C Framework for CMO Board Meetings - Jaleh Rezaei|Jaleh Rezaei]]).
5. **Red-team the deck:** part of the exec team plays the board. Get feedback at outline stage and on two drafts, adding the CFO or product head for the second ([[Not Failing Board Meetings as a CMO - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]).
6. **For the board's budget questions,** see [[Board-Level Questions On The Marketing Budget|Kellogg]] and [[annual-planning--ceo-and-board]].

### Days 90–180

1. **Reallocate the budget around day 80, baseline marketing's contribution at day 90, and hold a retrospective at day 180** ([[The CMO180 Onboarding Model - Kevin Briody|Kevin Briody]]).
2. **Then "get executors executing":** one hub as the source of truth for marketing work and a weekly team meeting, before deeper cross-functional work ([[Diary of My First 90 Days as a Fractional CMO - Asia Orangio|Asia Orangio]]).

## By stage

- **At ~$10M ARR** the CEO is usually a founder with firm channel views, and the conversations are mostly with them ([[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail|Jason Lemkin]]).
- **Around $20–30M ARR** the role becomes a company officer who drives business strategy with the C-suite, so expect the readout to be judged on strategy, not just marketing ([[What Kind of CMO Should You Hire - Carilu Dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]]).
- **Approaching IPO** the company reshapes its board and executive team to look like a public company, and the readout and board format must hold up for that audience (inferred from [[What Kind of CMO Should You Hire - Carilu Dietrich|Carilu Dietrich]] and [[CMO Success Stage by Stage - Kimberly Whitler and Jonathan Metrick|Whitler and Metrick]]).

## Templates

**The five CEO conversations, in order** ([[The First 90 Days - Michael Watkins|Michael Watkins]]): situation · expectations · style · resources · your development.

**The Friday note** ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Bernd Leger]]; [[15 principles for managing up|Wes Kao]]):
> **Week N.** *What I learned:* … *Who I met:* … *Early thoughts:* each marked with how sure I am.

**The recommendation** ([[15 principles for managing up|Wes Kao]]): "I recommend X. Here's why. Risk is Y, mitigated by Z. If you agree, I'll start with [step]."

**What I need from you** ([[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]): access to sales targets, pipeline data, customers and calls from day one · what's broken, what was tried and what's untouchable · a budget conversation by day 30 · patience.

**The day-90 readout outline** (contents: [[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]; shape: Kimberley Kasper):
1. Where we are.
2. What I've learned.
3. Where we're going: mission, operating principles and the plan.
4. Budget.
5. Team changes.
6. The explicit asks, all in one place.

**The quarterly board format** ([[The 3C Framework for CMO Board Meetings - Jaleh Rezaei|Jaleh Rezaei]]; [[CMO Board Meeting Success|Carilu Dietrich]]): about five slides · pipeline, awareness and conversion objectives, each with metrics · highlights and lowlights · an ask for help · every number pre-aligned with the CRO.

**The red-team schedule** ([[Not Failing Board Meetings as a CMO - Kyle Lacy|Kyle Lacy]]): outline → CEO feedback on direction → skeleton draft → red team with the CEO, sales leader and a co-founder → second draft → fresh red team adding the CFO or product head.

## Numbers and tactics ⏳

- 22% of CEOs and CFOs have significant clarity on marketing's accountabilities; 69% name failing to deliver promised results as the top removal trigger, as of 2025-02 ([[Gartner - Only 45 Percent of CMOs Surpass Senior Executive Expectations|Gartner]]).
- Of 170 CMO job descriptions, only 22% said how the CMO would be measured, as of 2017-07 ([[Why CMOs Never Last|Kimberly Whitler and Neil Morgan]]).
- Founder time: about 8 hours over the first quarter (3 hours in the first two weeks, 45 minutes weekly), the first five customer calls and one agreed number, as of 2026-10 ([[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Alexis Chevallier, recorded in the Pecka note]]). The weekly figure over a full quarter adds to more than 8 hours; check the original post before quoting a total.
- A real budget conversation by day 30, as of 2026-10 ([[The 90-Day Contract - What B2B Marketing Leaders and CEOs Owe Each Other|Kristen Pecka]]).
- About a fifth of a CEO meeting on the past, the rest ahead, as of 2024-01 ([[How to present to your CEO|Wes Kao]]).
- Monthly risk register with at least one metric in the red, as of 2026-04 ([[The B2B CMO Imperative 2026 - Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan|Jon Miller and Sydney Sloan]]).
- Board prep runs from 4–8 weeks out; stop editing and send the pre-read 2 days to 1 week before, as of 2024-06 ([[CMO Board Meeting Success|Carilu Dietrich]]).
- About five board slides, three standing objectives, as of 2022-08 ([[The 3C Framework for CMO Board Meetings - Jaleh Rezaei|Jaleh Rezaei]]).

## Where the sources disagree

**Follow the CEO, or lead them?** Build on the CEO's view ([[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail|Jason Lemkin]]); serve sales and educate a product-background CEO ([[Traits of a High-Performing CMO - Dave Kellogg on Exit Five|Dave Kellogg]]); take a strategy problem disguised as a marketing problem to the C-suite (Carilu Dietrich). All three agree you align first; they differ on how hard to lead. *Default:* build on the CEO's view and switch when the evidence says so. **Decided by:** whether the CEO's view of what works is backed by current evidence. Full record in [[first-90-days#Where the sources disagree|the hub]], split 5.

## Failure modes

- No written measures, so the expectations are bigger than your authority ([[Why CMOs Never Last|Kimberly Whitler and Neil Morgan]]).
- A weak link with the CEO, CRO, CFO or CPO ([[Mistakes New CMOs Make in Their First 3-4 Months|Carilu Dietrich]]).
- Choking in board meetings, or being the CRO's scapegoat ([[How to Be the CMO Everyone Wants to Work With - Dave Kellogg on Exit Five|Dave Kellogg]]).
- Asks spread across many meetings, so none land ([[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing|Jamie Gier, Bernd Leger and Isabelle Papoulias]]).
- A contrarian plan that overrides the CEO's view ([[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail|Jason Lemkin]]).
- The "CMO hit-and-run": arrive, declare everything broken, rebrand the unmeasurable, leave before it's measured (Kieran Flanagan, satirical post, 2024-12; no library note).
