# Positioning

**The job:** decide how the market should understand the product: what it beats, why it's better, who it's for and what it is. Then turn that into a pitch and messaging the whole company uses.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pull this play when** | Prospects on first calls ask you to start again or compare you to the wrong product; the whole pipeline drags; or the product, competitors or market have changed ([[How to Nail Your Product Positioning - April Dunford on Lenny's Podcast\|April Dunford]]). Also before a launch ([[Positioning Prep Part 1 - Decisions to Make Before a Positioning Exercise\|April Dunford]]) |
| **Time** | Prep, then about a week of working sessions ([[How to Nail Your Product Positioning - April Dunford on Lenny's Podcast\|April Dunford]]). A new pitch is live in two weeks at best, about a month typically (as of 2023-10) ([[A Step-by-Step Guide to Crafting a Sales Pitch That Wins - April Dunford on Lenny's Podcast\|April Dunford]]). Revisit twice a year ([[How to Manage a Big Positioning Change - April Dunford\|April Dunford]]) |
| **Owner and people** | The CEO drives it ([[How to Manage a Big Positioning Change - April Dunford\|April Dunford]]); marketing stewards it afterwards ([[A Step-by-Step Guide to Crafting a Sales Pitch That Wins - April Dunford on Lenny's Podcast\|April Dunford]]). **In the room:** about 12, at most 15: marketing, several sales voices, product, customer success, the CEO and any exec with strong views ([[Preparing for a Positioning Exercise - April Dunford\|April Dunford]]) |
| **You walk out with** | 1) A positioning canvas. 2) A category choice and style. 3) A storyboarded sales pitch. 4) A messaging document. 5) A test and rollout plan |
| **Done when** | Pilot reps refuse the old pitch, and sales leadership has rolled out the new one ([[Post Positioning - Testing and Execution - April Dunford\|April Dunford]]) |

## Before you start

Settle these before anyone meets, or you'll stop mid-workshop and redo work ([[Positioning Prep Part 1 - Decisions to Make Before a Positioning Exercise|April Dunford]]).

1. **Decide whether now is the time.** If the team still tries a new category every week, don't run the play yet. Finish experimenting first ([[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try|Anthony Pierri]]).
2. **Position for customers only,** not investors or employees ([[Positioning Prep Part 1 - Decisions to Make Before a Positioning Exercise|April Dunford]]).
3. **Decide what you're positioning:** product, company or suite, from the go-to-market motion ([[Positioning Prep Part 1 - Decisions to Make Before a Positioning Exercise|April Dunford]]).
4. **Name the champion:** the person who builds the shortlist and recommends to the economic buyer ([[Positioning Prep Part 1 - Decisions to Make Before a Positioning Exercise|April Dunford]]).
5. **Gather buyer evidence, not CRM fields,** which one vendor found wrong 85% of the time (as of 2023-10). Interview 8–12 recent wins and losses, using a neutral interviewer ([[State of Win-Loss Analysis 2023 - Pragmatic Institute and Clozd|Pragmatic Institute and Clozd]]). Ask what changed in their world; keep their words ([[Strategic Narrative - Andy Raskin on FounderCoHo|Andy Raskin]]; [[Mastering Messaging Copy and Clarity - Emma Stratton on Exit Five|Emma Stratton]]). Add call recordings and a demo ([[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try|Anthony Pierri]]).
6. **Agree bad-fit customers and vocabulary** (alternative, value, category) up front ([[Preparing for a Positioning Exercise - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).
7. **Pick the facilitator.** *Default:* in-house. *If* execs can't agree or a big bet rides on it, hire an outsider ([[How to Nail Your Product Positioning - April Dunford on Lenny's Podcast|April Dunford]]).

## The play

The core sequence is April Dunford's: five components in a fixed order, each built on the one before ([[A Quickstart Guide to Positioning - April Dunford|April Dunford]]). Each step's forcing question is in bold.

### Phase 1 · Week 1: the working sessions

1. **Competitive alternatives. "What would a prospect do if we didn't exist?"** Include doing nothing: B2B vendors typically lose about half their opportunities to the status quo (as of 2026-03). Use sales' list, not product's ([[A Guide to Advanced B2B Positioning - April Dunford on Lenny's Newsletter|April Dunford]]).
2. **Differentiated capabilities. "What can we do that those alternatives can't?"** Only what ships today, against today's shortlist ([[A Guide to Advanced B2B Positioning - April Dunford on Lenny's Newsletter|April Dunford]]).
3. **Differentiated value. "So what?"** Ask it of each capability until you reach making money, saving money or reducing risk. Keep a few themes, not ten ([[A Guide to Advanced B2B Positioning - April Dunford on Lenny's Newsletter|April Dunford]]).
   - *If a "value" is support, integrations or SOC 2,* ask "would anyone choose us for that alone?" If not, move it to objections.
   - *Exception:* buyers are fleeing a vendor that failed on exactly that ([[Value vs Objection Handling - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).
4. **Best-fit customers. "Who cares most about that value?"** Define them by account traits sales can target ([[Understanding the Job of a Market Category - April Dunford|April Dunford]]). *If* a firmographic segment hides several use cases, segment by use case and pick one ([[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try|Anthony Pierri]]).
5. **Market category:** last, in Phase 2. You can't judge a category before you know the value and who cares ([[Understanding the Job of a Market Category - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).
6. **Storyboard the pitch before you leave the room** ([[How to Nail Your Product Positioning - April Dunford on Lenny's Podcast|April Dunford]]).

**Output:** a filled-in canvas (*Templates*) and a pitch storyboard.
**Trap:** starting with the category or a fill-in-the-blanks positioning statement. Teams that picked the category first got results from "sorta bad" to "absolute disaster" ([[Understanding the Job of a Market Category - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).

**Pause point, end of Phase 1:**
- [ ] Do-nothing listed as an alternative
- [ ] A few value themes, each passing "so what?"
- [ ] Objection-handling items moved out of value
- [ ] Best-fit defined by traits sales can target

### Phase 2 · Final session: the category decision

1. **Hold the value and best-fit against the current category. "Does this frame point buyers at our value?"** If yes, don't touch it: do no harm ([[New Thinking on Market Categories - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).
2. **Pick one of Dunford's three styles:** head-to-head (win the existing category), big fish in a small pond (dominate an underserved sub-segment), or create a new game ([[New Thinking on Market Categories - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).
   - *Default:* big fish in a small pond. Most winners started as a niche and stretched the category later, Salesforce and Snowflake at $200–400M revenue (as of 2023-10) ([[A Quickstart Guide to Positioning - April Dunford|April Dunford]]; [[No New Categories - Greg Kogan|Greg Kogan]]).
   - *If* the current category already points at your value and you can win it as defined, go head-to-head ([[New Thinking on Market Categories - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).
   - *If* a young category is forming around you, join it and shape it ([[No New Categories - Greg Kogan|Greg Kogan]]; [[Why I Believe in Categories, Just Not Category Design - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).
   - *If* the founder wants a new category and sales wants an existing one, use a bridge: a known noun plus a point-of-view modifier, like "collaborative design" ([[The Greatest Debate In Positioning - Chris Guest|Chris Guest]]).
   - *Create a new category only if all four hold:* the product itself is category-creating ([[New Thinking on Market Categories - April Dunford|April Dunford]]); no existing category explains your value, true for about 10% of Dunford's clients (as of 2024-01) ([[Understanding the Job of a Market Category - April Dunford|April Dunford]]); you can fund two launches, product and category ([[No New Categories - Greg Kogan|Greg Kogan]]); and you have several million ARR and a Series A or more ([[The Greatest Debate In Positioning - Chris Guest|Chris Guest]]). Test it on one product or campaign first, keeping a foot in the old category ([[No New Categories - Greg Kogan|Greg Kogan]]).

**Output:** the category, the style, and one sentence on why.
**Trap:** an aspirational category the product can't support. Buyers see through it ([[New Thinking on Market Categories - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).

**Pause point, end of Phase 2:**
- [ ] Category chosen after value and best-fit
- [ ] Style and reason written down
- [ ] If "create": all four conditions checked and a test planned

### Phase 3 · Weeks 2–4: messaging and the pitch

1. **Write the messaging document first:** a tagline, three value points and the approved way to say each. Do it before the homepage, or every asset drifts ([[Positioning vs Messaging - April Dunford on Forget The Funnel|April Dunford]]).
2. **Build the pitch with sales, not for it** ([[How to Manage a Big Positioning Change - April Dunford|April Dunford]]). Use Dunford's structure ([[A Step-by-Step Guide to Crafting a Sales Pitch That Wins - April Dunford on Lenny's Podcast|April Dunford]]):
   - *Set-up:* the insight → the pluses and minuses of the alternatives → the "perfect world".
   - *Follow-through:* intro → each value theme with its capabilities → proof → objections → the ask.
3. **Write one pitch, for the champion,** and arm them for IT, procurement, security and legal ([[Why Positioning Fails to Make the Jump from Marketing to Sales - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).
4. **De-risk the decision; don't push.** Pressure lowers close rates with indecisive buyers ([[A Step-by-Step Guide to Crafting a Sales Pitch That Wins - April Dunford on Lenny's Podcast|April Dunford]]).
5. **Pick the core asset.** *Default (sales-led):* the deck ([[Strategic Narrative - Andy Raskin on FounderCoHo|Andy Raskin]]). *If buyers meet you through the website or product first:* the homepage, audience first, then hero → problem → solution → value propositions ([[The Definitive SaaS Homepage Framework|Anthony Pierri and Kyle Poyar]]; [[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try|Anthony Pierri]]). No source maps asset to motion; adaptation of the play.
6. **If people agreed in the room but now say different things,** rewrite the homepage hero together; it forces the disagreement out ([[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try|Anthony Pierri]]).

**Output:** messaging document, deck, talk track, draft homepage hero.
**Trap:** a pitch built by marketing or an agency alone, or one per persona. Sales won't use what it didn't help build ([[Why Positioning Fails to Make the Jump from Marketing to Sales - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).

**Pause point, end of Phase 3:**
- [ ] Messaging document before any homepage copy
- [ ] A sales leader co-wrote the pitch
- [ ] One pitch, aimed at the champion
- [ ] Objections in their own section

### Phase 4 · Weeks 3–8, then ongoing: test and roll out

1. **Don't A/B test the homepage to validate positioning.** It mixes messaging, design and CTA, on too little traffic ([[Post Positioning - Testing and Execution - April Dunford|April Dunford]]; [[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try|Anthony Pierri]]).
2. **Pilot the pitch with one or two of your best reps** on pre-qualified first calls with new best-fit prospects. Huddle after each call. It passes when the rep won't go back to the old pitch ([[Post Positioning - Testing and Execution - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).
3. **Never validate with your best customers.** They bought the old story ([[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try|Anthony Pierri]]).
4. **Test the messaging with about 15 target buyers who don't know you.** Ask open questions; stop when answers repeat. Don't substitute an internal review ([[Testing Messaging With 15 People - Peep Laja|Peep Laja]]).
5. **Have sales leadership roll it out,** with the pilot rep selling it to the rest of sales ([[How to Manage a Big Positioning Change - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).
6. **Make marketing the "positioning police"** and recertify reps quarterly ([[A Step-by-Step Guide to Crafting a Sales Pitch That Wins - April Dunford on Lenny's Podcast|April Dunford]]; [[Strategic Narrative - Andy Raskin on FounderCoHo|Andy Raskin]]).
7. **Measure use on recorded calls, not training attendance,** and compare win rates of users and non-users ([[How Do You Ensure Salespeople Use the Newest Deck - Udi Ledergor|Udi Ledergor]]).
8. **Hold the bet** for six months to two years ([[Strategic Narrative - Andy Raskin on FounderCoHo|Andy Raskin]]; [[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try|Anthony Pierri]]). Re-walk the steps when the product, competitors or market change ([[Shifting Positioning When AI Capabilities Are Rapidly Changing - April Dunford|April Dunford]]).

**Output:** pilot notes, the rollout plan and a twice-yearly review booked.
**Trap:** judging it on next month's conversion. Conversion can dip first, and launch to closed deal takes about 5–8 months (as of 2026-01) ([[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try|Anthony Pierri]]).

**Pause point, end of Phase 4:**
- [ ] Pilot used new prospects only
- [ ] Buyer test run outside the company
- [ ] Rollout led by sales leadership
- [ ] Next review date set

## Situations that change the play

| If… | then… |
|---|---|
| **Pre-launch** | Write a positioning *thesis*: keep it loose, see where the market pulls, then tighten ([[Positioning Prep Part 1 - Decisions to Make Before a Positioning Exercise\|April Dunford]]) |
| **Seed stage** | Aim for simple clarity and lock it. At scale in a crowded category, polish hard ([[Seed-Stage Positioning - Lock and Load - Dave Kellogg\|Dave Kellogg]]) |
| **Multi-product** | Position the lead product for new customers and the rest assuming it's owned, or position the suite as one offer ([[Positioning Prep Part 1 - Decisions to Make Before a Positioning Exercise\|April Dunford]]) |
| **PLG or website-led** | Make the homepage the core asset (adaptation of the play); its job varies by motion ([[Positioning vs Messaging - April Dunford on Forget The Funnel\|April Dunford]]) |
| **After an acquisition** | Run one joint exercise with both sides ([[How to Manage a Big Positioning Change - April Dunford\|April Dunford]]) |
| **A big feature launch** | Re-walk alternatives → capabilities → value. Reposition only for new value; otherwise change the explanation ([[Shifting Positioning When AI Capabilities Are Rapidly Changing - April Dunford\|April Dunford]]) |
| **When not to run it** | The team still tries a new category every week ([[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try\|Anthony Pierri]]) |

## Worked example (illustrative: fictional company)

**Tallyroom**: accounts-payable automation for wholesale distributors.

| Component | Tallyroom |
|---|---|
| Alternatives | Spreadsheets plus the ERP's AP module; a generic AP tool; do nothing |
| Capabilities | Reads distributor-format supplier invoices; matches them to partial deliveries automatically |
| Value themes | 1) Close the month days faster. 2) Stop paying for goods that never arrived |
| Best-fit | Distributors with many suppliers and frequent partial deliveries, keeping their ERP |
| Category and style | Big fish in a small pond: "AP automation for distributors" |
| Moved to objections | SOC 2, ERP integrations, support hours |
| Champion | The financial controller |

## Signs it's working, signs it's failing

- **Working within weeks:** pilot reps refuse the old pitch ([[Post Positioning - Testing and Execution - April Dunford|April Dunford]]); "start again" stops on first calls ([[How to Nail Your Product Positioning - April Dunford on Lenny's Podcast|April Dunford]]).
- **Working within months:** buyers describe you to peers in your words ([[State of Win-Loss Analysis 2023 - Pragmatic Institute and Clozd|Pragmatic Institute and Clozd]]); reps using the story win more ([[How Do You Ensure Salespeople Use the Newest Deck - Udi Ledergor|Udi Ledergor]]).
- **Aligned:** critiques shrink to wording ([[Strategic Narrative - Andy Raskin on FounderCoHo|Andy Raskin]]).
- **Failing:** ten value themes ([[A Guide to Advanced B2B Positioning - April Dunford on Lenny's Newsletter|April Dunford]]); the category picked first ([[Understanding the Job of a Market Category - April Dunford|April Dunford]]); a pitch per persona ([[Why Positioning Fails to Make the Jump from Marketing to Sales - April Dunford|April Dunford]]); reps back on the old deck ([[How Do You Ensure Salespeople Use the Newest Deck - Udi Ledergor|Udi Ledergor]]); relitigation before the review date ([[Seed-Stage Positioning - Lock and Load - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]).

## Why this play: the evidence

**Where the sources agree** (3+ independent sources, used to build the default route):
- The CEO drives it, with sales and product in the room ([[Preparing for a Positioning Exercise - April Dunford|April Dunford]]; [[Strategic Narrative - Andy Raskin on FounderCoHo|Andy Raskin]]; [[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try|Anthony Pierri]]).
- Build from buyers' words, not CRM fields ([[State of Win-Loss Analysis 2023 - Pragmatic Institute and Clozd|Pragmatic Institute and Clozd]]; [[Strategic Narrative - Andy Raskin on FounderCoHo|Andy Raskin]]; [[Mastering Messaging Copy and Clarity - Emma Stratton on Exit Five|Emma Stratton]]).
- Test with new prospects and outside buyers ([[Post Positioning - Testing and Execution - April Dunford|April Dunford]]; [[Testing Messaging With 15 People - Peep Laja|Peep Laja]]; [[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try|Anthony Pierri]]).
- Work inside an existing category by default ([[Understanding the Job of a Market Category - April Dunford|April Dunford]]; [[Why I Believe in Categories, Just Not Category Design - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]; [[No New Categories - Greg Kogan|Greg Kogan]]; [[Pioneer Advantage - Marketing Logic or Marketing Legend - Golder and Tellis|Golder and Tellis]]).
- Decide, then hold the bet ([[Seed-Stage Positioning - Lock and Load - Dave Kellogg|Dave Kellogg]]; [[Strategic Narrative - Andy Raskin on FounderCoHo|Andy Raskin]]; [[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try|Anthony Pierri]]).
- Reinforce after launch, judged on calls ([[A Step-by-Step Guide to Crafting a Sales Pitch That Wins - April Dunford on Lenny's Podcast|April Dunford]]; [[Strategic Narrative - Andy Raskin on FounderCoHo|Andy Raskin]]; [[How Do You Ensure Salespeople Use the Newest Deck - Udi Ledergor|Udi Ledergor]]).

**Where they split, and the default this play takes:**
- **Borrow a category or design one?** Play Bigger claims category kings take 76% of a category's market cap, defined after the fact ([[Play Bigger - Category Design|Play Bigger]]). Golder and Tellis found 47% of market pioneers failed (as of 1993-05, not software) ([[Pioneer Advantage - Marketing Logic or Marketing Legend - Golder and Tellis|Golder and Tellis]]). *Default:* an existing category or sub-segment. *Tie-breaker:* create one only when all four Phase 2 conditions hold; use a bridge when they half-hold.
- **Deck or homepage as the core asset?** Raskin says the deck; Pierri says the homepage forces alignment ([[Strategic Narrative - Andy Raskin on FounderCoHo|Andy Raskin]]; [[Positioning Is a Decision Not a Tagline - Anthony Pierri on Love at First Try|Anthony Pierri]]). *Default* (adaptation of the play): by motion, as in Phase 3.
- **Open with a market shift?** Raskin does; Dunford says "old way vs new way" carries no differentiated value ([[Strategic Narrative - Andy Raskin on FounderCoHo|Andy Raskin]]; [[A Step-by-Step Guide to Crafting a Sales Pitch That Wins - April Dunford on Lenny's Podcast|April Dunford]]). *Default:* Dunford's structure. *Tie-breaker* (no source covers this; adaptation of the play): keep the opening pilot reps won't give up.
- **Can you test positioning?** Not by A/B test, say Dunford and Pierri; Laja tests messaging with buyer panels ([[Testing Messaging With 15 People - Peep Laja|Peep Laja]]). *Default:* test the bet as a live pitch, the wording with a panel.
