> [!summary] Principles
> - Demandgen budget is a ratio-driven support cost of every seller, just like SCs, SDRs and managers. Adding a seller without adding the demandgen to feed them breaks the model.
> - "The baby robin problem": sales hires above plan, the pipeline doesn't grow with it, and every rep ends up starved and waiting for opportunities.
> - Adding sellers is a go-to-market decision, not a sales decision. Plan headcount and pipeline budget together, not in separate silos.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳
> - Worked steady-state example: per-AE quarterly quota × target coverage ÷ pipe/spend ratio = demandgen spend per AE. A 15:1 pipe/spend ratio is called "healthy" (as of 2025-02)
> - In his example, the per-AE demandgen cost comes out far larger than the fractional SDR and manager costs that plans routinely include (as of 2025-02)
> - Typical support ratios cited per AE: fractions of an SC, SDR and sales manager, plus smaller fractions of alliances, sales ops and enablement (as of 2025-02)

## Notes
- Short post. It turns "marketing needs more budget" into a mechanical consequence of the sales capacity plan, which is far easier to defend than a marketing ask.
- The silo is the cause. Sales asks for the seller, marketing's budget is "someone else's problem", and after a few repeats it becomes everyone's problem.
- Ignores ramp and timing for simplicity. A real plan has to phase demandgen ahead of seller start dates.
- Works when you know your pipe/spend ratio and the coverage you need. Fails when marketing isn't the main pipeline source, so a per-AE uplift should only cover marketing's agreed share.
- Best used in budget negotiation. Whenever an extra AE is proposed mid-plan, attach its demandgen cost to the request.
