> [!summary] Principles
> - **The CFO's view of the marketing budget:** sales capacity is built bottom-up from reps and quota; marketing targets are built in reverse from the pipeline the sales plan needs, then sanity-checked top-down.
> - **Work back to quota, not to the financial plan.** If sales is over-assigned, marketing's pipeline target must cover the quota on the street, or reps come up short by design. Then subtract the pipeline sales, partners and the product (PLG) will source themselves.
> - **Time-shift pipeline by segment.** Pipeline has to land one sales cycle before the quota it supports, so next year's Q1 enterprise pipeline is built in this year's H2. Plan about 18 months out, on a rolling basis.
> - **Budget holders and one GL pick list.** Each marketing sub-team (demand gen, PMM, events, ops) owns a mini-budget, and every team codes spend to the same mutually exclusive list of expense types. Big multi-team events get their own line.
> - **Run the budgeted CAC payback before the year starts.** Most companies check payback after each close, never on the plan itself, and the plan rarely matches the payback the board was promised.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2023-08
> - CJ Gustafson (startup CFO, writer of Mostly Metrics) puts pipeline coverage at roughly 200–350% of the new-ARR target for most SaaS companies, and under 2x for very efficient high-velocity SMB motions. Useful for checking the pipeline target finance will expect from marketing.
> - CJ Gustafson says SaaS sales quota is typically over-assigned 20–30% above the financial plan, and that sales typically sources at least 20% of its own pipeline. Marketing's pipeline target should cover the over-assigned quota, minus what sales, partners and the product source.
> - CJ Gustafson's pipeline lead times by segment: SMB pipeline must land 0 quarters before the quota it supports, mid-market 1 quarter and enterprise 2 quarters.
> - On the split of marketing spend between people and programmes, CJ Gustafson says young teams skew to people and mature teams to programmes, but the split should rarely pass 60/40 either way.
> - In the companion Part 5 post, CJ Gustafson suggests a 10% contractor placeholder in the first half of the year for unplanned projects, and keeping first-half spend light to avoid second-half cuts (as of 2023-09).

## Notes
- Gustafson is a startup CFO who has run FP&A at multi-billion-dollar tech companies. This is the finance side of the table a CMO negotiates with, which makes it unusually useful for anticipating CFO questions.
- Common mistakes he lists: a people/programme split where there aren't enough people to spend the programme dollars; ramping pipeline quarter on quarter without the spend to support it; over-allocating pipeline to partners you don't fund; leaning on one source until its CAC spikes mid-year.
- Expansion ARR is excluded from the marketing reverse funnel in his model; sales and CS own it.
- Related posts in the same series: Part 1 kickoff ([link](https://www.mostlymetrics.com/p/kicking-off-the-annual-planning-season), 2023-08: CEO+CFO set revenue, productivity and burn guardrails, then finance gives each leader a headcount envelope per quarter); Part 2 top-down target setting ([link](https://www.mostlymetrics.com/p/your-definitive-guide-to-annual-planning-e98), 2024-08: a CEO deck of required decisions, what worked, headwinds, priorities, guardrails); the 2024 kickoff ([link](https://www.mostlymetrics.com/p/your-definitive-guide-to-annual-planning), 2024-08) argues for plan approval at the Q3 board (around Thanksgiving) on a 15-month model, not in February. His staffing-mix post ([link](https://www.mostlymetrics.com/p/i-need-more-people-all-execs-ever), 2022-04) gives a rule of thumb of about 1/4 marketing to 3/4 sales in S&M headcount, and about 1/3 marketing to 2/3 sales in S&M dollars.
