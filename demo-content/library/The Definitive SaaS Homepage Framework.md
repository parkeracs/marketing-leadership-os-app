> [!summary] Principles
> - **The homepage is a product-marketing asset.** Its job is to answer what the product does, how it works, what it looks like, when you'd use it and how it helps. Until most of your market can answer those, resist executive pressure for vague, visionary copy.
> - **Choose the audience first,** across three dimensions: company type, persona/department, and situation (the job they're expending energy on). Vertical audiences anchor on a department or company type; horizontal products anchor on a shared situation. Prioritise by where growth should come from in 6–12 months, what your GTM can actually reach, and whether you're creating or capturing demand.
> - **Minimum viable positioning = segment + differentiation, against a reference point.** Competitive positioning names a universally understood alternative (Slack vs email). Contextual positioning places you in a workflow ("use us when you're doing X"). "Manual work" isn't a competitive anchor because every buyer pictures something different.
> - **Value propositions are built from six elements:** use case, current tool, problem, capability, feature, benefit. Fletch leads with capabilities (what the user can now do), backed by the feature that makes it possible, rather than with abstract benefits.
> - **Default page flow mirrors a sales call:** hero (what, who, use case, plus recognisable logos) → problem → solution intro → value propositions at feature-set level → optional proof, testimonials and persona sections.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-04
> - Anthony Pierri (of the positioning consultancy Fletch) claims about 80% of early-stage startups would benefit from his exact homepage structure (as of 2024-03, in the earlier version on Aakash Gupta's newsletter). This is his claim, not measured data.
> - Pierri and Kyle Poyar cite Loom, Notion and Rippling as large companies whose homepages still do product marketing (explaining what the product does) rather than vague, visionary copy.

## Notes
- Growth Unhinged is partly paywalled; this note holds synthesis and a link only. An earlier, fuller free version by Pierri is "The Ultimate Guide to Homepages" on Aakash Gupta's newsletter (2024-03-15): https://www.news.aakashg.com/p/the-ultimate-guide-to-homepages
- Fletch's four stages of a webpage: product marketing (what you say, why, to whom) → copywriting (clarity over cleverness) → design → CRO. Most teams skip stage one.
- **Disagreement:** Fletch leads with capabilities and features; Emma Stratton ([[The VBF Rule - Emma Stratton]]) leads with the value proposition and puts features last. Peep Laja's test data sides with "what before why" on the homepage ([[Testing Messaging With 15 People - Peep Laja]]).
- Pierri's view that positioning only needs segment + differentiation is deliberately simpler than April Dunford's five components (canon).
