> [!summary] Principles
> - **Category creation means launching two products at once:** the product and the category. If you can't fund two launches, you can't fund a product plus a category. The hard part isn't the strategy, it's moving buyers and their purchasing behaviour to the new label.
> - **Existing categories work like a trade wind.** Buyers already understand the problem, have budget, know the alternatives and know where to look. You spend on selling the product, not on explaining the shelf.
> - **Split existing categories into established (over ~5 years old, broad awareness) and emerging (under ~5 years, growing awareness).** Disrupting an established category has high present potential. Joining an emerging one lets you shape buying criteria and own the education before big players arrive, at far lower cost than inventing a category alone.
> - **Categories are words the market adopts, usually documented by analysts or publications after the shift happens.** Vendors influence them; they rarely coin the winning term. "Let your impact change the category, not your marketing strategy."
> - **Test a category before committing:** put one product, or one campaign, into the new category while keeping a foot in the old one (Netlify kept "static site hosting" running throughout its Jamstack push).

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2020-08
> - Greg Kogan (B2B marketing consultant to technical startups) found that of 25 B2B software IPOs in 2018–2019, 22 (88%) marketed themselves in existing categories the year before IPO. The exceptions were Fastly (Edge Cloud Platform), PagerDuty (Digital Operations Management) and Anaplan (Connected Planning); the sample is small, so treat it as indicative.
> - Kogan notes that Fastly had already raised $179M at a $650M valuation before switching from "CDN" to its own category, and that PagerDuty tried two other category labels first.
> - Teleport, a Kogan client, repositioned from "modern SSH" into the established Privileged Access Management category; Kogan reports revenue rose more than 600% within six months of the new messaging (claimed by the consultant).
> - Kogan's examples of category-creation attempts that were reversed: Drift abandoned "Conversational Marketing" for "Revenue Acceleration Platform", and D2iQ retreated from "Day 2 Operations" to "Kubernetes platform" within a year of renaming the company.

## Notes
- The strongest evidence-based rebuttal to *Play Bigger*. Counters the "No. 2 gets scraps" claim with Lyft, Pepsi and the marketing-platform market (HubSpot, Marketo, Mailchimp all large).
- Agrees with [[April Dunford]] that most winners start inside an existing or emerging category. Adds a useful middle option, *join an emerging category*, that maps to Dunford's sub-segment and reframe styles.
- Domino Data Lab story: rode the emerging "data science platform" category and focused on the verticals where demand was growing fastest (insurance first), rather than pushing the category itself.
- Sample is small (two years of IPOs) and survivor-only in the other direction, so treat 88% as indicative, not a law.
