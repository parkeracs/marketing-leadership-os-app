> [!summary] Principles
> - **Three planning imperatives for the CMO:** get your own reporting in order, calculate your CAC ratios, and get *into* the finance model to set its assumptions.
> - **Use CAC ratios to size the budget, not % of revenue.** A CAC ratio gives a direct GTM budget (new ARR × CAC ratio) from your own reality; % of revenue benchmarks invite "yes, but we're different" from the CEO.
> - **Drill into marketing's own CAC components,** or the ask looks fluffy next to sales.
> - **Base the starting plan on recent rates, not averages.** Q1 won't magically beat Q4. Separate new and expansion close rates rather than blending them. Model seasonality and let assumptions step up through the year if you want budget relief, not from day one.
> - **Benchmarks mislead** (size, ASP, motion, region, growth all differ). Agree how you'll measure, get your own metrics right and move them.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2024-09
> - Carilu Dietrich (hypergrowth marketing leader turned advisor) shows how a CAC ratio sizes the go-to-market budget directly: a SaaS company targeting $25M of new ARR at a CAC ratio of 1.5 needs a $37.5M sales and marketing budget.
> - Ray Rike (Benchmarkit, a SaaS benchmarking firm), presenting at Carilu Dietrich's SaaStr CMO brunch, tracks blended (new plus expansion) and new-only CAC ratios over 5- or 9-quarter windows; Dietrich would also run the last 2 quarters because conversion rates are volatile. The session noted that bigger deals cost more to win, especially in the $50–100K "messy middle".
