> [!summary] Principles
> - **Diagnose the situation before choosing the strategy (STARS).** Start-up, Turnaround, Accelerated growth, Realignment, Sustaining success. Each has its own urgency, challenges and openings. Turnarounds need fast, tough calls; realignments and sustaining-success situations give you time but constrain what you can change. In a realignment you reinvent the business; in sustaining success you have to invent the challenge.
> - **Most new roles are a portfolio of STARS situations.** For a marketing leader: demand gen may be a turnaround while brand is sustaining success and a new segment is a start-up. Diagnose per area, not just per company.
> - **Plan five conversations with the boss:** situational diagnosis, expectations, style, resources, personal development. Sequence matters: diagnosis, expectations and style first; resources once you've learned more; development last. Under-promise and over-deliver on expectations.
> - **Secure early wins that serve the long game.** Pick a few focused bets that build credibility and lay foundations, done in a way that fits the culture (unless changing the culture is the point). What counts as a win differs by STARS type.
> - **Balance learning-and-connecting against deciding-and-acting.** Both failure modes are real: acting too early to prove the hiring decision, or waiting so long people ask what you're doing. Expect to decide with 80–90% of the information.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2003-11
> - Michael Watkins (author of The First 90 Days) gives a rule of thumb for new leaders: by day 90, be clear on the major team changes you intend, and get there faster in a turnaround (as of 2020-08, MIT Sloan alumni webinar).
> - Watkins suggests negotiating the learning period before you start, for example agreeing with your new boss on a 45-day assessment and plan readout (as of 2020-08, MIT Sloan alumni webinar).
> - Watkins says he never claimed transitions take 90 days; he chose 90 days because a business quarter is a useful planning unit (as of 2021-10, Jacob Morgan interview).
> - Watkins said a third edition of the book was in progress, reorganising the self-management chapter around energy management (date unknown, from an undated interview with Lancefield).

## Notes
- The book itself is not reproduced. This note rests on Watkins' free material: HBS Working Knowledge interview "A Fast Start on Your New Job" (2003), his HBS Working Knowledge piece "How to Succeed With Your New Boss" (2002, https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/how-to-succeed-with-your-new-boss), the HBR IdeaCast "Picking the Right Transition Strategy" (2008), the MIT Sloan alumni webinar (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aLu3NazBbLw, 2020) and the Jacob Morgan interview (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_wc2MhD2l_o, 2021). See also [[Leading the Team You Inherit - Michael Watkins]].
- **Transition traps** (the *way* you win matters as much as the win): coming in with "the answer" from your last job, attempting too much, setting unrealistic expectations, getting isolated, and falling behind the learning curve. Pairs with [[The Quick Wins Paradox]].
- **Inside the five boss conversations:** *expectations* means what the boss needs short- and medium-term, what counts as success, by when and how it's measured. If expectations are unrealistic, reset them early rather than absorbing them. *Style* means preferred channel and frequency, and which decisions they want to be consulted on vs which you can make alone.
- **Two phases for early wins:** build personal credibility in roughly the first 30 days, then pick where to improve business results over the next 60, to reach the break-even point faster.
- The book's other building blocks: prepare yourself (break with the past), accelerate your learning (a learning plan), negotiate success (the boss), achieve alignment, build your team, create alliances (stakeholder map: whose support is make-or-break), manage yourself.
- `published` is the HBS interview date for the original edition; the 2013 updated edition added Accelerated growth to make STARS. Freshness is "aging" only because the window is measured from that date. The principles are classic and still the standard reference.
- **Adapting it to a marketing leader:** marketing's STARS mix often differs from the company's. A company in sustaining success can still have a pipeline turnaround. The boss conversations should cover what the CEO counts as marketing's number, who owns pipeline, and the brand-vs-demand expectation.
- **Early-win traps** (Soundview summary of the book): failing to focus; ignoring the STARS situation; importing the last company's idea of a win; picking wins that don't matter to the boss; winning by means the culture sees as manipulative. Early wins should both serve top priorities and model the behaviour you want. Pairs with [[The Quick Wins Paradox]].
- MIT Sloan webinar: Watkins quotes a former CVS CEO who made small decisions early to look decisive while deliberately deferring the big ones (about 52:00–54:00 in the video).
