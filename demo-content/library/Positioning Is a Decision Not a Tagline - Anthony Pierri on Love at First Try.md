> [!summary] Principles
> - **Treat positioning as a decision sprint, not a research project.** Gather the core decision-makers, put the viable options side by side and place a bet you'll hold for one to two years. Do the research *before* the sprint. If the team is still trying a new category every week, it isn't ready to decide.
> - **Positioning = target market + differentiation.** Messaging turns that into a story: problem framing and supporting arguments. Copywriting fits the messages to a specific asset.
> - **Ship it on the homepage to force real agreement.** There's one hero section, so agreeing on it surfaces hidden disagreement. Objections to the "copy" usually turn out to be objections to the strategy, such as not wanting to name a competitor. It also forces sales to tell the new story when prospects arrive with it.
> - **Firmographics are a weak proxy for segments.** Selling five use cases into "companies of 50–500 in North America" is five go-to-market motions, each with its own competitors, problem framing and proof. Segment by use case or job and pick the one that ladders into the biggest opportunity. Repositioning can go broader as well as narrower, whichever you can actually pull off.
> - **Don't validate a repositioning with your best customers.** They bought the old story and will tell you not to change. A big repositioning can't be A/B tested. Expect the old audience's conversion to dip and judge it over a long horizon.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-10
> - Fletch PMM, co-founded by Anthony Pierri, runs positioning as a two-week sprint: two intake questionnaires plus sales-call recordings and a demo, then three 75-minute workshops in week one (4–6 options ranked by risk, an internal decision, then refinement into a 6-slide positioning deck), then a homepage rewrite and live copy review in week two. It takes about 7 hours of client time (per fletchpmm.com).
> - Pierri's example: a trucking-analytics client repositioned from "analytics platform", where it was losing to Power BI and Tableau, to software for running driver bonus programmes; the founder later said it saved the company, and retention rose sharply (claimed, date unknown).
