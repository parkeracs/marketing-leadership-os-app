> [!summary] Principles
> - Marketing's target has moved in stages: leads → qualified leads → pipeline → qualified opportunities (SQLs) → efficient opportunities → opportunities that close → customers who renew → customers who expand. Each step fixed the flaw in the one before.
> - Lead scoring that was never regressed against closes, and an SDR process that treats every score the same, is worth little.
> - "Go get pipeline" fails without hard gates on pipeline entry and when SDR incentives let quota-carrying reps accept junk to reward their SDRs. Inflated pipeline lowers conversion, which raises the coverage target: a self-fulfilling loop.
> - The fix is a quota-carrying rep accepting only opportunities that meet documented criteria, with SDRs working for "the process" (increasingly reporting to marketing) and sales accountable for what it accepts.
> - Attribution has no right answer; a multi-touch system is still better than first/last touch for relative campaign efficiency. ICP success criteria should be renewal and expansion, not just close size and speed.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳
> - Typical old-style SDR cadence "6x6" (six emails, six calls) regardless of score; SDR bonus on accepted opportunity ~$200 (as of 2020-01)
> - Growth-stage (~$10M ARR+) companies have enough data to build ICP by regression (as of 2020-01)

## Notes
- A history of the MQL/SQL debate from a CEO's seat; the backstory for why stage acceptance and incentives matter.
