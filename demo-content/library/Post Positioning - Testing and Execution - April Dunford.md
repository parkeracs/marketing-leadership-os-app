> [!summary] Principles
> - Don't test positioning with homepage or landing-page A/B tests. They test messaging, design and CTA at once, you can't control whether the traffic is best-fit, and most B2B sites lack the volume for significance.
> - Test it instead as a sales pitch, live, with pre-qualified best-fit prospects. You see excitement and confusion, capture every question, and can rephrase on the spot.
> - Marketing and sales build the pitch together. One or two of the best reps (not six or ten) train on it, pitch real first calls, and the team tunes it after each call.
> - Write a messaging document before touching the homepage, so language stays consistent across campaigns, events and assets rather than drifting like broken telephone.
> - Say the same thing until you're sick of it: that's roughly when it starts to land. Review positioning on a regular cadence against market shifts, competitor moves and product changes.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-04
> - April Dunford (positioning consultant and author) recommends testing new positioning as a live sales pitch: one or two top reps use it on pre-qualified first substantive calls with best-fit prospects, iterating until the reps prefer the new pitch to the old.
> - Dunford's eight-part pitch structure, walked through at 11:42 of her podcast episode: insight, alternatives, perfect world, intro, value with capabilities, proof, objections and the ask.
