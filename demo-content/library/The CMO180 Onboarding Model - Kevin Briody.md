> [!summary] Principles
> - **Ninety days is a false finish line.** It's barely enough to diagnose. Alignment, strategic choices and measurable impact land in days 90–180. Planning only to day 90 either forces premature action or leaves you without a roadmap after it.
> - **Run three lenses at once, not learn → plan → act in sequence:** the revenue engine (business model, GTM motion, marketing's real pipeline contribution, the critical constraint); quick wins and burning buildings (triage); and organisational reality (how decisions actually get made, sales relationship health, capacity for change).
> - **Sequence the listening tour:** CEO, CRO and CFO first for revenue context, then product and CS, then your team, skip-levels, peers and partners. Track what people avoid saying, and treat contradictions between smart stakeholders as structural signals, not a contest.
> - **A quick win must pass a test:** visible to stakeholders who matter, achievable within 60 days with existing resources, solves a pain others already named, builds credibility with a specific group. If it needs major resources or a full strategy, it isn't a quick win.
> - **CMOs fail by misreading the system,** not for lack of marketing skill: pattern-matching from the last company, moving fast on the wrong things, or listening so long they lose the new-hire goodwill.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ (date unknown)
> - Kevin Briody's CMO180 onboarding model for a new CMO sets these milestones: meet the CEO on day 1, the CRO on day 3 and the CFO on day 5; a first quick win around day 25; a CEO check-in at day 30; an organisational assessment to the CEO around day 40; agreed shared pipeline metrics with the CRO around day 45; 3–5 strategic priorities by day 50; a revenue thesis to the exec team around day 60; budget reallocation around day 80; a CEO and board readout and a contribution baseline at day 90; and a retrospective at day 180.
> - Briody adjusts the model by situation: compress the listening phase in a turnaround; lean harder on diagnosis if you are a first-time CMO; and budget time to explain modern marketing if the company has never had a real marketing leader.
