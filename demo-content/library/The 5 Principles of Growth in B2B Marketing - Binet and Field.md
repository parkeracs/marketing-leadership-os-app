> [!summary] Principles
> - **Brand and activation work on different clocks, and you need both.** Activation converts today's in-market buyers; brand builds memories that make future activation cheaper and cut price sensitivity. Brand can make activation work better; activation can't build a brand.
> - **The B2B split leans slightly to activation**, because B2B buying is more rational than consumer buying. Binet and Field call the ratio a guiding principle, not a rule.
> - **The split moves with context** (from their *Effectiveness in Context*): a brand-new product or brand needs more activation at launch; bigger brands with high share can shift towards brand; rational, low-emotion categories need more brand, not less, when brand is hard to build.
> - **Share of voice sets budget size.** Brands whose share of voice (SOV) sits above their share of market tend to grow. Excess share of voice (ESOV) gives a way to size a budget against a market-share goal and to frame it in finance language.
> - **Innovators get a time-limited free ride.** A genuinely new product gets word of mouth that does the brand work early on. Binet warns the window closes within about two years as competitors arrive.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2019-10
> - Les Binet and Peter Field's analysis of IPA Databank B2B cases from 1998 to 2018, commissioned by the LinkedIn B2B Institute, puts the optimum B2B budget split at about 46% brand to 54% activation, against about 60/40 for B2C. Category variation is large: financial services runs about 80/20 brand-heavy (B2C data).
> - Binet and Field found 10 points of excess share of voice (ESOV) are associated with about 0.7 points of annual market-share growth in B2B, against 0.6 in B2C.
> - Binet and Field's Effectiveness in Context suggests about 35:65 brand to activation at the launch of a new brand, normalising later. Mark Ritson has argued for about 35/65 for a roughly two-year-old startup (2023 webinar, reported second-hand; as of 2023-01).
> - Binet and Field found balanced brand-plus-activation campaigns performed about 4x better than pure activation campaigns (as of 2020-01).

## Notes
- The underlying data is IPA effectiveness-award entries, which are skewed towards large, successful advertisers. Few startups are in the sample (see Jake Higgins' critique, https://jakehiggins.substack.com/p/the-wrong-and-the-short-of-it, 2023-02). Treat the ratios as directional for venture-backed SaaS.
- The canon's practitioner range sits more demand-heavy: Kyle Lacy holds 20–30% for brand ([[AI Marketing Has A Disease - Kyle Lacy on Topline]], 2026-05).
- The companion "95:5 rule" (Ehrenberg-Bass for the B2B Institute): only about 5% of B2B buyers are in market in a given quarter, which argues for reaching the whole category. Peter Weinberg and Jon Lombardo, https://www.marketingweek.com/peter-weinberg-jon-lombardo-95-5-rule/ (2021-09).
