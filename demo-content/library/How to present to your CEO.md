> [!summary] Principles
> - **Conversational presentation, not monologue.** Frame up front whether you want feedback, an FYI or sign-off; invite interruption; once you have buy-in, stop presenting and move on.
> - **Forward, not backward.** CEOs care about what's next, where you're blocked and what the risks are, more than status. Make the blocker easy to clear by recommending the default yourself.
> - **Aim for the right decision, not your exact recommendation.** A proposal that surfaces gaps or gets a reasoned "no" still added value.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳
> - Suggested split of meeting time: about a fifth on the past, the rest on what's ahead (as of 2024-01)
> - Tactics: send a pre-read or agenda in advance; open with a reminder of where you left off last time; batch approvals and have the next few steps ready while you have the CEO's attention; bring a draft or mock-up rather than describing an idea (as of 2024-01)

## Notes
- Twelve points, also covering BLUF (bottom line up front), having supporting docs open and "know your numbers": anything on a slide must be defensible and precisely scoped (which programme, which period).
- "Remind them where you left off" guards against the CEO asking why you're doing this at all, weeks after agreeing to it. It's the meeting-level form of re-earning buy-in.
- Prepare for the obvious follow-ups on any headline number, such as lead quality, the next-biggest channel and the method.
- A pre-read signals preparation, and weak ideas are harder to hide in writing.
- Written for operators with infrequent CEO access; with a CEO you see daily, batching matters less.
