> [!summary] Principles
> - **There is no 90-day onboarding luxury in growth, marketing or sales.** Revenue forecasts don't pause, so learning and delivering run together.
> - **Sequence the doing:** day 1 protect what already works (find it, don't break it, remove friction); days 2–30 quick wins; around day 30 shop a big bet; from day 30 onward draft the strategy.
> - **A quick win is something you're about 80% sure will work from past patterns.** Usually optimisations. Ask everyone "if you were me, what would you focus on?"
> - **Stress-test the big bet before committing.** Ask people why it won't work; drop it if it doesn't survive.
> - **Write the strategy draft early, while the outsider lens lasts.** Her first instincts land about 60–70% right; the draft is what gets refined.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2025-07
> - Elena Verna (growth leader whose roles span Miro, Amplitude, Dropbox and Lovable) lists the quick wins she reaches for in a new growth role: key-page optimisation, onboarding, pricing-page changes, trials, tests of new paid channels and analytics clean-up.
> - In interim roles Verna reverses her usual new-job sequence: big bet first, then quick wins, then protecting what already works, then strategy. She works to a 6 to 12 month window with a tightly scoped mandate agreed before she joins.

## Notes
- Anti-patterns she names: overpromising early wins before you know the context; shipping without stakeholder alignment; fixing things that only look broken.
- If nothing is working (broken funnel, unclear positioning, no PMF): unblock one core path and rally around one metric.
- PLG/growth lens (Miro, Amplitude, Dropbox, Lovable). Translate the tactics for sales-led B2B; the sequence transfers.
