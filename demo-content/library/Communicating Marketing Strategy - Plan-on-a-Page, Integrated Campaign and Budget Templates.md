> [!summary] Principles
> - The financial plan isn't the end of planning: after it's signed off comes the detailed planning, socialising with interdependent teams and company-wide communication. A one-page plan with laddered goals and visible dependencies carries it.
> - "Show them the beacon, not the blizzard": keep the detail for the team, bring the plan-on-a-page back at the start of every quarter to check in, make trade-offs and replan.
> - Don't plan at full capacity. Leave slack for what the CEO and the market will demand after the plan is inked, or you'll be forced to drop something. Use allocation models ("how would we spend $100") to make relative priority an explicit conversation.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳
> - Plans at 85–90% capacity (as of 2025-01)
> - Integrated campaign themes should run at least 6 months (as of 2025-01)
> - Plan-on-a-page and campaign templates from an Inverta / Uptempo webinar series (close to an older SiriusDecisions/Forrester template); Uptempo recommended for spend tagging beyond spreadsheet scale (vendor content) (as of 2025-01)

## Notes
- Budget work runs in four passes: how much overall (benchmarks), how to allocate, exactly where to spend, then ongoing tracking against spend and impact.
- Allocation can sit above campaigns (segment, industry, geography). A 20/80 split is a different strategy from 50/50, so make the split the debate.
- Integrated campaigns: one buyer-pain theme that lasts, with all tactics multiplying it, rather than each sub-team running its own loosely related activity.
- Keep two calendars: a detailed tactical one for the team and a high-level company calendar that also shows competitor and world events for execs.
- Planning gets better year on year only if budget, campaign, performance and ROI data are kept clean all year.
- Caveat: much of the piece showcases Uptempo features. Treat the tool parts as vendor-adjacent.
