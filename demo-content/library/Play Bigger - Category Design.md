> [!summary] Principles
> - **The category makes the brand, not the other way round.** Buyers file products into mental "containers". They must buy the idea of the category before they compare vendors in it. Their phrase: position yourself or be positioned.
> - **Category design starts with the problem, not the name.** Sequence: name the missing thing you see in the world → your solution → the category name (market type + problem + who has it) → a point of view (the problem, the cost of inaction, the future, your answer) → a product blueprint and ecosystem map → company-wide mobilisation → "lightning strikes".
> - **A lightning strike is a company-wide moment, not a campaign.** Put a date on the calendar, build belief inside first (the CEO commits publicly to staff), lead with the problem, and repeat strikes over years. Peterson stresses precision: a strike on 25 target accounts can beat a 2,000-person event.
> - **Design, not creation:** winning isn't being first to ship. It's being the first whose definition of the problem tips at scale. Categories can be redesigned (Apple and the phone).
> - **Product design, company design and category design must advance together.** The CEO owns it.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2015
> - The authors of Play Bigger (Ramadan, Peterson, Lochhead and Maney) claim category kings take 76% of their category's market cap, from their own analysis of US venture-backed tech companies founded 2000–2015 (PrivCo data; about 4,400 with a Series A, 69–75 IPOs). Market cap includes last private valuations and acquisition prices, and "category king" is defined after the fact as the company that ended up dominant, so quote it as the authors' claim.
> - Play Bigger's research also claims an IPO "sweet spot": in its sample, companies that went public at 6.5–10.5 years old created the post-IPO value.
> - A related stat often cited alongside Play Bigger: Eddie Yoon (HBR) reported that 13 category creators on Fortune's fastest-growing list for 2009–2011 delivered 53% of incremental revenue growth and 74% of incremental market-cap growth (as of 2013-03).

## Notes
- **This is the counter-view to [[April Dunford]].** She starts with competitive alternatives and picks the category last; Play Bigger starts with the problem and treats the category as the strategic prize. Both cite Ries and Trout and both put the CEO at the centre.
- **Evidence caveat:** the 76% figure is survivorship-shaped. The king is identified after the fact and failed category attempts aren't counted, so it doesn't show that *trying* to create a category pays. See [[Pioneer Advantage - Marketing Logic or Marketing Legend - Golder and Tellis]] and [[No New Categories - Greg Kogan]]. Quote it as the authors' claim, never as a finding.
- Other free sources: playbigger.com/what-is-category-design (the seven building blocks); Stanford eCorner talk "How to Be a Category King" (2016-05); Play Bigger's "CMO Coffee Talk" recap PDF (lightning strike detail); Lochhead's own blog post separating category creation from category design (2021-04). Later books: *Niche Down*, *Category Pirates*, *The Existing Market Trap* (2025).
