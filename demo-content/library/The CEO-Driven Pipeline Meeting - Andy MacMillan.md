> [!summary] Principles
> - **The CEO runs a weekly sales-and-marketing pipeline meeting and doesn't delegate it.** MacMillan ranks it with the exec staff meeting as one of the two most important meetings on his calendar.
> - **"Pipeline" means different things to each side.** To marketing it's top of funnel; to sales it's whether reps have enough quality late-stage deals to hit this quarter. The meeting exists to close that gap.
> - **Alternate the view weekly, with both teams in the room every time.** Marketing weeks walk the funnel from spend to MQL, SQL and closed deal, hunting leaks. Sales weeks use a standard RevOps deck of coverage by rep and stage, and the group works the reps who are short.
> - **It's an alignment meeting that happens to use numbers,** not a numbers review.
> - **Expect weeks of unglamorous set-up:** agreeing definitions and stages and fixing reporting until everyone trusts the numbers. Over time the meeting tells you when to fund marketing, where to add reps and how to restructure territories.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-07
> - Andy MacMillan (a scale-up CEO writing about how he runs his company) starts his weekly CEO-led pipeline meeting with 10–12 people: the CRO, regional sales leaders, RevOps, the CMO, demand gen, field marketing, product marketing and the CEO.
> - For the sales-view weeks of that meeting, MacMillan uses one slide per front-line sales team, with two stacked bars for each rep: quota against attainment, and pipeline coverage by stage. It shows quickly which reps are short of pipeline.
> - MacMillan suggests splitting the meeting into separate regional sessions once a company passes roughly $100M in revenue.
