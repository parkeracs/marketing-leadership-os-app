> [!summary] Principles
> - **The debate is mostly about time horizon.** The positioning camp (Dunford, Pierri) is right that leaving an existing category means stepping out of the river of inbound demand, so you must create purchase demand from nothing. The category camp (Lochhead) is right that permanent share-and-price combat in the wrong category is also expensive. Category design pays later and costs now.
> - **The third option is a "bridge category":** keep the existing category noun and add a modifier that signals your point of view. Examples: horseless carriage → car; collaborative design (Figma) versus Sketch's interface design. You catch existing demand and prime the market for a new frame.
> - **"Dam the demand"** (a Category Pirates idea): intercept buyers while they shop the old category and redirect them. "You think you want that, but you need this." The modifier sits in front of a known category: electric car, smart watch, cloud software.
> - **Feature bridges vs POV bridges:** a functional modifier ("treadmill desk") describes the product. A POV modifier ("collaborative") forces the buyer to choose a belief, which is the stronger form.
> - **Revisit the category once you have resources:** with several million in ARR and a Series A or more, a company can drop the bridge and push for the novel category.

- His companion piece "Positioning For Traction" (2024-03) lists what the two camps share: differentiation, POV, top-down ownership, customer-centricity. It also names the real disagreement as **where you start**: Dunford with competitive alternatives, Play Bigger with the problem.
