> [!summary] Principles
> - **Build three plans, named by confidence of hitting them.** **C-60** (60% confidence) is the base plan: everyone's goals, the board plan, the hiring plan. **C-10** is the stretch plan, paid in bonuses if hit. **C-90** keeps C-60's costs but cuts revenue, purely to manage cash and runway.
> - **Draft C-60 fast from an "L4M" model:** average the last four months' growth in revenue, each cost line and burn, roll forward, then adjust for known big deals, trends or costs.
> - **Don't hide from C-90.** Founders who avoid the downside plan get caught out; if runway looks short under it, decide now what to cut or raise.
> - **Set the sales plan at C-60** so most reps can hit it (aim for ~80% attaining) and base the VP Sales' OTE on it; pay well beyond OTE towards C-10 ([Dear SaaStr: how should I set the sales plan](https://www.saastr.com/dear-saastr-how-should-i-actually-set-the-sales-plan-for-the-year-for-my-vp-sales/), 2025-04).

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2024-11
> - Jason Lemkin (SaaStr founder) says the stretch plan (C-10) is usually about 20% above the base plan (C-60) on revenue, while the downside plan (C-90) cuts revenue by about 20%, which typically pushes burn up 20–25%. He advises keeping 16 months or more of runway.
