> [!summary] Principles
> - **The story is the strategy, so the CEO authors it.** Raskin only takes engagements where the CEO leads hands-on. Messaging debates are really strategy debates, and only the CEO can make the hard cuts and manage "story backlash" when choices become visible.
> - **Run it through a small narrative team:** the CEO plus up to four leaders (sales, marketing, product, sometimes CTO/COO). Kickoff to gather story pieces, customer interviews, CEO + Raskin draft, team critique, rebuild, then design. Expect the first critique to be the low point, as people see their ideas cut.
> - **Interview customers about the shift, not the product.** Ask what has changed in their world that makes the product more valuable now. Customer language acts as the tiebreaker between executives' competing directions.
> - **The sales deck is the core narrative asset,** not an internal messaging document. Fragmented message docs never get reused outside marketing. Website, content and investor deck flow from a tested sales narrative.
> - **Test in real sales calls before rolling out widely.** Working = prospects nod along and open up about challenges that set up your features. "Stop wasting my time" means wrong prospect or wrong story. Update the story when the strategy changes, not on a calendar.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2025-11
> - Andy Raskin (strategic-narrative consultant) says his narrative engagements run about 6–8 weeks, and from week 3 or 4 the draft goes into live sales calls with a few reps before wider rollout (Sales Transformation Podcast, date unknown; consistent with this 2025-11 interview).
> - Raskin advises aiming for a strategic narrative you can commit to for roughly six months to two years (Notion Capital AMA, date unknown).
> - Raskin's sign of leadership-team alignment on a narrative is not that everyone loves it but that objections shrink to wording requests; he says about one team in seven or eight needs an extra iteration (LinkedIn, as of 2023-01).
> - Raskin cites Marc Benioff's rollout tactic at Salesforce: a certification so every employee, not just sales, can deliver the short version of the current pitch.
