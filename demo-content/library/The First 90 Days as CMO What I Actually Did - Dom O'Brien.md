> [!summary] Principles
> - **The organisation doesn't wait for your 90 days.** Live campaigns, a launch already scheduled, a team member heading for the door and commitments made to sales all need decisions in week 1. A clean listening phase is mostly theoretical in a scaling company.
> - **Days 1–30 are for finding what's on fire and what's quietly smouldering.** Protect what's genuinely good (working campaigns, solid processes, excellent people) and leave it alone. Hunt for what everyone has managed around for so long they've forgotten it's a problem: an unquestioned channel strategy, a weekly CEO report nobody uses, an underperforming agency that predates everyone.
> - **Ask slightly naive questions early:** "Why do we do it this way?", "What would happen if we stopped this?", "What would you change?" People tell a newcomer things in the first two weeks that they won't say once you're part of the establishment.
> - **The quick-wins trap:** optimising for *visible* wins over *correct* ones. Accelerate decisions you'd make anyway (if the agency should go in month three, cut it in month two). Don't manufacture early decisions just to look decisive.
> - **Plan for several scenarios,** and work out which version of the CMO role you were actually hired into. A hidden CAC problem, a product gap marketing is meant to paper over, or a CEO–CFO split on marketing's job each change the plan. Is that version the one you can do well, the one you want, and the one the business needs?

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-03
> - Dom O'Brien (CMO, writing about his own first 90 days) advises stress-testing the plan before day 1: what happens if the budget is cut in month two, the inherited launch slips, or the person described as excellent turns out to be the structural problem?
