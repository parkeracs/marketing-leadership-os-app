> [!summary] Principles
> - Differentiated value is what you can do for the customer's business that no alternative can. It's the reason to pick you.
> - Support, services, training, modular architecture, compliance (SOC 2, HIPAA) and integrations are usually objection handling: their absence disqualifies you, but they're rarely why you win. Test: would anyone choose you for that alone?
> - Sell value to the champion and economic buyer, and handle the objections of IT, procurement, legal, security and end users, who can kill a deal but can't make one.
> - Exception: when buyers are fleeing a vendor with terrible support or stability, those can become genuine, quantified value themes.
> - Some capabilities drive retention, not acquisition. Leaving them out of the pitch doesn't mean they don't matter to the business.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2025-02
> - In April Dunford's example of the exception to her rule, a challenger whose buyers were fleeing an unstable vendor used uptime statistics and a guaranteed one-hour support response to make stability a value pillar rather than just objection handling.
