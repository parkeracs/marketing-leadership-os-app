> [!summary] Principles
> - **Build the budget twice and expect a gap.** Top-down (RevOps and finance set bookings and pipeline targets; work back to win rates, deal sizes, cycles, marketing's sourced share by channel) tells you what the business needs. Bottom-up (leaders' view of coverage needs, regional events, waste to reallocate) tells you what the people closest to the work think they can deliver. They never match.
> - **Close the gap by tagging every budget line to a "mode" and a metric.** **Direct**: a measurable path to pipeline (paid, demand-gen programmes, field events, ABM). **Influence**: moves deals already in motion (video, content, research, PR, AR); give evidence if not pipeline. **Enablement**: makes GTM better (competitive intel, enablement content, the tooling behind Direct and Influence). **Internal ops**: fixed cost of the department (AI and token spend, data, culture, training).
> - **Add a "risk if cut" column** so every team documents what breaks if a line goes unfunded. It's the best defence in a budget review.
> - **Every submission carries funnel maths** from history, a benchmark or a labelled judgement call, and leaders follow up on whether judgement calls are working.
> - **Set the rules before submissions:** one attribution model for everyone, shared influence-metric definitions, benchmarks labelled as benchmarks, who breaks ties on modes, and dashboards built before you promise monthly tracking.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-10
> - Kyle Lacy (CMO at Docebo, a public company) sets a guardrail for "Influence" marketing spend, the content, research, PR and analyst work that moves deals already in motion: 10–20x pipeline influenced for every $1 spent. Replace it with your own data where you have it.
