> [!summary] Principles
> - **Revenue = pipeline × conversion, and conversion depends on AE capacity.** Plan backwards: target → deals → opportunities → meetings → marketing budget (via cost per meeting) → AE headcount. Hire AEs only when the pipeline forecast shows they're needed. Don't hire first and tell them to prospect.
> - **AEs close, they don't prospect.** Inbound plus BDRs feed the calendar. AE self-sourcing is mostly theatre and poor economics.
> - **Marketing is measured on closed-won.** Canaani, a former marketer, holds marketing to revenue: "you don't count assists" if the shot misses. Brand is the deliberate exception, with no MQL or pipeline target, so it can stay creative.
> - **Each function owns its own maths.** Growth owns top-of-funnel conversion and cost per meeting, RevOps owns the instrumentation, sales knows how many meetings it will get this quarter.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳
> - Datarails moved from ~90% outbound to ~90% inbound after outbound stopped converting in 2022. New-ARR forecast landed within 5% in three of four quarters. Sales cycle runs 30–45 days (as of 2026-03; SMB/mid-market FP&A).
> - Norton (Owner): AE cap of four booked and three held demos a day, the point where quality starts to break. Reps self-source no more than 10–15% of deals. Meetings-per-AE capacity is reset quarterly at Datarails (as of 2026-03).
> - Illustrative planning maths: 200 deals at 50% conversion needs 400 opportunities; budget set from a hypothetical ~$3k cost per meeting (as of 2026-03).

## Notes
- Read in full on the show's Substack write-up plus a transcript-checked summary (operatorssaid.com).
- Works when: inbound-led, short-cycle SMB/mid-market with a predictable cost per meeting. Fails when: long enterprise cycles, where outbound or territory-based models dominate.
- Overproduction is a real failure mode: on days when every AE is in a demo at once, managers take hot handoffs and BDRs get promoted to add capacity. Marketing and sales must plan capacity together.
- Datarails replaced rep-chosen closed-lost reasons with paid customer exit interviews, giving a better read on lead quality than rep opinion.
