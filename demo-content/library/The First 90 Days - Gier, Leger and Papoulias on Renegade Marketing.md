> [!summary] Principles
> - **One shot at the big ask.** Leger kept asking for time, then returned at day 90 with an end-to-end assessment: mission, operating principles, full plan, budget and team changes, and his specific asks. Asks made piecemeal get lost. His CEO gave him 90 minutes with the whole exec team.
> - **Seed the conclusion before the readout.** Leger sent the CEO a Friday note every week (what I learned, who I met, early thoughts), so the final proposal was already half-agreed. Papoulias showed her written 90-day plan to the CEO on day three and used it to steer what she was pulled into.
> - **Restraint reads as seniority.** Strategy takes time; tactics are fast. Jumping to "it's a brand problem, change the logo" in week one burns credibility. Leger's CEO noticed he spoke rarely but meaningfully.
> - **Quick wins buy time, and the best ones help sales.** Examples: a two-week tiger team writing tactical messaging sales kept asking for; joining a launch's packaging and pricing work; opening C-suite doors on live deals from your own network. Gier found the pipeline pressure came more from the CRO than the CEO.
> - **Set expectations before day one ("T-minus").** Gier tests how the CEO views the CMO role, the stage and what happened to the predecessor during the interview process, and states her assumptions. A first-ever CMO is harder: the company doesn't know what it doesn't know.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2023-11
> - Leger, a B2B CMO interviewed on Drew Neisser's Renegade Marketing podcast, sat through a two-day sales QBR in his first weeks and later presented the sales reps' quotes, with their photos, back to the QBR. It earned him a reputation as a listener.
> - Papoulias, a B2B CMO on the same episode, was invited to a board meeting between accepting the offer and her start date, and called it the single most valuable onboarding step.
> - Before starting, Leger wrote an outside-in assessment of the company of about 40 slides and sent it to the CEO and leadership team.
