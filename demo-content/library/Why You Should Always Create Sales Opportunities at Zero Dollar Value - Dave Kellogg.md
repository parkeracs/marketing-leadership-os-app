> [!summary] Principles
> - **Create every opportunity at $0.** Enter a value only once it has been discussed with the customer. Placeholder values (a flat $25K, or ASP) create an "air bubble" in early-stage pipeline, and you can never tell a real $100K from a fake one.
> - **"Pipeline" with no adjective means all opportunities with a close date in the period.** Don't quietly mean "stage 3+ this quarter". Say which you mean.
> - **Pipeline you ignore in analytics gets ignored in operations.** If conversion is measured only from a late stage, stages 1–3 become a dumping ground. Inspect and scrub all of it.
> - **Stage weights come from regressions on consistent stage definitions,** not thin air or arbitrary zeroing.
> - **Pipeline entry is a safe-deposit box: two keys turn.** One from the SDR/BDR and one from the seller. The criteria can be debated, as long as they're written down (from Kellogg's reply in the comments).

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2022-02
> - In Dave Kellogg's (CEO and board member) illustrative funnel, stage-2 (sales-accepted) opportunities close at about 16%, inside the 10–25% range he typically sees.
> - To benchmark against peers who put placeholder values on new opportunities, Kellogg suggests reporting a separate "implied pipeline" rather than polluting the real one.
> - Kellogg warns of the opposite game, where sales holds deals at $0 to "cry poor". Catch it by reviewing opportunity count per rep alongside dollar coverage.
