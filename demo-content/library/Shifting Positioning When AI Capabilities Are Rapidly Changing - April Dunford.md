> [!summary] Principles
> - A structured process is what saves you from positioning whiplash. When capabilities change, re-walk the steps rather than vibing whether the change is big.
> - Re-check in order: are prospects now seeing you as an alternative to something new? Have your distinct capabilities changed (rivals use the same models)? Do any of this shift your value themes?
> - Value themes evolve more slowly than features. A new capability that strengthens an existing theme changes only how you explain it, not the positioning.
> - Rework from step one only when a capability unlocks genuinely new value unrelated to your current themes.
> - If your positioning is really a feature list, it will feel like it needs to change every release.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-09
> - April Dunford (positioning consultant) reports that AI startup founders she met worried frequent foundation-model releases would make their positioning out of date within weeks.
> - Dunford's worked example is an application-security vendor: if its AI agent moves from detecting threats to responding automatically, the value theme is the same and no repositioning is needed, but adding a compliance-audit agent creates new value and means reworking the positioning. A test for when a product change warrants repositioning.
