> [!summary] Principles
> - **Two meetings, two jobs.** The forecast call manages conversion of deals that already exist, this quarter. The pipeline generation meeting manages supply for the next one to two quarters. Merge them and the forecast always wins the airtime.
> - **Track creation as a weekly pace, not a quarterly total.** Eleven weeks into a thirteen-week quarter you can't fix a total, only a rate you've been watching since week two.
> - **Show source mix with each source's conversion to close.** A quarter that hits its creation target through a low-converting channel is a supply problem that surfaces two quarters later.
> - **The pipeline meeting's unit is sources and segments; the forecast call's unit is deals.** Marketing, SDR leadership, sales and RevOps attend the first; sales leadership the second.
> - **Run them in sequence.** The forecast call sets next quarter's projected coverage gap; the generation meeting turns that gap into a weekly creation pace by segment and source, with owners.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2025-12
> - Pete Furseth (founder of ORM Technologies, a revenue-analytics vendor) suggests running the pipeline generation meeting weekly or fortnightly and the forecast call weekly, with reps submitting their numbers before the call so it works only the exceptions.
