> [!summary] Principles
> - **Earn the brand line by hitting pipeline.** Lacy's rule: fund the demand you need from most of the budget, then protect the rest for brand and memorable bets. Marketing leaders without pipeline credibility lose the brand line first when budget tightens.
> - **Plan the budget as one sales-and-marketing envelope, with sales in the room.** Resist the sequence where sales' AE capacity model comes first and marketing is handed whatever is left. Ask the CEO what they actually care about. Usually it's sales-and-marketing efficiency (CAC), because that is what the board asks about. Agree to hit that and keep the choices inside marketing.
> - **Fix a bad relationship with the sales leader immediately,** or consider leaving. Sales will always hold more weight at the exec table because it carries revenue.
> - **Keep the CMO–CRO tension at exec level.** Lacy (and Sam Jacobs, from running CRO searches) argue against folding marketing under the CRO for efficiency's sake. Sales leaders find brand, creativity and long-term bets harder to value, and the brand items that don't attribute to closed-won are the first cut. Exception: if marketing can't do pipeline and conversion, reporting to the CRO may be right.
> - **Start positioning from a one-page CEO manifesto,** not a framework. Messaging pillars, ICP and personas flow from it.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳
> - Lacy's split: 70–80% of budget on demand, 20–30% held for brand that "nobody but the marketer" touches (as of 2026-05). Sam Jacobs cites market research favouring 60/40 brand/demand against a typical 70/30 demand-heavy reality (as of 2026-05, source not named).
> - Jacobs advises CEOs and boards hiring a CRO at roughly $30–40M ARR to take marketing out of the remit (as of 2026-05).
> - Lacy reports product and engineering now ship faster than marketing can keep up with, thanks to AI, which raises the value of product marketing (as of 2026-05).

## Notes
- Contradicts the "CRO as gatekeeper" setup in part: Lacy wants the CMO reporting to the CEO and presenting a joint sales-and-marketing case, not routing through the CRO. Both views agree that the plan must be built with sales.
- Lacy is now at a public company. He says his envelope argument was made at Series A–C, and that public-company planning differs.
- He acknowledges "every CEO thinks they're a marketer". Asad Zaman points out that few marketing leaders actually own a revenue number, which is the root of the brand-budget problem.
