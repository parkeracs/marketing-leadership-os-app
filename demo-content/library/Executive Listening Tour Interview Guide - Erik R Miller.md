> [!summary] Principles
> - **A listening tour produces impressions; a structured one produces understanding you can compare.** Ask the same core questions every time so answers can be lined up, not just remembered. The structure stays under the conversation, so to the other person it should feel like a good discussion.
> - **Cover four areas:** business (how we really make money, what limits it, what's already committed), organisational (how decisions actually move, where handoffs fail), customer (who knows customers best and what they know), leadership (what each leader thinks the priorities are and how they define success).
> - **Mix three question types in every conversation:** open questions to discover; scaled 1–5 questions asked identically so answers line up; ranking questions that force trade-offs into the open (e.g. "rank these initiatives in the order you'd protect them if budget were cut").
> - **The variance is the finding.** Ask every senior leader the same alignment questions, separately and word for word: top three priorities; how we'll know we succeeded a year from now; who owns key decisions; the biggest constraint on growth; what the new leader should change first and what not to touch. Where leaders quietly disagree on things that should have one answer is often the most useful discovery of the transition.
> - **Consensus is a finding, not a verdict.** Use variance only on questions that should have one answer, and check low variance against what's actually happening. A team can agree and be wrong together.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ (date unknown)
> - Erik R. Miller's guide to a new executive's listening tour says to stop adding interviews once new themes stop appearing. A small company reaches that point quickly; a global one needs wider coverage.
