> [!summary] Principles
> - **Separate the account from the person.** A PQA (product-qualified account) is usage aggregated at the company or workspace level. A PQL exists only when a real buyer or decision-maker is inside that usage. An MQL is the buyer marketing has to find when the usage has no buyer in it.
> - **Three lead buckets, three playbooks.** Usage with a buyer in it; usage with no buyer (marketing must source one and connect it to the usage); a top-down lead with no usage at all. They convert differently and often need different sales teams, so don't report or route them as one funnel.
> - **Never point sales at a buyer-less PQA.** It produces cold outbound that users ignore and a worse product experience. Up-market, most of the work is finding a buyer who is not yet a user.
> - **Score PQAs on volume, velocity and breadth of usage,** plus behavioural tells (admin transfers, visits to terms or security pages). Close hand-raisers first, then approach lookalike accounts that behave like hand-raisers but haven't asked.
> - **Prove it by hand before you buy tooling.** Run PLS manually with existing systems and close sales collaboration, then automate. Revisit the PQA definition with sales every quarter: it drifts.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2023-04
> - Elena Verna (growth leader at Netlify, Miro and Amplitude), on Lenny's Podcast, says durable enterprise contracts at those companies typically followed at least about 12 months of self-serve usage. A guide to how long product-led sales takes to mature into enterprise deals.
> - Verna says sales outreach to an account should stop when it drops back below the product-qualified account (PQA) usage threshold.
