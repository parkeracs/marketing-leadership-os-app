> [!summary] Principles
> - **Next year's plan is won or lost in this year's Q3–Q4.** Recurring revenue only recognises 1/12 of ACV a month, so a slow Q1 can't be made up later.
> - **Build a real operating model now,** with exact headcount and budget: back into the reps, SDRs, MQLs, marketing budget and marketing team you need month by month to hit an end-of-year ARR target.
> - **Hire ahead of need:** reps you need for Q1–Q2 must be hired and ramping by mid-Q4. CS too.
> - **Start next year's demand gen in Q4,** and work out how to fund it. MQLs have a lead time; if Q4 lead flow is short you miss Q1 and Q2.
> - **Pad the plan:** carefully calculated rep and lead numbers assume too much efficiency and a too-perfect hiring plan.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2019-10
> - When planning next year's go-to-market hiring, Jason Lemkin (founder of SaaStr) advises multiplying the calculated number of sales reps and leads or MQLs by 1.5, or by 2 if the company is growing very fast.
> - Jason Lemkin says it takes at least about 60 days to ramp a SaaS sales rep, usually more, which is why reps needed for the first half of next year must be hired and ramping by mid-Q4.
