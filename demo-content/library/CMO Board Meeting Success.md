> [!summary] Principles
> - **Answer three questions:** strategic (market dynamics and the growth levers over several years), present (did we hit pipeline and revenue, and why), and future (will we hit next quarter and the year, and why).
> - **Don't give a weather report.** Many CMOs over-index on the present: funnel metrics and activity. Boards want digested insight and a thesis to debate. Earn the seat as the voice of the customer and market.
> - **Be aligned with sales and their data before the meeting.** Pre-meet the CRO and agree numbers, plans and remedies. No pipeline discrepancies, no finger-pointing.
> - **Be explicit about the ask:** FYI, advice, a decision or money.
> - **Use a small, consistent set of stats** and report the same ones every quarter to build trust.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2024-06
> - Carilu Dietrich (hypergrowth marketing leader turned adviser) tells CMOs in board meetings to start every answer with "Yes", "No", a number or "I don't know".
> - Carilu Dietrich advises keeping board slides to the core summary and putting supporting data in a pre-read appendix or a day-of appendix, treating the meeting as an open-notes test.
> - Carilu Dietrich suggests that, with the CEO's approval, the CMO pre-meets individual board members to check the agenda and learn their hot buttons and history.
> - Carilu Dietrich's board-meeting logistics checklist covers meeting dates, the pre-read deadline, team prep time, CEO revision time, time to align with the CRO, design, and a calm buffer before the meeting.
> - Carilu Dietrich's prep timeline for a CMO's board section: debrief with the CEO after each meeting; 4–6 weeks out, agree marketing's agenda with the CEO; 4–8 weeks out, assign data and insight projects; 3–6 weeks out, outline and first draft (using the QBR for analysis); 2–4 weeks out, revise and prepare Q&A; 1–2 weeks out, revise with the CEO, CFO, sales and design; 2 days to 1 week out, stop editing and send the pre-read.
> - The slide outline Carilu Dietrich sees in effective CMO board decks: market view (competition, ICP evolution, win-loss, TAM and SAM, linked back to the fundraising story); marketing focus areas and how they ladder to company strategy; quarter stats and forecast (pipeline percentage to goal by segment, sourced and influenced trends, conversion, with MQLs and brand awareness debated as appendix items); CAC, payback or magic number; next-quarter and rest-of-year forecast; upcoming goals; and a short "sizzle reel".
> - For optional storytelling in a board deck, Carilu Dietrich suggests Latané Conant's won and lost deal profile, or the journey of one or two big deals from first touch to close.

## Notes
- Five steps: know the three questions, be strategic, clear and brief, be prepared, align with sales and the CEO, and be calm and confident. Christine Heckart's "less is more" is quoted.
- For a new CMO the first board meeting is usually within the first quarter. Combine with [[How to Onboard Your CMO]] (the likely brief: assessment, priorities, metrics, goals, sequencing, asks).
- You don't need every problem solved, only a deep grasp of it, a solution, a timeline and metrics.
- Pairs with [[Six Tips on Presenting to the Board of Directors]] (Kellogg) and [[Board-Level Questions On The Marketing Budget]].
- Context she gives: boards' expertise is strategy, finance and governance. She cites Spencer Stuart data that only about 4% of Fortune 1000 directors are CMOs, so marketing detail rarely lands.
- Chandar Pattabhiram (Workato, ex-Coupa and Marketo CMO), quoted: keep a few consistent high-level slides. When the business has a problem the board will demand every detail; then return to simple and brief.
