> [!summary] Principles
> - Companies overthink category. Starting a positioning project with 'help us define a new category' produces an aspirational category the product in market can't support.
> - You need a category-creating product to justify a category-creating position. Otherwise buyers see through it ('it just looks like a CRM') and sales spends forever arguing.
> - First rule of market categories: do no harm. Then aim for a frame that sets off true assumptions so marketing and sales start further along.
> - Workshop test: hold the agreed value and best-fit definition against the current category. If it points buyers at your value, don't touch it. Change it only when it points them elsewhere.
> - Three styles of play: Head-to-Head (win the existing category), Big Fish Small Pond (dominate an underserved sub-segment) and Create a New Game. Each needs different conditions, and the last carries long-term risks.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-03
> - April Dunford (positioning consultant and author of Obviously Awesome) says she has worked with roughly 300 companies since the book's first edition.
> - In Dunford's positioning workshops, bigger companies change their best-fit customer definition least; smaller ones often learn who truly fits from the value step of the exercise.
