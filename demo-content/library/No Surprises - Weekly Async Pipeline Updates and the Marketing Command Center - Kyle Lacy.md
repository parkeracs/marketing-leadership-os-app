> [!summary] Principles
> - **"No surprises" needs infrastructure, not a slogan.** The worst thing a leader can do is surprise the people above, beside and below them; preventing it takes a built reporting rhythm that drives the number proactively rather than reporting it after the fact.
> - **A written weekly rhythm with a fixed deadline and a fixed review slot.** Pipeline owners update async by Friday end of day; the CMO, CRO and RevOps lead review on Monday and log questions, risks and gaps in the same document; owners answer them in the next Friday update.
> - **The update is two questions:** on or off track, and pacing vs plan; if off track, what's broken, what you're doing and what changes in the next 1–2 weeks. On track with nothing material changed is a one-line confirmation; off track means detail every week until resolved.
> - **Build it jointly with the CRO.** When CMO and CRO work from the same context every week, the "I thought you said…" arguments about whose memory of the data is right disappear.
> - **Make marketing visible before anyone asks.** A standing, always-on view of campaigns, goals, calendar and pipeline stops leadership wondering what marketing is doing; trust erodes through "a thousand small information gaps".

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-04
> - Kyle Lacy (CMO of Docebo, a public software company) runs the weekly pipeline update in a shared Slack canvas, with RevOps updating the numbers in a shared dashboard; updates are required even when the numbers are flat ("if you own a row of pipeline, you own the update"). The format is written up as a team Read Me.
> - Docebo's marketing chief of staff rolls the weekly pipeline updates into a fortnightly marketing update for senior leadership, published as an always-on "marketing command center" (campaign updates, OKRs, calendar, pipeline data) that she vibe-coded with Lovable.
> - Lacy says they adjusted the cadence four times in its first quarter of use (it launched at the start of 2026): expect to iterate on the rhythm, but keep the commitment.
