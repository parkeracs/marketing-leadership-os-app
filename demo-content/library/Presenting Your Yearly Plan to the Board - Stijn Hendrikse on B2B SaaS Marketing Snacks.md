> [!summary] Principles
> - **Lead with the red, not with your chin.** Don't bring an ask you can't defend under questioning. Open the year-in-review with what didn't go to plan and what you'll do about it. You aren't expected to be perfect at the job, just the right person for it.
> - **Agree three numbers with the CEO and board before writing the plan:** the growth target, net revenue retention, and the profitability balance (a Rule-of-40-type number). Every marketing dollar is then judged against growth plus profit.
> - **Present a go-to-market plan, not a marketing plan.** A siloed marketing plan loses the room. Show the whole pipeline mix (marketing, sales-sourced, partners, referrals), agreed with the sales and CS leaders.
> - **Turn initiatives into unit-economics drivers** (conversion, ASP, churn) so the plan visibly adds up to the revenue target. Filter hundreds of ideas down to the 5–10 that matter, then the few for Q1.
> - **Set quota uplift in a hierarchy:** the further from the deal, the smaller the stretch over the financial plan. A CRO on the same uplift as reps has an incentive to keep weak sellers.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2023-12
> - Stijn Hendrikse (serial SaaS CMO, founder of the agency Kalungi) uses a sales-productivity litmus test: net new bookings should be at least 3x the fully loaded cost of sales.
> - Hendrikse recommends setting sales targets 10–30% above the financial plan, higher where bookings are less predictable, with a smaller uplift the further a role sits from the deal; his example ladder is CRO 15%, managers 20%, reps 25%.
> - Stijn Hendrikse calls gross revenue retention of 95–97% best in class for B2B SaaS. (The note also recorded "2–3% churn", which does not match: 95–97% retention means 3–5% annual churn. Check the episode before quoting the churn figure.)
