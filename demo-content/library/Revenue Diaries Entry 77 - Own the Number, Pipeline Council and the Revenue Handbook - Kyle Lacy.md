> [!summary] Principles
> - **Own a number tied to revenue, and build the model with finance** rather than having it handed down. Lacy bonuses the team on pipeline and revenue; at Docebo, product marketing co-owns pipeline with the product managers for its products.
> - **Weekly async pipeline updates by every pipeline owner** (inbound, outbound, partnerships): on track or off, pacing vs plan, and if off, what's broken, what you're doing and what changes in the next 1–2 weeks. RevOps updates the numbers; updates are required even when flat. CMO, CRO and VP RevOps review them early in the week.
> - **Never surprise the CEO.** Flag a trend (e.g. enterprise unit volume falling while dollars hold) before you have the fix.
> - **Monthly (or twice-quarterly) pipeline council** with CEO, CFO, CMO, CRO, VP Sales, RevOps and revenue marketing, run from a four-part pipeline memo: what's working and what needs fixing; attainment by channel, segment and industry; coverage for the next two quarters; next quarter's goals by segment in units and dollars.
> - **RevOps owns the pipeline council, not marketing or sales.** When marketing runs it, sales gets defensive, and vice versa; RevOps is "Switzerland", so the talk stays on the pipeline rather than blame. The monthly memo to the exec team feeds straight into it.
> - **Write a signed revenue handbook** (inbound follow-up SLAs, how fast an AE must convert to an opportunity, territory, account ownership, parent/child logic). Its real job is arbitration of BDR–AE credit fights.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-08
> - Kyle Lacy (CMO, Docebo) feeds his pipeline owners' weekly async updates into an AI model that drafts the monthly pipeline memo; the team edits the draft, cutting hours of work to a couple.
> - Lacy's monthly demand memo to the executive team has three parts: an executive overview of what's working and what's not, pipeline attainment for the quarter with a year-over-year comparison, and a look ahead to next month; open exec questions about how pipeline is produced get answered there (as of 2026-03).
> - Lacy's attribution rule of thumb: with a sales cycle under 90 days, direct source attribution is defensible; longer than that, model pipeline from assumptions (average sales price, cycle length, win rates by segment, coverage ratios) built with finance and RevOps, and state the assumptions (as of 2026-03).
> - Lacy bonuses each marketing team on a version of the number: revenue marketing on direct-sourced pipeline, product marketing on pipeline for its SKUs plus adoption metrics tied to expansion, and brand, creative and events on overall pipeline and bookings (as of 2026-03).
