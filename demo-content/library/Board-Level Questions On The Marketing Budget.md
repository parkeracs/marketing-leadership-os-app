> [!summary] Principles
> - Be board level, not ops level: the board judges objectives, budget provenance, efficiency trend, alignment with the CRO, and whether it can trust the CMO to deliver. Prepare for all of it; present only a short custom deck.
> - "Where did the budget come from?" is the first test. A trended budget gets challenged on growth versus revenue; a modelled one gets challenged on who built the model and whether sales, marketing and finance share one model or fight over several.
> - Visible CMO–CRO misalignment, or a CMO caught between CEO and CRO priorities, is itself a board red flag. Settle it before the meeting, not in it.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳
> - Suggested deck of 5–8 slides: current-year performance (metrics left, OKRs right) → next-year OKRs → org chart → top-down S&M analysis (CAC ratio, CAC payback, sales/marketing expense ratio vs history and benchmarks) → marketing budget mix (people/programs/infra, cost per oppty) → pipegen and coverage model by source ("horseman") by quarter → demandgen budget by channel (pipe/spend, cost/oppty) → a menu of 3–5 optional programs with costs and benefits (as of 2024-12)
> - Boards expect a view on AI experiments for marketing efficiency, including AI SDRs (as of 2024-12)
> - Avoid: cheerleading while missing plan, 28-slide decks with big appendices, number-dense "banker slides", recycled QBR slides (as of 2024-12)

## Notes
- Written for budget season: a checklist of roughly twenty questions directors actually hold, then a mapping of those questions onto eight slides.
- Asks whether any marketing money sits outside marketing (CEO pet projects, user conference). Surface it yourself before a director finds it.
- Brand and other "squishy" spend gets defended on two counts: can the CMO justify it, and do the CEO and CRO agree it's a priority. Their visible backing counts as much as your argument.
- Pipeline is framed as shared. Each source owner (marketing, SDRs, sales, partners) should accept a target and have a plan for it, and marketing should own coverage, not just generation.
- The last slide is a deliberate upsell. Sell the ideas that didn't fit the baseline as priced options to pull in extra money. Works when the baseline is already credible. Fails when it isn't.
