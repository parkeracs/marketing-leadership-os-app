> [!summary] Principles
> - Disagreement about what to position against is the root of most weak positioning. Each function is biased: marketing by rivals' visible spend, product by 'horizon competitors', sales by the one big lost 'whale' deal.
> - Fix it by asking what a prospect would do if you didn't exist, selling what's on the truck today, and never forgetting the status quo.
> - Product pessimism (a team convinced its product is an undifferentiated loser despite wins) blinds companies to their strengths. Causes include a best-fit definition so broad that every loss looks like a product gap, long hypothetical competitor lists, and dismissing sales' reasons for wins.
> - Differentiated value is the step teams struggle with most: assuming buyers understand an unusual feature, stopping before value the buyer recognises, going so abstract the differentiation disappears, or listing ten themes nobody remembers.
> - Decide what you're positioning (product, company or suite) from your GTM motion before the workshop.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-03
> - April Dunford (positioning consultant to about 300 companies over 10 years) says B2B vendors typically lose about half of their opportunities to the status quo, and she has seen companies lose more than 80% that way. A reminder to position against "do nothing" as well as named rivals.
> - Citing Matt Dixon's JOLT research, April Dunford says about half of B2B status-quo losses come from buyer indecision rather than a real preference for the current solution.
> - April Dunford holds that B2B has only two pure points of value, making money and saving money, with reducing risk sometimes added as a third (insurance). Useful as a test of whether a value statement goes far enough.
> - In April Dunford's positioning workshops, product teams often list twice as many competitors as sales teams do, a sign of product's bias towards "horizon competitors" that never appear in deals.
