> [!summary] Principles
> - **Pipeline has four sources:** marketing/inbound, SDR/outbound, alliances (partners who refer, not resell) and sales/outbound. PLG companies add a fifth, product. Every source gets its own generation target in the plan.
> - **Set source goals as opportunity counts, not percentages.** A percentage target lets marketing celebrate beating its share while the company is short of the absolute number it needed.
> - **Over-allocate when a new source is spinning up.** Don't cut marketing's target by the share you hope a new alliances or SDR team will deliver. Keep marketing funded for the higher number, so the plan is covered if the new source flames out (plan to about 110% of need).
> - **The mix serves a goal:** cost-effective opportunity generation with broad market reach, ultimately visible in the CAC ratio. Don't optimise the mix for its own sake.
> - **Attribution is unanswerable; manage around it.** Use one opportunity, one source. Credit marketing for leads from marketing programmes even at partner events, and track partner influence separately, so you don't disincentivise the spend.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2021-10
> - Dave Kellogg (enterprise software CEO and board member) suggests a reasonable pipeline source mix for an enterprise SaaS company of about $50M ARR, each within ±10 points: marketing 60%, SDR outbound 10%, alliances 20% and sales outbound 10%. He has seen marketing's share anywhere from about 0% to 95%.
> - Kellogg says SDR outbound and sales outbound make most sense in named-account territory models, and that alliances usually delivers under 20% of pipeline because the CEO never gives it a concrete target.
