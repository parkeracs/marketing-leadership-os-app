> [!summary] Principles
> - **Message testing is qualitative, so it runs on saturation, not statistical significance.** Around 15 target buyers surface the same themes as 150. Spend the budget on more rounds of testing, not bigger samples.
> - **Ask open-ended questions,** and judge a study by whether the insights help you decide what to do next, not by its sample size.
> - **Don't review messaging internally as a substitute.** The team already knows what the product does, and committee approvals wash out the point of view. Test with ICP buyers who don't know you.
> - **On the homepage, get to the point.** Lead with the most powerful message, then the second and third, each covering clarity, relevance, value and differentiation. Use written words, not a video few will watch.
> - **In B2B, "what" before "why".** Wynter's test data suggests buyers don't care about your purpose until it's crystal clear what you do. Specifics (how it works, screenshots) beat generic outcome claims every competitor makes.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2025-09
> - Peep Laja (founder of the B2B message-testing firm Wynter) uses about 15 target-buyer respondents per qualitative message test.
> - Laja cites a company that lifted visit-to-demo conversion by 40% after testing its messaging with 15 target buyers (anecdote, claimed).
> - Laja says average clarity and resonance scores across Wynter tests with VP- and C-level buyers sit around 3.5 out of 5 (GoodContent podcast). A baseline for reading your own test scores.
> - Laja says fewer than 10% of homepage visitors watch an explainer video (as of 2024-10).
> - Wynter says its message-test results come back in about 48 hours from a verified B2B buyer panel (vendor claim, as of 2026).
