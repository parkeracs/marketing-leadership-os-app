> [!summary] Principles
> - **Fix in order, and gate on fit.** Business fundamentals (product/market fit, a shared ICP, win/loss, a tier 1 list, customer research, a stop-doing audit), then systems and data, then capturing every hand-raiser, and only then creating demand. Demand gen amplifies whatever already exists: if fit or ICP can't be confirmed, pause capture and creation. In creation, ship one programme, optimise it until it runs well, then stack the next.
> - **Diagnose one constraint before acting.** A funnel audit should name the bottleneck in one sentence with one focus metric. Six diagnoses (from Affect's funnel audit): *Lead Gen 101* (activity, not pipeline), *Marketing / Sales Misalignment* (demand exists, handoff broken), *Product / Market Fit Issue* (qualifies but won't close), *Unrealistic Goals* (healthy funnel, unbacked target), *Efficient Funnel, Expensive Wins* (unit economics) and *Healthy Funnel* (a volume game). Read the levers in a fixed order: demo → SQO rate, demo quality and volume, win rate, ACV, paid spend, CAC payback.
> - **Measure in two layers.** A system of record (required source field, agreed stage definitions, CRM reports on pipeline, win rate, cycle length and tier 1 progress) plus triangulation (self-reported attribution, call recordings, quarterly macro patterns against spend). Then sort every programme into scale (fixes the constraint), continue (reaches the ICP, gets no new money) or stop.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-10
> - Kuehnle's product/market fit checks before scaling demand spend: cohort retention split by ICP and non-ICP; win rate and cycle better inside the ICP; 40% or more of real users "very disappointed" without the product (the Sean Ellis test); and the last 10–20 wins describable in the same terms (pain, trigger, buyer, outcome).
> - ICP alignment test: sales, marketing and CS each mark fit or no-fit on the same list of about 25 accounts, and the differences are the finding. Build an explicit anti-ICP alongside it.
> - Win/loss on the last 20–30 closed deals, split into product and offer reasons and AE or vertical discrepancies. A tier 1 list of 25–250 named accounts, tagged in the CRM and tracked through stages from unaware to advocate.
> - For a handoff problem (good demos not becoming SQOs), Kuehnle's fixes are booking from the form confirmation, a shared SQO definition and a lost-demo review of the last 20 demos; he expects demo → SQO to move within 30–60 days.
> - For an efficient funnel with expensive wins, Kuehnle suggests modelling a 20–30% ACV increase on CAC payback, holding paid spend flat or cutting it 10–20% while watching demo volume, and cutting channels whose cost per SQO runs well above the blended average. For a lead-gen-heavy funnel, move budget from cost-per-lead to cost-per-SQO optimisation and ungate content.
> - Demand capture: put a scheduling tool (Calendly, Chili Piper) in the form's confirmation flow so hand-raisers book instantly; where that isn't possible, a response SLA under one hour, measured in the CRM with a named owner.
> - Systems hygiene: SPF, DKIM and DMARC on every sending domain and tool; a documented owner and sync direction for each CRM–MAP field; duplicate rules with a named weekly reviewer; source as a required picklist.
> - Self-reported attribution: a free-text "How did you hear about us?" on every form, stored separately from source and asked again on the first call; a quarterly review of branded search, direct traffic, referral share and tier 1 engagement against spend and content shipped.
> - Creation programmes, in order: answer-the-question content distributed to the tier 1 list, customers and executive social; one flagship asset, gated only if data shows gating produces qualified pipeline; a monthly webinar repurposed into a playbook, blog posts, long video and short clips; a monthly CEO letter; customer meetings; small events; paid social behind content already working organically.

A good starter pack / checklist for Demand Gen leaders to get right before trying to scale budgets/headcount.
