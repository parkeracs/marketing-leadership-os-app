> [!summary] Principles
> - **Know which question you're answering.** An efficiency question ("how efficient is marketing?") is best answered with the **marketing CAC ratio** (last quarter's marketing expense ÷ this quarter's new ARR), which is standard and benchmarkable. A cost question ("what would 50 more opportunities cost?") needs the **incremental** cost.
> - **Incremental cost = demand-gen programme cost per opportunity**, not total marketing cost per opportunity. More opportunities don't need more marketers, a bigger PR retainer or more tooling.
> - **Always have the answer in your pocket.** A CMO should know instantly what it costs to fill an opportunity shortfall, and have a ready spending plan for surprise money mid-quarter. Doing the maths in advance is what makes marketing agile.
> - **Speak precisely and footnote definitions** ("demand-gen cost per opportunity") so no one accuses you of understating cost.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2025-03
> - Dave Kellogg's illustrative example: a $9M marketing budget split 45/45/10 between people, programmes and tech that produces 1,000 opportunities costs $9,000 per opportunity in total, but only $3,250 per opportunity in demand-gen cost. The lower figure is the one to use when pricing extra opportunities.
> - Until a team has a real incremental spending plan, Kellogg suggests uplifting the average demand-gen cost per opportunity by about 25%, because the cheapest "low-hanging fruit" opportunities have already been captured.
