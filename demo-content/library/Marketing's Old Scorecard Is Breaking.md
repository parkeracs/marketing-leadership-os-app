> [!summary] Principles
> - **Sales and marketing share one number.** If sales misses, marketing missed too: not because marketing owns the close, but because perception, messaging and brand upstream of the deal are marketing's work. The plan should hold one revenue view, not two separate scorecards.
> - **Measure marketing on pipeline that closes and on accounts it influenced, tracked at account level over time.** Sourced-only counting undervalues deals marketing touched, sped up or kept alive. A point-in-time volume number hides how marketing moves deals.
> - **Partnership is an operating model, not a stated value:** shared metrics, shared pipeline reviews and shared accountability when the number goes the wrong way.
> - **Be willing to retire metrics that feel productive** (MQLs delivered, cost per lead, linear MQL-to-pipeline conversion) once they stop predicting outcomes.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳
> - Suggests replacing linear MQL-to-pipeline metrics with a predictive "4D pipeline" view built from buying signals, multi-threaded engagement, call-intelligence sentiment and customer health data (as of 2026-08). The author is CMO of a revenue-intelligence vendor, so treat this as a direction, not a standard.
> - AI-assisted ideation is cited as saving about 11 hours per marketer per week (as of 2026-08, source not given).
> - Content velocity and "speed from signal to market" proposed as marketing KPIs (as of 2026-08).

## Notes
- Written by a CMO, published as a Topline editorial. It's the marketing side of the shared-number argument, so pair it with a CRO voice before treating it as the CRO view.

- Adds to Kellogg's "settle differences before the board": the thing to settle is the shared definition and target, not just the plan.
