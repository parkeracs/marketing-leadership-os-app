> [!summary] Principles
> - Positioning built by marketing alone gets rejected by sales, product and the CEO. Build it with the people who must use it, both for buy-in and because each holds unique input.
> - Each function brings something only it knows: sales knows who actually shows up in deals, product knows the differentiated capabilities nobody is marketing, the founder knows the original point of view.
> - Invite any executive with big opinions, even HR, legal or the COO. It's far easier to surface disagreement in the room than to defend the positioning to them afterwards.
> - Before the workshop, agree which current customers are obvious bad fits, so the positioning isn't stretched to cover everyone who ever paid.
> - Align on vocabulary first (what 'competitive alternative', 'value', 'category' mean) to avoid costly confusion mid-session.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-02
> - April Dunford's recommended core room for a positioning workshop: marketing, sales (often several sales voices across regions or segments, plus experienced AEs), product (including owners of acquired products), customer success and the CEO or founder.
> - Dunford puts the ideal positioning-workshop size at about a dozen people, 15 at most; beyond 15 it becomes hard to facilitate.
> - Dunford says observers, such as the content and enablement people who will do the downstream work, are fine in the workshop if they know their role is mainly to watch.
