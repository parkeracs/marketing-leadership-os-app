> [!summary] Principles
> - A budget is trended "buckets of money", made and owned by finance. A plan starts from market position and strategy, sets concrete objectives, and costs each one with linked detail.
> - The cutting test: budgets get cut across the board, plans get cut by removing a strategic objective and everything that depends on it.
> - Plans push budget ownership down to every people manager and challenge past spend with a stop/continue/start review, rather than trending last year forward.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳
> - Telltale sign: placeholder hires ("TBH1, TBH2") in a budget versus named, located roles in a plan (as of 2014-01)

## Notes
- Planning asks "what must we achieve in X, and what will it cost?" Budgeting asks "how would we spend more on X?"
- Plans triangulate several models with sensitivity analysis, and anticipate knock-on needs. New sales teams will want leads, a new vertical needs a sales kit, a seasonally weak quarter will be weak again.
- Revenue targets should be negotiated and judged partly against external measures such as market share, not just beating the number, which rewards sandbagging.
- Budgets aren't bad. They're needed for accountability and cash forecasting. They just aren't strategy.
- For a CMO: structure the marketing plan as costed objectives, so any cut becomes a visible choice about which objective to drop, not a silent percentage haircut. It also gives the team ownership, since each manager holds their own line.
