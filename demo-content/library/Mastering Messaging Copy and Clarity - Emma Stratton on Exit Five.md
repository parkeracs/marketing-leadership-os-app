> [!summary] Principles
> - **Positioning is the strategic decision; messaging is its first appearance in words.** Positioning decides what the product is, who it's for and the unique value. Messaging is the handful of core sentences that bring that to life and direct all later copy.
> - **Don't load differentiation onto one sentence.** Differentiation comes from product, customer, experience and content too. Expecting a homepage value proposition to differentiate on its own is an unreasonable demand on a line of copy.
> - **The real blocker is refusing to narrow.** Teams water messaging down to cover personas they don't sell to yet, or products five years out, and end up generic. A clear choice of audience makes the message distinctive.
> - **Kill abstraction.** Abstract words (anything that doesn't conjure the same picture for everyone, e.g. "strategic solution") make B2B copy vague. Concrete language is the fix.
> - **Run customer and prospect interviews with a messaging lens,** not a case-study lens: triggers for looking, the challenges, value in their own words. Their words shape the message hierarchy and give you the language.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2025-04
> - Emma Stratton (founder of Punchy and author of Make It Punchy), on the Exit Five podcast, says differentiation in B2B messaging has become markedly harder since she started in 2016, because markets are more crowded and company websites copy each other.
