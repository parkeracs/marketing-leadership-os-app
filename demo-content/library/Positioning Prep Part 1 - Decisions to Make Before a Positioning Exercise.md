> [!summary] Principles
> - Settle four decisions before convening anyone, or you'll call a timeout mid-workshop and redo work: is now the right time, who is the positioning for, what exactly are you positioning, and which persona.
> - Pre-launch you only have a positioning thesis. Keep external positioning deliberately loose, see where the market pulls you, then tighten.
> - The method is for customer positioning only. Investor and employee positioning are different jobs; mixing them produces mushy positioning.
> - Multi-product scope follows the GTM motion: position the lead product for new customers, then position the rest assuming the lead product is owned; or position a suite as one integrated offer.
> - Position for the deal champion, the person who builds the shortlist and recommends to the economic buyer. Co-champions are fine if named.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-01
> - April Dunford (author of Obviously Awesome) says that in 16 in-house product launches she never got the positioning exactly right before launch, which is why she treats pre-launch positioning as a thesis.
> - The second edition of Dunford's Obviously Awesome (February 2026) restructures her positioning process into prep work, five core steps matching the five positioning components, and post-work.
