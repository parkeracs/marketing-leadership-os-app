> [!summary] Principles
> - A pipeline scrub is a discrete, scheduled event with a definition of "clean", not a continuous activity. "We're always scrubbing" signals there is no process and no accountability.
> - Scrubs and forecast calls are different meetings: the forecast call looks at this-quarter deals expected to close, so low-probability and out-quarter opportunities never get inspected there.
> - Scrub against a written rulebook, the **Pipeline Management Rules (PMR)**, covering four fields: close date, stage, forecast category, value.
> - Once sales has accepted an opportunity (stage 2 / SAL), "those weren't real opportunities" is not an available excuse.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳
> - Scrubs run in weeks 2, 5 and 8; scrubbed pipeline is presented Monday of weeks 3, 6 and 9; week-3 snapshot is the basis for early-quarter analytics (as of 2018-05)
> - Rough cost of pipeline a first-line enterprise manager oversees: ~$50K per rep, $300–400K for a team of 6–8, assuming ~$2.5K per opportunity (as of 2018-05)

## Notes
- Opens with a CEO/sales VP dialogue where pipeline drops week after week "because we cleaned it", ending in a forecast cut. Diagnosis: no definition of a scrubbed pipeline, no process, no ownership of quality.
- A scrub reviews **all** opportunities for every rep, this quarter and out-quarters, checking each against the PMR. Managers should be able to ask a rep "what's the rule for close date in the PMR?"
- Timing matters because scrubs move coverage ratios, conversion rates and weighted forecasts. Fixed scrub weeks keep the analytics comparable quarter to quarter.
- In-flight clean-up between scrubs is fine as a supplement, never a substitute.
- Useful for marketing: a fixed, scrubbed week-3 snapshot is what makes marketing's pipeline contribution and conversion numbers defensible in disputes with sales.
- Kellogg's later "Ten-Point Sales Management Framework" (2021) repeats this as "thrice-quarterly pipeline scrubs", run in small regional groups with each rep in turn.
