> [!summary] Principles
> - **Written to CEOs, but it's the new CMO's request list.** If the CEO doesn't offer these things, ask for them.
> - **Visible CEO endorsement before you arrive** (to the board, the C-suite, the marketing leads and the first all-hands) gives the CMO cover for unpopular decisions later.
> - **Fast, deep customer exposure:** shadow the CEO's prospect and customer meetings, run a listening tour with the most successful customers, and get win-loss recordings.
> - **More CEO time at first, not less.** Senior hires still need context and filtering early on; build trust in person.
> - **Make expectations explicit:** update the quarter and year OKRs with the CMO's new insights, and agree a 30/60/90 plan of activities, decisions and deliverables.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2024-04
> - Carilu Dietrich (writing to CEOs) lists ten documents a new CMO should receive: the last 4 board decks; the last fundraising deck; last year's company and marketing OKRs and scoring; this year's OKRs; the financial plan with finance and sales assumptions (attainment, close rates, product splits) for last year and this; the marketing budget summary for both years; company dashboards; marketing dashboards; past QBRs; and any market research. A new CMO can use it as a request list.
> - Dietrich recommends the CEO and a new CMO meet twice a week for the first few months.
> - Dietrich expects clear marketing dashboards to be in place within a new CMO's first 2 months.
> - At a new CMO's first board meeting, Dietrich says the likely role is an assessment of the market, company and team, plus priorities, key metrics, goals, sequencing and asks.
> - Dietrich suggests a working-styles session (DISC, Enneagram, Myers-Briggs) at a new CMO's first C-suite offsite.

## Notes
- Ten CEO-side recommendations: lay the foundation, customers, reference material, time together, product and company strategy, update OKRs, 30/60/90 plan, dashboards, first board meeting, culture and "unmentionables", C-suite offsite.
- "Culture and unmentionables": the CEO should brief the CMO on the conflicts under existing relationships that could derail them. Ask for this directly.
- The CMO-side list is only sketched: meet leaders and movers, inspect the numbers, run your own team offsite to refine OKRs, 30/60/90 goals, budget and vision, present at the first all-hands and board, and find and promote quick wins ("market marketing").
