> [!summary] Principles
> - A CRO asking for more coverage than the norm is a symptom to diagnose, not a target to accept. Six diagnoses: the pipeline isn't scrubbed; you lose too much; the pipeline is low quality; sales is more powerful than marketing; there's no nurture program; reps are squatting.
> - Fix by cause: unscrubbed → scrub all quarters; losing → sales training, messaging, win/loss, competitive work; low quality → revisit ICP and targeting; no nurture → move "maybe later" prospects out of the opportunity system and into nurture.
> - The **floating bar**: acceptance standards drift with how full a rep's pipeline is. Starving reps accept junk. Written definitions are the only partial control.
> - Precision in language: say "week-3 current-quarter pipeline coverage", not just "coverage". Prefer current-quarter over annual or rolling pipeline.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳
> - Coverage norm 3x, i.e. ~33% week-3 conversion; CROs increasingly asking for 3.5–5x (as of 2021-03)
> - Rough math: a third slips or derails, win half of the rest, convert a third (as of 2021-03)
> - Expansion pipeline can convert near or above 100% because reps enter deals late (as of 2021-03)

## Notes
- Directly useful for the "marketing leads are bad / we need more pipeline" dispute: walk the six diagnoses before agreeing to a higher marketing pipeline target.
- "Rolling hairballs": out-quarter fake opportunities used as nurture reminders. Squatting: fake opportunities to keep an account through a territory change.
- Definitions: slip = close date moves; derail = project cancelled or pushed outside the valid window; missing the short list is a loss, not a derail.
- Segment coverage (new logo vs expansion) when mix or conversion by segment is unstable.
