> [!summary] Principles
> - **Hand-raisers only is disciplined but too passive and too late.** By the time a buyer asks for a demo, the shortlist is usually set; waiting for hand-raisers concedes the first-mover advantage.
> - **Replace the binary MQL with three tiers, each its own contract with sales:** (1) hand-raisers — act now, no scoring needed; (2) MQX — signal evidence of buying activity at an ICP account (pricing visits, competitor comparisons, several people engaging); (3) MEX — the right people at the right accounts engaging with content but showing no buying signals. Each tier has its own definition, expected conversion and play.
> - **The X matters:** for most B2B deals the qualified unit should be an account or buying group, not a lead.
> - **Don't pitch tier 3.** Lead with insight about a problem they may not have prioritised, to create demand among the 95% not in market.
> - **The tiers are leading indicators, not the scoreboard.** Measure marketing on total pipeline created, total revenue and pipeline per marketing dollar.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-04
> - Jon Miller (co-founder of Marketo and Engagio, former CMO of Demandbase) cites 6sense research that 94% of B2B buyers rank their shortlist before talking to sellers, and 95% buy from their day-one shortlist. This is his case for engaging accounts before they raise a hand.
> - Jon Miller also cites the *Solution Selling* claim by Mike Bosworth that sellers win 90% of the time when they engage buyers while their pain is still latent (old and unverified).
