> [!summary] Principles
> - At scale the marketing job becomes a portfolio of bets from a fixed budget, against a clock: short-term (digital, growth, SMB), medium-term (enterprise, field) and momentum (brand, PR) bets running together.
> - There are two ways to fail on spend. Underspending (cutting everything that isn't working fast) leaves no pipeline; overspending (redeploying your last company's playbook quickly) burns budget before it's proven.
> - The CEO's counterweight runs against the CMO's instinct. Push a cautious CMO to take more risk at lower ROI. Make a fast spender justify the budget against clear goals and force-rank, holding them only to aggregate results.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳
> - Many B2B companies spend 10–30% of new bookings on marketing, more if well funded; e.g. +$30M new bookings implies a $3–10M marketing budget (as of 2025-04)
> - Heads of marketing typically get about a year to build pipeline; many are fired around months 12–14 (as of 2025-04)
> - Webinars and paid can take 2–10x longer to convert than high-quality inbound (as of 2025-04)
> - Embedded SaaStr stat: 63% of CMOs changed jobs in 16 months vs about half of all roles (as of 2025-04)

## Notes
- Things that fail when small (billboards, big event presence) can work once you have a mini-brand, so last year's "doesn't work" list needs re-testing as you scale.
- 1x ROI is acceptable while scaling: it buys customers you wouldn't otherwise get. This is a direct counter to "every bet needs a business case at high ROI".
- CEOs probably have to be the marketer for free and smallest customers, because most CMOs naturally steer spend toward bigger deals.
- You get what the CMO enjoys: ask candidates (and yourself) for the top three things you like doing in marketing. The plan will drift toward them.
- Brand works once you have a mini-brand, but the CMO still has to show the idea has a shot at this company.
- US venture-scale framing; the bookings ratio needs flagging before use in bootstrapped contexts.
