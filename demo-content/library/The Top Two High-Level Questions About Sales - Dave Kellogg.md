> [!summary] Principles
> - **Two questions run the whole pipeline conversation:** did we give sales a fair chance at the number (starting pipeline coverage), and did they convert it (pipeline conversion)?
> - **Coverage is a joint product of four sources; conversion belongs to sales.** Unless the CEO delegates it, nobody owns overall coverage. Kellogg wants the CMO named "quarterback of the pipeline": hit marketing's own target *and* forecast day-one next-quarter pipeline across all sources, then act early.
> - **Set quarterly pipegen goals by opportunity count, not dollars.** Early-stage dollars are a gameable proxy.
> - **Avoid rolling-four-quarter pipeline.** There's no rolling target, and it hides "always two quarters out" deals. Watch the opportunity histogram by rep too: enough total pipeline can hide feast and famine across sellers.
> - **Coverage goals on day one; analytics off a week-3 snapshot.** Improve conversion via higher-quality pipeline (more ICP, intent data), making the product easier to sell, a better seller hiring profile, and keeping the scrub, forecast call and deal review separate.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2021-10
> - Dave Kellogg (enterprise software CEO and board member) says 3.0x starting pipeline coverage is a reasonable target until a company has its own conversion history. The method is superseded by [[Target Pipeline Coverage is Not the Inverse of Win Rate]] (2024-06).
> - Kellogg defines week-3 conversion as the quarter's new ARR divided by current-quarter pipeline in week 3; for example, $2.7M ÷ $10M = 27%. A 3x coverage target implies an expected conversion rate of about 33%.
