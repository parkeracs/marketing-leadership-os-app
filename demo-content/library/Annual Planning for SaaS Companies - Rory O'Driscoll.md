> [!summary] Principles
> - **The board member's real question:** what do I have to believe will stay the same, or get better, for this plan to work? Make that easy to answer.
> - **Reduce the plan to about ten key assumptions,** each shown against the two prior years' actuals. Use the same KPIs the operating teams run the business on.
> - **Start with two or three business goals, agreed with the CEO.** If the CEO and CFO can't state them, the process is doomed.
> - **Show simple funnel math from top of funnel to closed deals, agreed by sales and marketing.** A board member often spots a gap between what marketing expects and what sales needs. Sanity-check programme growth against new-sales growth.
> - **Plan a mid-year check-in.** Companies front-load S&M spend and then taper to hit breakeven, and the cost of under-investing shows up next year. A mid-year check-in lets the board green-light more investment if the plan is on track, or hold to the plan if it isn't.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2017-11
> - Rory O'Driscoll (partner at Scale Venture Partners, writing as a SaaS board member) recommends an annual plan board deck of 5–10 slides: assumptions, the revenue build-up and headcount additions, then quarterly financials and a multi-year P&L, with everything else in the appendix.
> - Rory O'Driscoll advises calling out new go-to-market initiatives, such as international expansion or a channel programme, separately from the core business, each with its own success measure.
