> [!summary] Principles
> - **Own your onboarding.** No one is as invested in your success; the more senior you are, the sooner you're expected to contribute. Work out what information, access and decisions you need, and an easy win for month one.
> - **Share hunches, not just questions.** Beginner's mind is a one-time asset. Write down what you agree and disagree with, and turn notes (a customer call, a doc) into analysis and a forward-looking recommendation.
> - **Read with an active stance.** Ask how you'll use each document, which data you'll check weekly, and what patterns are emerging.
> - **Assume you'll be asked to recommend soon:** biggest gaps, start/stop/continue, priorities given finite resources, and the sequence.
> - **Assume you'll lead the meeting.** Behave as the driver, not the fly on the wall.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2023-10
> - Wes Kao (co-founder of Maven, writing on management and communication) lists seven moves for onboarding yourself into a new team: take charge, develop a point of view, review work actively, pick up the unspoken rules, gather what you need to make recommendations, identify which decisions you'll own, and assume you'll lead.
