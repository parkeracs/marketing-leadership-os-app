> [!summary] Principles
> - **Assess four areas:** people (right people, right seats), processes (clear ways to get work done), strategy (is it the right work), operations (budgets, performance tracking, data flows).
> - **Month 1 is the information download.** Get access to every tool, folder and "marketing graveyard" (old personas and decks). Approach it with healthy scepticism. Learn the company's own definitions of "trial" and "conversion", both the business meaning and how the systems implement it.
> - **Write a "CMO's mandate" document in weeks 3–4:** your areas of focus, how to work with you, what people can expect from you, and which areas you'll investigate. A written document over a deck, plus a Loom walkthrough. Alongside it, a strategy covering only the next two quarters, so assumptions get tested before long commitments.
> - **Get financial alignment with the CEO/CFO before tactics.** Keep forecasting (where past performance projects), budgeting (what cash allows) and planning (backing into the target) apart. They're different exercises. The CMO and CFO need to be close.
> - **Month 3: "get executors executing."** One hub as the source of truth for marketing work, a weekly team meeting so updates aren't repeated in every 1:1, and only then deeper cross-functional work with product and sales.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2025-06
> - Asia Orangio (DemandMaven founder, working as a fractional CMO at an early-stage company) found that agreeing budget and goals with a CEO who also acted as CFO took two to three weeks of iteration.
> - In month two of that engagement, Asia Orangio's resourcing moves were hiring a paid-ads agency and making a hire to own SEO and content.
> - In hindsight, Asia Orangio would have done the financial planning earlier and closed visible resource gaps faster.

## Notes
- Fractional engagement at an early-stage company, not a full-time VP/CMO at scale. The sequence carries over, but adjust the pace for a larger team.
- Episode page has a full written summary. Orangio founded DemandMaven (a growth consultancy).
- The "mandate document" is the most reusable artefact here. It plays the role Papoulias gives her published 90-day plan in [[The First 90 Days - Gier, Leger and Papoulias on Renegade Marketing]].
- Her closing point: you won't catch everything in 90 days. Address the mission-critical issues first.
