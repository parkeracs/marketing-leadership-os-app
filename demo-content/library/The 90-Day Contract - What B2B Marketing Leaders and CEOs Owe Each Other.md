> [!summary] Principles
> - **Make the unspoken CEO–marketing-leader contract explicit.** Most early failures come from expectations neither side said out loud.
> - **What the marketing leader owes by day 90 (five things):** a real relationship with sales (live calls, not a kickoff); a sharper narrative, not a rebrand; a pipeline model you built yourself; and one bet in motion that didn't exist before you arrived.
> - **Build your own pipeline model.** Know conversion by segment, where deals stall, which sources actually influence pipeline and what marketing can realistically move. An inherited model tells you what someone else believed; if you can't defend the maths, you don't own it yet.
> - **The first bet must come from inside the business,** be small enough to test and have a clear signal. Importing the last company's playbook is the reckless version.
> - **What the CEO owes:** access from day one (sales targets, pipeline data, customers, sales calls); transparency about what's broken, what was tried and what's untouchable (a tagline, a channel the board loves); a real budget conversation by day 30; and patience. Checking a B2B bet every two weeks kills it.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-10
> - Kristen Pecka (B2B marketing leader) advises a new marketing leader to pull six months of sales-call transcripts and run them through AI to find where the company's story breaks down with buyers.
