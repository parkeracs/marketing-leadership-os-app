> [!summary] Principles
> - Find the **request behind the request**. Asking why usually shows the real problem, and sometimes shows the original ask needn't be done at all.
> - Requests come from the requester's own pressures. Understanding what they care about makes urgent asks easier to reshape, and less frustrating.
> - Set goals *and non-goals* in advance from the Marketing Decision Dashboard, and invite other teams' leaders to contribute during planning, so ad hoc work drops later.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2025-02
> - MKT1's Marketing Decision Dashboard, used to set goals and non-goals before ad hoc requests arrive, covers marketing advantages, stack-ranked ICPs, key storylines written as "Perceptions", and revenue levers and funnel focus.
> - MKT1 recommends a structured intake form for day-to-day requests, balancing enough detail to assess the request against not making it so onerous that people avoid it (Jenny Thai). State the review cadence on the form, keep a running list, and review it on a fixed rhythm so requests don't vanish into "an abyss".
> - Emily Kramer routes requests straight into her project tool (Asana), prioritises them weekly, and tags the requester in a comment saying why it was or wasn't prioritised and when it will be done, optionally piped to Slack.
> - MKT1's template library includes an example marketing requests form (Typeform) that connects to a project tool and Slack, and suggests adding a short GACCS brief to the form.

## Notes
- Episode 1 of the Dear Marketers podcast, with Grace Erickson, Jenny Thai, Devon Watts and guest Sandy Mangat. The team quote on asking why comes from Jenny Thai.
- Match the goal horizon to the company. If the business can't see nine months ahead, set near-term priorities and adjust, rather than forcing an annual plan.
- Bring stakeholders in at two points: planning (contributing to goals) and project kickoff (aligning on the brief). Leaving them out of either invites requests later.
- Non-goals are the explicit "we're not doing this" list. They are what makes saying no defensible.
