> [!summary] Principles
> - **Buy-in is not binary.** An approval at kick-off decays; on long-horizon work you have to re-earn it continuously or lose support midway.
> - **Beginning / middle / end.** Get buy-in for the strategy and its trade-offs up front; in the middle, re-sell the strategy and re-state the accepted trade-offs and how you're mitigating them; at the end, put narrative on the numbers so the audience knows what "good" looks like.
> - Flagging a risk once does not protect you: leaders are busy and quietly hope the downside won't happen, so they're surprised when it does. Repetition is the operator's job, not a sign the leader failed.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳
> - Suggested mechanism: a standing line in the regular (e.g. bi-weekly) update reminding leaders of a risk already accepted and how it's built into the forecast (as of 2025-03)

## Notes
- Built around a coaching case: a PM won a CEO's agreement to a launch with an explicit cannibalisation risk; months later the CEO reacted as if the risk was news and questioned the whole decision, so a launch that hit its targets still read as a disappointment.
- Most operators sell at the start, some recap at the end, very few re-sell in the middle, which is where most of a project's life actually sits.
- "Narrative on numbers": don't expect metrics to speak for themselves; say what they mean against the expectation you set.
- Applies managing up, down and laterally, including to your own team.
- Works when the trade-off was genuinely agreed; it won't rescue a plan whose risks were never surfaced in the first place.
