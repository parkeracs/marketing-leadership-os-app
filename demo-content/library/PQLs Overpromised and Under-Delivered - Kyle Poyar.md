> [!summary] Principles
> - **True PQLs are a small fraction of pipeline at mature hybrid companies.** Product users usually aren't the buyers, and adding a sales touch to them doesn't change the money much.
> - **Orient on the account, not the lead.** Combine firmographics, product usage, website activity, marketing and field engagement and third-party data to prioritise accounts (PQAs).
> - **Navigate to the economic buyer.** Marketing, sales and product should all work towards the person who signs, using users for intel and introductions.
> - **Put sales time where it's incremental:** where a human generates value beyond what self-serve would have closed anyway. Working only PQLs means working deals that might have converted alone.
> - **Use value-selling to create new pipeline,** not just to close what the product already created.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2024-05
> - Kyle Poyar (ex-OpenView, author of Growth Unhinged) gives no numbers in this LinkedIn post. His claim that product-qualified leads (PQLs) are a "small fraction" of pipeline at mature hybrid product-led and sales-led companies comes from his conversations with revenue leaders.

## Notes
- Read in full. Poyar (ex-OpenView, Growth Unhinged) was one of the people who popularised PQLs; this is his public correction.
- Companion post (2023-11): PQAs beat PQLs because the user is rarely the economic buyer, companies often have several sub-accounts on one domain (a consolidation play), and fit matters as much as usage. He suggests an outbound SDR role that works PQAs to reach the decision maker.
- Growth Unhinged "PLG and sales in 2023" (2023-06) gives his five steps: start from the customer journey, **validate that sales is incremental**, pick product signals, define sales plays, then comp and ops.
- Contradicts older benchmark claims that PQLs convert at 15–30% and should be the default sales lead. Both can be true: PQLs convert well but are low-ACV and low-incrementality.
