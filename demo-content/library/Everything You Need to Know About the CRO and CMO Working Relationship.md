> [!summary] Principles
> - **A friendly relationship is not a partnership.** A CMO can feel the CRO "has their back" while being steered under the bus: the problem is reduced to "top of funnel" and the conversion problem quietly drops out of the story.
> - **Five tells you're under the bus:** the CEO calls it a top-of-funnel problem; the CRO spends more time with the CEO; close rate is measured from a late stage (post-demo) instead of the sales-accepted stage; coverage targets keep rising; the marketing budget is flat while sales grows, so all the CAC efficiency lands on marketing.
> - **Real collaboration means admitting weakness to each other, helping regardless of who'd be blamed, and holding each other accountable** (the CMO listens to discovery calls; the CRO attends the webinar).
> - **Go to the CEO with one joint message:** shared root cause, what each function will focus on, and which conversion rates (MQL→stage 2, stage 2→close) you'll both track.
> - **The CEO's job is a safe space, not a firing squad.** If both leaders were hired well, a replacement is unlikely to do better; replacing them costs a whole executive cycle.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2022-07
> - Dave Kellogg (enterprise software executive and board member) puts a typical enterprise software close rate on sales-accepted (stage 2) opportunities at 15–25%. A sanity check for pipeline coverage maths.
> - Dave Kellogg warns against close rates calculated from a "demo" stage or stage 4: he says demo is not a stage, and measuring from it shifts responsibility for conversion onto marketing.
