> [!summary] Principles
> - **Priority misalignment with the CEO is the top reason VPs lose the job.** For marketing leaders it usually means wanting to run the last company's playbook.
> - **Write a detailed 60-day plan before day one** and get the CEO to agree to it. Cover what you'd do first, who you'd hire, which consultants you'd use, what you'd fix and what you would *not* change.
> - **Page one is a force-ranked top five.** Go through it with the CEO until you're on the same page. Lemkin suggests both of you sign off on it.
> - **The CEO has already been the marketer.** By even modest revenue the founder has evidence on what works (events, agencies, paid search), so the job is to do it better, not to reinvent it.
> - **The plan forces you to learn the product and company** before you arrive, rather than winging it after.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2024-11
> - Jason Lemkin (founder of SaaStr) says an incoming VP's 60-day plan can be slides, a doc, or a Trello or Asana board; the force-ranked top-five page agreed with the CEO is the part that matters.
> - Jason Lemkin observes that by $10–20K of monthly recurring revenue, a SaaS founder-CEO typically holds firm views on which marketing channels work, so a new marketing leader should build on that evidence rather than reinvent it.

## Notes
- Lemkin's original also uses the plan as a hiring test ("if you can't agree, don't take the job"). That part is out of scope here. After accepting, the move is the same: get the force-ranked top five agreed before or in week one.
- The "what you don't want to change" list is an under-used credibility signal: it shows respect for what already works.
- Companion post: ask any VP for their full wish list; they will only really do the top 3–4 (https://www.saastr.com/ask-every-vp-you-interview-for-a-list-of-everything-they-want-to-do-theyll-only-do-the-top-4-5/, 2023-04).
- Pairs with [[The 1 Reason CMOs Fail]] (align with the CEO's vision; build on what works).
- Fails when: the CEO's view of what works is out of date or wrong. See [[Carilu Dietrich]] on changing plans through modelling and evidence.
