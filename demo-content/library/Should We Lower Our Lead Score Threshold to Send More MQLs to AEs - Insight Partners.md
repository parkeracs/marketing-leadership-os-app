> [!summary] Principles
> - **Run a lead scoring council,** co-owned by sales and marketing, with CS and (for PLG motions) product. Meet at least quarterly and calibrate the model to lifetime value, not MQL volume.
> - **Lowering the threshold is a capacity question, not a quality debate.** Extra lower-quality leads are worth it only if they add bookings after accounting for reps' finite energy: more leads lower the win rate on the good ones.
> - **The test:** feed lower-scoring leads only if the drop in win rate on existing leads is smaller than the new leads' win rate × (new leads ÷ existing leads). Reps are usually energy-limited before they're capacity-limited.
> - **A/B test by territory before going live.** Raise lead flow in one territory, hold another constant, and compare win rate, lead-to-pipeline conversion, cycle length and ACV. Collect rep feedback too.
> - **The lowest-quality leads (content downloads) almost never merit sales time.** Nurture them until they cross the threshold.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2022-10
> - Jeremey Donovan (Insight Partners) works through an example: a sales team getting 50 leads a quarter at a 30% win rate gains from 10 extra lower-scored leads winning at 20% only if the original win rate drops by no more than 4 points. A way to test whether lowering the MQL threshold actually adds revenue.
> - Donovan shows that hiring a dedicated account executive (AE) to work tier-2 leads fails the economics at a 5x quota-to-OTE ratio when entry-level AE on-target earnings start around $120K (US figures).
