> [!summary] Principles
> - **Get finance's guardrails before you model.** Finance plays by rules marketers rarely see: S&M as a % of opex or revenue, marketing as a % of revenue, people vs programme splits, CAC ratios, and an expectation of more efficiency every year. Ask FP&A for them first, so a $10M bottom-up ask doesn't meet a $5M envelope.
> - **Top-down guardrails, then bottom-up plans.** Give each sub-team a budget range, let them build bottom-up inside it, then roll up against the agreed goals. Every campaign must map to a company goal.
> - **Build funnels per motion** (mid-market, enterprise, PLG), then test the assumptions. A free funnel calculator turns cost per MQL or per opportunity into a budget, and shows what a 2-point conversion drop costs.
> - **Map risks and dependencies (impact × likelihood) and share them up front.** It opens the scenario conversation: if the launch slips, money moves to predictable pipeline or goes back to finance.
> - **CAC is a guardrail, not a driver.** Marketing doesn't control all its inputs, so CFOs use it to stress-test a plan, not to build one. Plan on cost per outcome at each funnel stage instead.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2024-11
> - Rowan Tonkin (CMO of Planful, a financial planning software vendor) targets a blended ratio of about $0.12 of marketing spend per $1 of pipeline created, varying by product and region.
> - Tonkin's guardrail for the marketing programme budget: 55 to 75% "strategic and productive" spend tied to goals, a smaller experiments line (the "CMO slush fund"), and the rest non-strategic, such as legal, shipping, print and learning and development.
> - Tonkin says SaaS finance teams often split marketing budget about 65/35 between programmes and people, because headcount is a fixed cost that can't flex.
> - Tonkin observes that since the push for profitability, CFOs have widened programme budgets relative to headcount, favouring contractors and agencies because they can flex.

- Book recommended: *The Next CMO* by Peter Mahoney and Scott Todaro, on operationalising marketing planning and marketing accounting.
- Tonkin's other tips: track budget vs planned vs committed vs actual, on an accrual basis; cut tech first when you inherit a bloated budget.
- **If company goals aren't set when marketing starts planning, draft them yourself and take them to the CEO** [17:41]. Gerhardt adds that the exec team must agree its 3–5 company goals first, or the plan breaks down.
- **To get approval, talk results in financial terms and "tell the whole truth":** label experiments as experiments, and teach finance rather than preach to it [34:01–36:33].
- **For brand or a new market with no data,** agree an experiment with finance: the company goal it serves, leading indicators, what success and failure look like, and when you'd scale or pull back. Finance will then often ring-fence a discrete bucket for it [44:48–47:14].
- Experiments line sized at 10–20% of programme budget in Exit Five's write-up of the session ([8 Steps to Survive Annual Marketing Planning](https://exitfive.com/newsletter/8-steps-to-survive-annual-planning/), as of 2024-12).
