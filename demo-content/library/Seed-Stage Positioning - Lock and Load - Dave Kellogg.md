> [!summary] Principles
> - Stage changes the positioning job. At a larger company in a crowded category, 'why buy mine?' deserves near-perfectionism. At seed stage, the job is the quickest route to simple clarity about what the product is.
> - Think hard but don't agonise: debate, decide, train the team, execute. Park concerns in a doc to revisit in two to four quarters unless material new information arrives.
> - Stop worrying about category creation at seed. Prove product-market fit; guiding analysts to a category name is a problem for two to four years out.
> - Over-polishing early positioning is often avoidance of the real test: people understanding the product and saying no.
> - Early buyers are visionaries who will map the benefits themselves. Explaining business benefits matters later in the market's evolution.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2022-01
> - Dave Kellogg (CEO and board member) advises that once a seed-stage company locks its positioning, it should revisit it after two to four quarters unless material new information arrives.
