> [!summary] Principles
> - **Derive the demand-gen budget from the sales target, not from last year's spend.** Walk up the funnel from the new-ARR target: deals needed (target ÷ ASP), then opportunities, SALs and MQLs at historical conversion rates, then MQLs × cost per MQL = programme budget. Drivers are inputs; everything else is calculated.
> - **Quarterise and phase-shift it.** Leads made now close quarters later, so the model must lag MQL → SAL → close and extend into next year with a growth assumption. Most of this year's MQLs actually support next year's bookings.
> - **One funnel per distinct motion.** New business, upsell, channel, and inside vs field sales each have different deal sizes, speeds and conversion rates. Averaging them starves the small-deal team of opportunities. If a segment's ASP is very different, give it its own funnel; every key sales target owns one.
> - **Use time-based close rates for precision.** Cohort opportunities by quarter created and track when they close. Then the model shows expected closes from existing pipeline, plus the opportunity gap (or surplus) per quarter, so you can pull generation earlier to cover a big Q4.
> - **Show the efficiency proof.** Put last year's actuals and a benchmark column beside the plan, and add a row showing the plan's efficiency improvement. It proves marketing is raising its own bar, and flags which conversion rate is the weak link.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2019-12
> - Dave Kellogg's (CEO and board member) worked example of working back from a bookings target: $2M of new ARR a quarter at a $75K average sale price, with 20% SQL-to-close, marketing sourcing 80% of deals, 10% MQL-to-SAL and $250 per MQL, needs about $333K a quarter of demand-generation programme spend.
> - Kellogg's quick rule for total marketing budget is to annualise programme spend and double it, which assumes the old 50/50 people/programmes split. His modern split is 45/45/10 people/programmes/infrastructure, or 40/40/20 for tech-heavy teams.
> - In Kellogg's example, 0%, 50% and 100% growth in new ARR need $1.09M, $1.47M and $1.85M of demand-generation spend respectively, showing how fast the budget scales with the growth target.
> - Kellogg's "standard range" for the overall close rate of accepted opportunities, measured over six quarters, is roughly 15–22% (as of 2020-01).
