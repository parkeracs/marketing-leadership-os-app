> [!summary] Principles
> - No single metric shows weak positioning: it drags the whole pipeline (low response, slow middle, churn after sale). Listen to first sales calls for 'can you start again?', 'so you're like Salesforce', or 'I could do that in a spreadsheet'.
> - Most positioning problems are misalignment: the founder, marketing, sales and product each tell a slightly different story. Positioning is a team sport, so get them in one room.
> - Workshop output is two things: a positioning document (the input for messaging) and a storyboarded sales narrative that marketing and sales turn into deck, demo and script and use to test it.
> - Before product-market patterns are clear, keep positioning loose and let the market pull you. Tighten once you see the common thread across the customers who love you.
> - B2B segmentation is about account characteristics you can list and target (size, stack, team shape). Personas are about people in the 5–7-person buying group. Don't confuse them.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2022-06
> - April Dunford (positioning consultant) estimates that about 40% of B2B deals are lost to "no decision" rather than to a competitor.
> - Dunford runs her positioning engagements as a week-long sprint with prep before and work after; teams doing it alone often finish in a couple of days.
> - Dunford's typical client is a post-Series A or B startup, often with $5M to $10M revenue.
> - Dunford suggests bringing in an outside positioning facilitator when an opinionated exec team can't agree, when there's a big bet riding on it (for example hiring 10 to 15 reps or a major marketing investment), or when a large company wants a safety net.
