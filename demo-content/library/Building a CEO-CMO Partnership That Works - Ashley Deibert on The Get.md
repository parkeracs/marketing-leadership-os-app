> [!summary] Principles
> - **Facing a big budget cut, bring scenarios, not a list of channels.** Show what each level of cut means (scenario A drops events, scenario B turns off digital), add your recommendation and the reasoning, then let the management team decide.
> - **Align the CRO before you take it up the chain.** She took her cut scenarios to the CRO first, because sales would feel the fewer events and ads. A peer already on side makes the exec decision easy.
> - **When you cut, keep what the data says is gaining ground and what keeps you visible in the market,** and pick channels that are easy to turn back on. In her case: BDR and digital cut; events and customer marketing protected; AI tools covered part of the BDR gap.
> - **Focus, not "more with less".** Align with the CEO, sales and product leaders on their top priorities. Start this in the interview process, and re-check with them when new asks surface.
> - **Name the key stakeholders for a big programme up front,** and route other opinions politely to that group, so feedback doesn't stall delivery.

> - Ashley Deibert (CMO at Marigold, interviewed on The Get podcast, roughly 2025) has run marketing budgets from about $200K to $20M and leads a team of about 60. Context for the scale behind her budget-cut advice.
