> [!summary] Principles
> - Three triggers force a positioning change: your product changes, your competitors change, or the market changes (regulation, economy, crises, big technology shifts). AI is hitting all three at once.
> - A point of view about the future is no longer optional. Confused, risk-averse buyers want vendors to have opinions on where the technology is going and what to do now.
> - Position against the competition that is on prospects' shortlists today, not the noisy future threats on LinkedIn. Ignore future competitors in positioning but never in the roadmap. You can position one way today while building towards another.
> - In periods of upheaval you can hype the future more than usual, but connect it to value today. Tilt too far forward and deals stall; stay too present and you look legacy.
> - Customer signal matters more than ever. Formalise it: customer advisory boards (refresh membership), win/loss analysis (wins are more useful than losses for positioning), and executive account assignments.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-05
> - April Dunford (positioning consultant) says every product she worked on in the previous two years was AI-native, re-architected for AI or facing an existential AI threat; the last comparable scramble she saw was March 2020.
> - Dunford says bolt-on chatbots no longer reassure buyers, so non-AI-native SaaS companies now need a view on how their product works in a world of AI agents.
> - An executive account programme Dunford has seen at larger clients: every VP and above, across all functions, owns a large customer account and calls it monthly. One way to formalise customer signal for positioning.
