> [!summary] Principles
> - **CRM loss reasons and rep opinions are poor inputs to positioning.** Go to the buyers who decided, including those who chose a competitor or did nothing.
> - **Interviews beat surveys for B2B decisions.** They capture the trigger, the hidden criteria, the politics and who actually decided, which a scale question can't.
> - **Neutral interviewers get more candour.** Politeness bias makes buyers soften criticism to the rep or PM who sold to them. Use a third party, or at least someone with no stake in the deal.
> - **Treat win-loss as a programme, not a one-off.** The benefit compounds with time, and findings should be shared across sales, product and marketing, not kept in one inbox.
> - **For positioning, the useful outputs are:** the real competitive alternatives (including do-nothing), the criteria you actually win on, and how buyers describe you to a peer.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2023-10
> - Clozd (a win-loss research vendor) analysed its own buyer interviews and found the closed-lost reason recorded in the CRM was wrong 85% of the time, and the wrong competitor was tagged 65% of the time (vendor data). A case for interviewing buyers rather than trusting CRM fields.
> - The Pragmatic Institute and Clozd survey of about 700 sales, marketing, product and executive respondents found 36% of companies used a third party for win-loss analysis; 62% of those were satisfied, against 28% with internal programmes (vendor-sponsored).
> - The same survey found 75% of companies running win-loss used three or more feedback channels, up from 60% in 2020.
> - Clozd's later material claims 63% of companies saw higher win rates from win-loss analysis (84% for programmes over two years old), that insights saturate at about 25–30 interviews per segment, and that interview participation runs at 15–30% against 3–5% for surveys (undated vendor guide; treat as indicative only).

## Notes
- **Vendor-sponsored.** The third-party findings favour the sponsor's business model, so read them that way. The CRM-versus-buyer mismatch is the most useful and most independently plausible finding.
- Practitioner sample sizes vary: independent PMMs commonly report themes repeating after 8–15 interviews per segment, far fewer than Clozd's 25–30. For a positioning exercise, 8–12 recent wins and losses across main segments is a workable floor.
- Practitioner question sets worth borrowing (full script in [[Win-Loss Interview Template for Product Marketing]]; programme example in [[How Atlassian's Sales-Focused Win-Loss Program Works]]): ask "what would you have used if we didn't exist?", "were there criteria you never told vendors?", and "how would you describe us to a colleague?". For losses: "was that something you saw in the product, heard in their messaging, or experienced in the sales process?", which separates product, positioning and sales-execution gaps.
