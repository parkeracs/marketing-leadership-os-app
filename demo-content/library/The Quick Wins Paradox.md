> [!summary] Principles
> - **The pursuit of a quick win is where new leaders derail.** Leaders who were struggling showed five behaviours of someone over-intent on an early win: obsessing over details, reacting badly to criticism, intimidating people, jumping to conclusions and micromanaging.
> - **Thriving new leaders paired a results focus with change management.** They set a clear direction, built constructive relationships and grew the team's capability, rather than delivering the win personally.
> - **Aim for a collective quick win, not an individual one.** The team you inherit is also in transition. A win the team delivers with you earns credibility and readies them for harder wins; a win you deliver over their heads can leave toxic fallout even if it lands.
> - **Some leaders got the win and still lost.** The lasting value of an early win is how the team was managed through it, not the result itself.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2009-01
> - Mark Van Buren and Todd Safferstone's HBR article draws on the Corporate Executive Board's survey of more than 5,400 new leaders and their managers' performance ratings.
> - The article includes a diagnostic for new leaders to spot collective quick wins, ones the team delivers with them rather than ones they deliver alone.
