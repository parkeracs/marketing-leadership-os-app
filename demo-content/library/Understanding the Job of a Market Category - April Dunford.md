> [!summary] Principles
> - A category's only job is to point a prospect who knows little about you towards your differentiated value, and away from what you don't do. It doesn't replace messaging, the pitch or the point of view.
> - You can't start with the category, because you have no way to judge a category until you know your differentiated value and who cares most about it. Starting there is the tail wagging the dog.
> - A category that sets off false assumptions (your 'CRM' is really for teams with no sales function) makes marketing and sales spend their time unwinding them.
> - Even would-be category creators should work the other components first. The exercise shows whether a new category is genuinely needed and which concepts to anchor it on.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2024-01
> - April Dunford (positioning consultant) says about 10% of her clients end up best positioned in a newly created market category, because no existing category explains their value. A useful base rate before assuming you need a new category.
> - Dunford reports that client teams who picked their market category before working out the rest of their positioning got results ranging from "sorta bad" to "absolute disaster".
