> [!summary] Principles
> - Categories emerge from the market's 'rugby scrum' of technology, customers, vendors, analysts, investors and media. They aren't designed in a conference room.
> - The best way to create a category is to go sell some software. Solve a problem for a persona (often in one industry), repeat it, then expand to adjacent buyers and problems.
> - Category design pulls startups into the wrong work: naming the future category, drawing category blueprints, writing elaborate five-year points of view, and mobilising analysts and media before there's demand.
> - An early company needs two things: a clear account of what it sells now, and why it exists. The 'mushy middle' of a future category between them is where time gets wasted.
> - If a category does start to emerge, help shape it and fight hard to win it with the Market Leader Play. Winning a category matters; the disagreement with Play Bigger is only about how you get there.

> [!warning] Numbers and tactics ⏳ as of 2026-08
> - Dave Kellogg's (CEO and board member) long-standing rule: industry analysts name categories, vendors don't.
> - Kellogg's tactic is to write an evaluation guide that sets the yardstick for the category, but only later, once the category has demand of its own.
